# Community Guidelines

*Welcome. Please read these before you post — they are both our shared standard and the rubric our moderation reviews against.*

## Why we are here

Essay Collections exists for one purpose: to help one another **come closer to Christ** and to **build Zion** through thoughtful, faithful writing. We are members of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints who love ideas and take both the life of the mind and the life of discipleship seriously. This is a place for careful essays — the kind you would be glad to have a friend, a bishop, or the Lord read over your shoulder.

We aspire to a rare combination: the intellectual rigor of a serious essay forum married to the charity and reverence of covenant discipleship. Ask hard questions. Reason carefully. Cite your sources. And do it all in a spirit that seeks light rather than heat, and that builds up rather than tears down.

> *"Charity suffereth long, and is kind... rejoiceth in the truth"* (1 Corinthians 13:4, 6). Truth-seeking and charity are not in tension here; they are the same work.

## Core expectations

**Charity and civility.** Speak as a disciple of Christ speaks. Disagree — often, and well — but always with kindness and respect for the person on the other side. No personal attacks, contempt, sarcasm meant to wound, or bad-faith characterizations of others' views. Assume the best of your fellow Saints.

**Intellectual honesty.** Argue in good faith. Represent others' positions fairly (steelman before you critique). Be honest about your uncertainty, cite your sources, and distinguish your own speculation from established doctrine. Change your mind when the evidence warrants; that is a strength, not a defeat.

**Faithfulness.** Content should be consistent with, supportive of, and **not in opposition to** the doctrine of the Church and the counsel of its leaders. The plain standard is: **nothing anti-.** This is a space for the faithful to think out loud together, not a platform for opposition to the Church, its leaders, or its teachings.

**Reverence for sacred things.** Some things are sacred and are not for public dissection or casual treatment. Respect the confidentiality of temple ordinances and covenants, and write about holy things with the reverence they are due.

**Constructive framing.** Hard questions are welcome — see below — but frame them to seek understanding and to build faith, not to unsettle it. The same question can be asked to find light or to spread doubt; ask it to find light.

**Keep it clean.** Nothing hateful, demeaning, harassing, obscene, profane, or vulgar. Write as you would speak in a chapel.

## Hard questions are welcome

Let us be clear, because it matters: **sincere, hard questions asked in faith belong here.** Doubt honestly wrestled with, difficult passages of history or scripture, tensions between faith and scholarship, the genuinely unresolved — these are not off-limits. Some of the most faith-building writing begins with an honest question.

The line is not between easy questions and hard ones. **The line is between honest inquiry that seeks light and content that seeks to tear down.** One says, "I am wrestling with this; help me see." The other says, "This proves the Church wrong." The first is discipleship. The second is not welcome here. Ask your hardest questions — and ask them as one who is reaching toward Christ, not away from Him.

## What is not allowed

The following will be flagged, and may be hidden, removed, or result in a warning or ban:

- **Apostate or anti-Church content** — material that attacks, opposes, ridicules, or seeks to undermine the Church, its leaders, its doctrine, or its ordinances. (Honest questions asked in faith are not this — see above.)
- **Hateful or demeaning speech** toward any person or group — including on the basis of race, ethnicity, nationality, sex, or any other characteristic. Contempt has no place in Zion.
- **Harassment** — targeting, dogpiling, or persistent hostility toward another member.
- **Personal attacks** — going after the person instead of the argument.
- **Sexually explicit or graphic content**, obscenity, profanity, or vulgarity.
- **Contention and needless polemics** — argument for its own sake, inflammatory framing, and rhetoric designed to provoke rather than illuminate.
- **Spam and self-promotion** — advertising, link-dumping, repetitive posting, or using the forum primarily to drive traffic elsewhere.
- **Sharing others' private information** — anyone's personal or identifying details without consent.
- **Revealing sacred things** inappropriately — treating temple ordinances or covenants irreverently or in explicit detail.

## How moderation works

Moderation is meant to protect the community, not to police thought. It works in layers:

1. **A daily review.** Once a day, an automated moderation bot reviews the last 24 hours of essays, comments, and chat against these guidelines and **files flags for human admins to review.** The bot does **not** delete or hide anything on its own — it only raises possible issues for a person to judge.
2. **Human admins decide.** Admins review each flag and may **dismiss** it, or take action: **warn** the author, **hide/pause** an essay while it is discussed, **delete** content, or in serious or repeated cases **ban** a user. Every admin action is recorded in an append-only audit log.
3. **You can help.** If you see something that crosses these lines, **report it** — reports go into the same review queue admins already watch.
4. **Appeals.** If your content was moderated and you believe it was a mistake, reach out to the admins. We would rather restore a good-faith essay than lose it, and we will tell you plainly what the concern was.

We aim to be **firm but generous**: firm about the standard, generous with the person. When in doubt, admins are asked to prefer the gentlest effective action, to assume good faith, and to write down their reasons.

## A closing word

These guidelines are a floor, not a ceiling — the real standard is the Spirit of Christ. Write things worth reading. Be quick to understand and slow to take offense. Let this be a place where a thoughtful Latter-day Saint can bring their whole mind and their whole heart, wrestle with real questions, and come away nearer to the Savior than when they arrived.

*Admins and leadership may refine these guidelines over time as the community grows.*
