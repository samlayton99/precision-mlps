# expC13 specification: five constructions with every construction and inference operation at p bits

This is the normative definition of the comparison. `common.py`, `select.py` and `methods/*.py` implement it; `tests/test_expC13_protocol.py` and `tests/test_expC13_constructions.py` check it.

## Precision and arithmetic

$p$ is the number of significant binary digits of every floating-point value, including the implicit leading bit (binary64 has $p=53$). The format is $(p, e_{\min}, e_{\max}) = (p, -958, 959)$ for every $p$ and every method: IEEE-style exponents, gradual underflow below $2^{-958}$, overflow to infinity above $(2-2^{1-p})\,2^{959}$. This is binary64's exponent field less 64 binades at each end, so one range serves the whole sweep, and at $p=53$ it differs from binary64 only in the extreme binades. $Q$ is round to nearest, ties to even, into the format.

All model arithmetic is pfloat 0.1: each of $+,-,\times,\div,\sqrt{\ }$ is one correctly rounded operation; negation, absolute value, comparison, $\max(z,0)$, and multiplication or division by a power of two that stays in range are exact. There is no fused multiply-add, and no compensated or extra-precise summation in any method. Sums are sequential in the order stated for each quantity; dot products and readouts start from the first term (or the bias).

**tanh** is pfloat's $\tanh_p$ (expC11 SPEC, "Model", item 3): $\tanh|z| = -E/(E+2)$ with $E=\mathrm{expm1}(-2|z|)$ by Cody-Waite reduction and a Taylor polynomial whose degree grows with $p$, computed only with the operations above; within 3 ulp at every $p$ tested. The same $\tanh_p$ serves every method and both construction and inference. No method uses a correctly rounded library tanh, an FP64 tanh, or higher internal precision.

**Constants** are rounded once into the format: integers $Q(n)$; the Mhaskar bias point $Q(\ln 2/2)$; Chebyshev node values $Q(\cos(\pi(k+\tfrac12)/M))$; tanh's internal constants and LAPACK's machine parameters (pfloat). External hyperparameters (QUILLS' bandwidth $\lambda$, Mhaskar's difference step $h$, steepness factors) are exact numbers chosen outside the model and rounded once. Everything computed from these is computed at $p$ bits.

## Data and the oracle

Every method receives the same kind of target information: it chooses sampling locations, which are format values, and the oracle returns $Q(f(\tilde x))$, the target at that exact location rounded once. $f(\tilde x)$ is evaluated by mpmath with $p+96$ bits of working precision; the returned value is checked to round identically at both ends of its uncertainty interval (otherwise the run stops). Sampling locations defined as real numbers (grid points $-1+2i/(n-1)$, Chebyshev nodes) are rounded once into the format.

Targets on $[-1,1]$: $e^x$ (entire), $\sin(4\pi x)$ (entire, oscillatory), $1/(1+25x^2)$ (poles at $\pm i/5$), $\sin(8\pi(x+1)^2)$ (the chirp of expC09/expC11/expC12).

## Networks

**One hidden tanh layer** (QUILLS, Mhaskar, staircase, Costarelli-Spigler): $y(x) = c + \sum_{j=0}^{W-1} a_j \tanh(w_j x + b_j)$. Forward pass: $z_j = \mathrm{add}(\mathrm{mul}(w_j, x), b_j)$, $t_j = \tanh_p(z_j)$, then $s = c$ and $s = \mathrm{add}(s, \mathrm{mul}(t_j, a_j))$ for $j = 0, \dots, W-1$ in the method's neuron order.

**Deep ReQU** (ChebNet): hidden layers $h^{(k)} = \sigma(A_k h^{(k-1)} + b_k)$ with $\sigma(z) = \max(z,0)^2$ computed as $m=\max(z,0)$ (exact), $\mathrm{mul}(m,m)$; linear output layer; $h^{(0)} = x$. Each neuron's pre-activation is $s = b_i$, then $s = \mathrm{add}(s, \mathrm{mul}(A_{i,u}, h_u))$ over its nonzero inputs $u$ in the construction's listed order.

**Size** is the number of nonzero weights and biases (all layers, output layer included). **Budget:** at most 3073 nonzero parameters, the size of a 1024-neuron one-hidden-layer network. Neuron counts and depth are reported alongside.

## Measurement

Validation grid: the 1024 midpoints $-1+(2i+1)/1024$. Reporting grid: the 8001 points $-1+2i/8000$. The two are disjoint. A network is evaluated at the rounded grid point $Q(x)$ and compared with $f(x)$ at the exact point, so input rounding counts as error, equally for every method.

Errors are relative $L^2$ over the grid, $\|y - f\|_2/\|f\|_2$, and relative $L^\infty$, $\max|y-f| / \max|f|$. The relative $L^2$ error is computed in a 320-bit format from $f$ evaluated at 384 bits; $L^\infty$ takes the maximum of the 320-bit residuals rounded to binary64 (a few digits suffice). The meter only reads a network's $p$-bit outputs; no higher-precision value reaches a model. Every reported error is recomputed at 640 bits and the two must agree to 12 significant digits.

## Selection

For each target, method and $p$, every candidate in the method's grid (below) within the budget is built at $p$ bits and scored on the validation grid; the smallest validation error wins (ties: fewer parameters, then the earlier candidate). The winner alone is evaluated on the reporting grid. Candidates whose parameters or validation outputs are not finite are recorded as invalid. The chosen model is saved exactly (format values) and replayed; the replayed outputs must be bit-for-bit identical.

## Methods

Each method is the construction of the paper's comparison appendix, built at $p$ bits. Where the source paper leaves a choice free, the choice is a validated hyperparameter. Where a documented numerical or structural improvement exists that keeps the method's defining feature, it is offered as a labelled option, and the faithful configuration is also reported on its own (`*_classical`, `*_appendix`, `*_paper`).

**QUILLS** (`methods/quills.py`). Width $W=1024$, halo $H=24$, $N=W-2H-1$ intervals. $h=\mathrm{div}(2,Q(N))$, centers $c_j=\mathrm{add}(-1,\mathrm{mul}(Q(j),h))$ for $j=-H..N+H$, $\gamma=\mathrm{div}(Q(\lambda),h)$; hidden slope $\gamma$, bias $-\mathrm{mul}(\gamma,c_j)$. $\lambda$ is expC09's refined rule with $e_{\rm tol}=2^{1-p}$ and the target's frequency scale (the basic rule for $e^x$), computed offline. Training data: $M=4801$ points $-1+2i/4800$. Readout: pfloat `lstsq`, reference LAPACK `DGELSS` ported to $p$ bits, on $[\Phi, \mathbf 1]$ with RCOND $=2^{1-p}$. No hyperparameter is tuned (one candidate). This replaces expC12's FP64 SVD exception.

**Mhaskar** (`methods/mhaskar.py`, `methods/chebyshev.py`). Degree-$d$ discrete Chebyshev projection from $M_c=4096$ first-kind nodes, $c_0 = (\sum_i y_i)/M_c$ and $c_k = 2(\sum_i y_i T_k(x_i))/M_c$ with $T_{k+1} = \mathrm{mul}(\mathrm{mul}(2,x),T_k) - T_{k-1}$ and sequential sums over the nodes; conversion of the truncation to monomials by the $T_k$ coefficient recurrence; $x^r$ replaced by the normalized centered difference $H_{r,h}(x)=\sum_j(-1)^{r-j}\binom rj\tanh(b_0+(j-r/2)hx)/(h^r r!\,c_r)$ with $b_0=Q(\ln 2/2)$ and $c_r=\tanh^{(r)}(b_0)/r!$ from $c_0=\tanh_p(b_0)$ and $(r+1)c_{r+1}=[r=0]-\sum_{j\le r}c_jc_{r-j}$; merged into $2d+1$ neurons with slopes $\mathrm{mul}(\mathrm{div}(h,2),Q(s))$, $s=-d..d$, bias $b_0$, offset 0. Operation order: expC12 `pbit_kernels.c` (bit-identical, tested). This is Mhaskar (1996), Lemma 3.2, eq. 3.19 (per-monomial centered differences in the slope, all at one bias $b$, weights drawn from a fixed set $\{hr\}$), with the correct $(2h)^{-p}$-type normalization (eq. 3.19 as printed is off by $(-2)^{p}$), and the faithful configuration (`mhaskar_appendix`). The polynomial differs from the paper's Theorem 2.3 (Chebyshev interpolation at the degree's own nodes); swapping it in changes the faithful errors by at most 8% on $e^x$ and $\sin4\pi x$ at $p=24,53,80$ (checked). Option (common grid, our variant, not in the paper): the same kind of slope set $b_0+jh$, $j=-m..m$, realizing every monomial up to degree $2m$ by maximal-order differences: $x^k\approx\sum_j L_{kj}\tanh(b_0+jhx)/(c_kh^k)$ with $L_{kj}=[t^k]\ell_j(t)$, the Lagrange basis on the integer nodes $-m..m$ (exact rationals rounded once). Readout $a_j=\sum_k e_kL_{kj}$, sequential in $k$, with $e_k=\mathrm{div}(a_k,c_k)$ divided $k$ times by $h$; $m=\lceil d/2\rceil$, so degree $d$ costs $2\lceil d/2\rceil+1$ neurons. Grid for both: 29 degrees $0..128$ (expC12's grid; its entries 160..511 never compete, since no degree above 7 won at any $p\le 53$ there and the bits needed grow like $d^2$) $\times$ 65 steps $h\in[10^{-4},4]$ (geometric).

**Classical staircase** (`methods/staircase.py`). $q(x)=f(-1)+\sum_{j=1}^N[f(x_j)-f(x_{j-1})]S(\kappa N(x-t_j))$, $S=(1+\tanh)/2$, $x_j=-1+2j/N$. Tanh form: readouts $\mathrm{mul}(\mathrm{sub}(y_j,y_{j-1}),\tfrac12)$, slope $Q(\kappa N)$, biases $-\mathrm{mul}(Q(\kappa N),t_j)$, offset $\mathrm{mul}(\mathrm{add}(y_0,y_N),\tfrac12)$ (the sigmoids' constant parts telescope). Faithful configuration (`staircase_classical`): $\kappa=1$, $t_j=x_j$, the appendix's network. Options: $\kappa\in\{\tfrac18,\tfrac14,\tfrac12,1,2,4,8,32\}$ (covers Costarelli-Spigler's logistic threshold $\kappa=\ln(N-1)/4$ and near-Heaviside slopes), jumps at cell midpoints $t_j=-1+(2j-1)/N$, and with midpoint jumps a halo of $K=8$ extra samples per side extrapolated from the three nearest in-domain samples by the quadratic Lagrange formula (integer weights; no oracle calls outside $[-1,1]$), which removes the first-order boundary error of the truncated smoothing kernel; $N+2K$ neurons. $N\in\{8,\dots,1024\}$.

**Costarelli-Spigler** (`methods/costarelli.py`). Theorem 5.4 of Costarelli-Spigler (2015), sampled: $q(x)=\sum_{k=-K}^K A_k\psi(wx-k)$, $\psi(t)=\sigma(t+1)-\sigma(t)$, $\sigma=(1+\tanh)/2$, scale $w$ and truncation $K>w$. $A_k=F(u_k)$ with $F$ equal to $f$ on $[-1,1]$ and linear tapers to 0 on $[1,2]$, $[-2,-1]$ (the appendix's extension; $\mathrm{mul}(y_{\pm1},\mathrm{sub}(2,|u_k|))$ outside). Merged by summation by parts into $2K+2$ neurons $\sigma(wx-j)$, $j=-K-1..K$, readouts $\mathrm{mul}(\mathrm{sub}(A_{j+1},A_j),\tfrac12)$, slope $Q(w)$, bias $Q(-j)$, offset 0 (exact telescoping). Faithful configuration (`costarelli_appendix`): $u_k=k/w$, $K=2w$. Options: $K=w+\lceil p\ln 2/2\rceil+1$ (the tail of $\psi$ decays like $e^{-2|t|}$, so this truncation is below unit roundoff; the paper allows any $K>w$), and centred samples $u_k=(k-\tfrac12)/w$ at the centre of $\psi(wx-k)$, which removes the half-cell shift responsible for the $O(1/w)$ interior error. $w\in\{2,\dots,490\}$.

**ChebNet** (`methods/chebnet.py`). Tang-Li-Yu (arXiv:1911.05467 v3, Theorem 3), with the layer algorithm of Li-Tang-Yu (CiCP 2020, Theorem 2.2) and $T_{2^k}=2T_{2^{k-1}}^2-1$ in place of powers. Coefficients: the same $p$-bit projection as Mhaskar, zero-padded to $2^{m+1}$, $m=\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor$, then the hierarchical split $r_0=c_0$, $q_0=c_M$, $r_j=\mathrm{sub}(c_j,c_{2M-j})$, $q_j=\mathrm{mul}(2,c_{M+j})$, recursively (equal to the paper's $S_m$, tested). ReQU primitives (Lemma 2): identity $t=\beta_1^\top\sigma(\omega_1t+\gamma_1)$, product $ty=\beta_1^\top\sigma(\omega_1t+\gamma_1y)$, square $t^2=\sigma(t)+\sigma(-t)$, $\beta_1=\tfrac14[1,1,-1,-1]$, $\omega_1=[1,-1,1,-1]$, $\gamma_1=[1,-1,-1,1]$. After hidden layer $k$ the network carries $P^{(k)}_i=P^{(k-1)}_{2i}+T_{2^{k-1}}P^{(k-1)}_{2i+1}$ and $T_{2^k}$; layers are collapsed so the network is a standard MLP (weights $\pm\tilde c/4$ and $\pm1,\pm2,\pm\tfrac14$, biases $\pm\tilde c\pm1$ with one rounding). Nodes with $i2^k>n$ are omitted and nodes with $i2^k=n$ are constants in biases (the pruned tree; for $n=2^{m+1}-1$ the padded construction). $n=1,2$ use the paper's explicit networks. Faithful configuration (`chebnet_paper`): as above. Option: scale $\tilde c$ by the power of two nearest $1/\sum|c|$ and undo it in the output weights (exact), which keeps carried values $O(1)$ for the identity map's absolute rounding. Degrees $n\in\{1,\dots,127\}$ within the budget (the budget admits $n\le 95$). Sensitivity arm (`chebnet_neurons`): the same construction under a budget of 1024 hidden neurons instead of 3073 parameters (degrees up to 255), because ChebNet spends about 8 parameters per neuron and 32 per degree, so the budget's unit decides how much degree it can afford.
