# Fixed-center optimization exploration

This campaign tests whether coordinate scaling, neighboring readouts, gradient EMA, sampling-based learning-rate control, and selective resets improve GD or Adam on the existing fixed-center tanh problem. SSBroyden metric resets and conditional promotion to independently trainable affine hidden parameters are separate comparisons. The campaign budget is ten allocated H200 GPU-hours, at most two GPUs concurrently, through Slurm.

[Local mechanism calculations](mechanism.md) explain what the gradient filter can change in a fixed quadratic mode, and why neighboring tanh features retain exponential high-frequency attenuation. These calculations guide interpretation; the measured finite-network spectra determine whether the remaining residual occupies weak directions.

The model and corrected-halo geometry reuse D06. All fixed-center readouts, output bias, and slopes train jointly. Training minimizes half-MSE in FP64. The same scalar rate applies to every native coordinate. For $c=Tz$ and $\lambda=h\gamma$, physical GD mobility is $\eta TT^T$ for readouts and $\eta/h^2$ for slopes. Physical-coordinate controls instead train $c,\gamma$ directly. `collective`, `individual`, and `neighbor` mean the existing square-root allowance, individual allowance, and anchored cumulative-difference maps, respectively.

`reference_xavier` reuses the existing initializer exactly: physical ordinary readouts already scale as $O(h)$. `individual_gaussian` uses each individual allowance times the same unit Gaussian draw. The output bias starts at zero. Coordinate comparisons encode the same physical arrays. `physical_xavier` initializes slopes as before; `lambda_xavier` uses the same draws in the dimensionless bandwidth. These initialization comparisons change starting functions and are reported separately from coordinate comparisons.

The gradient filter is $\bar g_t=\alpha_{\rm EMA}\bar g_{t-1}+(1-\alpha_{\rm EMA})g_t$, followed by $\widetilde g_t=g_t+\rho\bar g_t$, before Adam or GD. Its first EMA value is the first gradient. It follows the active function in HazyResearch/precision-ls at revision `ae2a876ab8adcacf099528d5d3a02430920baeb9`. The separately labeled normalized and post-optimizer variants are controls. No parameter averaging is implicit.

Training uses $16N+1$ grid points; the 32,768 midpoint validation grid may guide the adaptive sweep. A separate 65,536 midpoint grid is reserved for final selected recipes. D34's empirical polynomial coefficient map is fixed at its original 2,048-point grid and reused at every width. Sine and mixed target amplitudes retain D06's definitions; Runge and matched polynomial targets retain D34's definitions. Failed trials are retained, and finite scientific screening runs receive at least 20k updates; rate finalists receive 100k before longer comparisons.

Implementation checks compare the unchanged trainers, analytic and automatic gradients, paired physical maps, EMA recurrence and gain controls, tiny-gradient statistics, and target definitions. The tests establish execution correctness rather than a favorable scientific result.

`python -m experiments.expD35_optimization_exploration.run --root <run-root> --frontier 20000` runs the initial 240-case matrix. Optional `--coordinates`, `--optimizer`, and `--target` filters select throughput probes without changing case identities. A JSON list supplied with `--manifest` defines later paired comparisons. Reinvocation resumes every parameter, optimizer moment, EMA, per-coordinate age, and RNG stream. It never substitutes an earlier best checkpoint for the live state.

Use the Slurm launcher after deploying a committed checkout into `exploration-code`. It preserves the allocated device mask, checks the active job/step, and uses local batch logs to avoid the prior shared-volume logging failure. `--worker 0 --workers 2` and worker 1 partition independent groups. Scalar traces are compressed, physical parameters are reconstructable from saved native states, and checkpoints occur every 20k updates. Source hashes, immutable case configurations, and origin-checkpoint hashes record provenance.

The agreement experiment draws eight independent stratified batches at the same saved parameter state every 3,000 updates. It records all 28 pairwise gradient cosines, summarized separately for all coordinates, readouts, and geometry; the saved arrays also permit recomputation. The scheduler uses the global mean cosine, smoothed with coefficient 0.9 and initialized to one. Agreement below the chosen threshold increases the shared rate by $1/0.9$; otherwise it multiplies it by 0.9. Rates stay between $10^{-16}$ and a previously screened stable ceiling. Undefined agreement holds the rate. A predetermined factor-0.9 decay at the same times separates scheduling from the measured-agreement decision. These are explicitly labeled departures from the constant-rate baseline.

Recycling tracks an EMA with coefficient 0.99 of $|w_j|\operatorname{mean}|\tanh(\gamma_j(x-t_j))|$. Neurons become eligible after 5,000 updates. A fractional accumulator implements replacement rates per eligible neuron per update. The lowest-utility eligible neurons receive freshly drawn slopes and zero physical readout weights; centers stay fixed. This deletes their previous contribution, so the measured function jump is recorded. Adam moments, bias-correction ages, and both gradient-filter histories are cleared for affected native coordinates. Neighboring coordinates require clearing the changed cumulative suffix. Replaying the same masks without changing parameters isolates optimizer-state reset; random eligible neurons with the same event counts provide a selection control.

`design.py` writes auditable JSON manifests from completed validation histories. The score is the mean relative MSE over saved evaluations in the final fifth of the current horizon; median, 90th percentile, and best are retained separately. The first promotion keeps two rates per target/optimizer/map and runs each with seeds 0 and 1 to 100k. Later EMA, initialization, and agreement screens select their controls from these longer runs. A numerical failure is excluded from promotion but remains in the case ledger.

Comparisons specify a common update horizon. A control already promoted to 100k is scored using its saved 16k–20k evaluations when compared with a new 20k intervention; its later error is not substituted. The ranking utility accepts an explicit horizon for this purpose.

The SSBroyden extension reuses the pinned source and accepted-step integration correction from D06. It compares no inverse-metric reset, full reset to native identity, replacement of the geometry block by identity with zero cross blocks, and a blend $0.9H+0.1I$. A reset leaves the parameters unchanged and restarts search/descent history, so stale secants do not cross the intervention. Traces count accepted physical updates and include curvature, guard activation, secant error, block gradient norms, physical motion, and alignment with native steepest descent. Focused checks verify metric covariance of the first physical step and exact resume across reset boundaries. Explicit search failures remain failures even when their MSE is small.

The first guard screen exposed positive stored directional derivatives at failed searches. The added `non_descent` arm resets the inverse metric and search history to native identity when $-g^THg\geq0$ or is nonfinite. This is a result-driven intervention, not a change to the unchanged SSBroyden control. `audit_ssb.py` checks the saved gradient and inverse metric in 80-digit arithmetic, separating an indefinite stored matrix from roundoff in the scalar product. Periodic resets still test a different question: whether deliberately forgetting curvature preserves useful coordinate scaling.

After the 20k screens, the 100k promotion retains two raw-gradient EMA recipes per target/optimizer/map, exact-rate controls, GD gain controls, Adam normalization and post-optimizer controls, and the best rate for each initialization pair. The best initialization and EMA settings are also combined at three rate factors. Seeds 0 and 1 use the same selected recipes. Agreement scheduling is extended mainly for Adam; the GD neighbor/sine comparison remains as a check, while the other GD agreement arms showed no reason to spend more budget on them. Terminal numerical failures are not relaunched. SSBroyden's next screen compares the periodic resets and the non-descent reset, with smaller line-search guards retained as diagnostic controls.

`--capture-last 2048` saves every native parameter state in the final 2,048 updates without changing the optimizer. `diagnose.py` uses these actual consecutive states for readout-first/geometry-second function increments, and records their Fourier energies, signed descent terms, and cross term. Frequency bands include DC (the spatial mean), indices 64–127, and 128–255 explicitly; energies sum to MSE by Parseval's identity. Detached SVD fits use several disclosed relative cutoffs and record coefficient norms and validation error. They diagnose the remaining fixed-geometry readout problem and never replace trained readouts.

The spectral analysis samples one adjacent update pair per 16-update block, with deterministically shuffled, balanced offsets to avoid always sampling the same phase of a periodic oscillation. New dense captures save the device's raw and filtered gradients at these indices. Earlier captures recompute the training-grid gradient and label its source. Alignment with the midpoint validation-grid gradient is recorded separately: a descent step for the training quadrature need not align with that different gradient. The time-mean residual and its temporal fluctuation energy are diagnostics, not an averaged checkpoint substituted for training.

The dense-window analysis also fixes the readout SVD basis at the first saved state and projects subsequent residuals and actual function increments into the same singular-value bands. Energy outside that fixed span is recorded separately. Snapshot diagnostics split the slope gradient by constant/linear versus remaining residual, and by the numerically retained readout span versus its orthogonal complement. The latter uses a disclosed $10^{-12}$ relative singular-value cutoff; it is a numerical diagnostic, not an exact rank claim.

Final frozen recipes are evaluated with `precision.py` on the reserved 65,536-point midpoint grid. A fixed 257-point subset is reevaluated at 80 decimal digits. The audit separately measures the error of the rounded physical coefficients, the exact native coordinate map using the stored constants, FP64 forward-evaluation error, and target-evaluation error. These distinguish optimization error from numerical evaluation error; the subset is a numerical audit, not a replacement for the full-grid error measurement. No final-grid score is used to choose hyperparameters.

A cluster quota error interrupted the first metric-reset launch. Immutable history files are now consolidated into per-case ZIP archives after checkpoint boundaries. Every member is checked against its SHA-256 digest before the original file is removed. Live states, handoff checkpoints, latest snapshots, and replay masks remain directly accessible; validation histories also have a compact JSON index. This changes storage layout only. `history.py` performs the same verified consolidation on earlier completed histories, and the ranking code reads both layouts.

A second quota interruption required offloading immutable archives to the local evidence directory. CPU-only Slurm jobs compared each archive's SHA-256 digest against its downloaded copy before removing the redundant cluster copy. Per-case `offloaded_archives.json` ledgers preserve archive sequence numbers, so resumed training cannot overwrite an earlier archive identity. All 1,894 then-existing parameter/solver checkpoints passed array loading, dtype/shape checks where applicable, and hashing before restart. Checkpoints remain on the cluster; the complete archived histories are retained locally. Both interrupted GPU allocations count against the campaign budget.

The conditional affine promotion adds independent offsets $\beta_j$:

$$
f(x)=b_0+\sum_jw_j\tanh\!\left[\frac{\lambda_j}{h}(x-t_j^{\rm ref})+\beta_j\right],
\qquad
a_j=\frac{\lambda_j}{h},\quad b_j=\beta_j-\frac{\lambda_jt_j^{\rm ref}}h.
$$

Thus every affine slope and hidden bias can change. The same native scalar rate updates readouts, $\lambda$, and $\beta$. The fixed reference centers define coordinates; they do not constrain the learned centers. A checkpoint release appends zero offsets and retains the previous parameters and Adam moments, with fresh Adam state for the new offsets. Both the affine release and its fixed-center continuation restart the gradient filter from the current gradient. Later scheduled-finishing forks explicitly set `reset_filter=false` to retain that state too. Cold comparisons use grid centers, jitter within half a spacing, or sorted random centers over the original halo extent. The readout map stays tied to the original ordering throughout training. This promotion is separate from the primary fixed-center comparisons and excludes neuron recycling and SSBroyden in its initial assay.

The width promotion ranks a recipe by the arithmetic mean of its two seed scores at the same horizon. Width 512 receives a three-rate screen and 100k confirmation before width 1024. The stronger-memory extension tests $\rho=32$ because several GD optima were on the earlier $\rho=8$ boundary. Adam guards are compared at $10^{-8},10^{-15},10^{-24}$ outside the square root. A separate GD screen extends the constant shared rate to $10$ under bandwidth-Xavier initialization: stability under the old initialization does not justify limiting the new initialization to the old rate neighborhood. Warm EMA forks start after 100k ordinary updates and preserve optimizer state, isolating a late intervention from the initial geometry transient.

For reference, with corrected-halo allowances $\alpha_j$, the readout maps are

$$
\text{collective: }c_j=\sqrt{\alpha_j}\,z_j,
\qquad
\text{individual: }c_j=\alpha_jz_j.
$$

The neighboring map instead uses

$$
A_j=\sum_{k=1}^{j}\alpha_k,\qquad q_j=A_jz_j,
\qquad w_j=q_j-q_{j-1},\qquad q_0=0,\qquad b_0=\alpha_0z_0.
$$

Thus its function can also be evaluated as $b_0+\sum_{j=1}^{W-1}q_j(\phi_j-\phi_{j+1})+q_W\phi_W$. The final anchor is retained. Initial slopes use the absolute value of the original Gaussian Xavier draw; subsequent slopes are unconstrained. `lambda_xavier` applies those same draws directly to $\lambda=h\gamma$.

The shared-graph SSB confirmation makes reset modes runtime inputs to one compiled function. Its no-reset and late-reset controls agree exactly until the first reset, including their failure if it occurs earlier. The subsequent `neighbor_stable` assay evaluates the same model through cancellation-aware tanh differences, with the analytic derivative $d(\tanh a-\tanh b)=\operatorname{sech}^2(a)\,da-\operatorname{sech}^2(b)\,db$. Its unchanged-arithmetic controls remain separate cases. Final precision audits report both the rounded physical-weight function and the exact native coordinate map, as well as the two FP64 evaluation forms.

The final width-512 recipes are frozen after the two-seed 100k rate and Adam-guard comparisons. Constant-rate runs extend to 500k, with new seeds 2–4 and exact-rate no-EMA controls on seeds 0–1. Matched finishing policies fork the same 100k parameters and complete optimizer/filter state for 500k further updates. Polynomial/Runge transfer uses the frozen degree-nine recipe on new seeds without target-specific retuning. A small recycling follow-up uses the selected sine and mixed Adam recipes: earlier recycling helped one poorly initialized sine setup, which does not establish a benefit after initialization and rate tuning. It compares two replacement rates and replays each event sequence with state-only and random-neuron controls for 20k additional updates.

The gradient-filter and agreement experiments adapt ideas from [Liu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.12295) and their [released code](https://github.com/HazyResearch/precision-ls/tree/ae2a876ab8adcacf099528d5d3a02430920baeb9). The neuron experiment adapts low-utility replacement from [Dohare et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11338828/). These are single-target tanh optimization experiments, rather than replications of the papers' sequence-model or continual-learning settings.
