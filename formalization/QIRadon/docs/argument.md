# The construction and its proof

The goal is one fixed, finite, one-hidden-layer network that approximates a
known target uniformly on the closed unit ball in dimension d. Width counts
hidden neurons, including halos and any neurons used to implement the anchor.
All constants may depend on the fixed dimension, target regularity, boundary
margins, and activation. Arithmetic cost and fixed-precision feasibility are
separate from this approximation theorem.

## 1. Turn the target into scalar profiles

The raw analytic endpoint starts with a real function f, a bounded holomorphic
extension to a complex neighborhood of a larger real ball, a radius R>1, and
a positive complex margin. The formal source bridge handles dimensions d≥2;
the scalar machinery separately handles one-dimensional profiles.

An explicit smooth radial cutoff produces a compactly supported smooth function
that equals f throughout the unit ball. We do not assume a function is both
compactly supported and globally analytic. The boundary construction supplies
local analytic continuations of the profiles actually used on the interior.

Normalized Radon/Fourier reconstruction gives real scalar profiles g_theta,
indexed by unit directions theta, such that

\[
f(x)=f(0)+\int_{S^{d-1}}
  [g_\theta(\theta\cdot x)-g_\theta(0)]\,d\sigma(\theta),
\]

where sigma is the probability measure on the sphere. The normalization
constants are included in the profiles. This identity is proved for the
original target, not assumed as a profile oracle.

Two different regularities matter: how each profile varies along its scalar
coordinate, and how the centered integrand varies with direction. The source
bridge derives both from the original local analytic assumptions. The angular
norm concerns the centered difference displayed above; no separate regularity
hypothesis on the angular function g_theta(0) is inserted.

## 2. Construct a finite approximation along one spoke

Let phi be the activation. The general kernel theorem assumes that its r-th
derivative satisfies phi^(r)=m K, with m nonzero and K a real Schwartz kernel.
The quantitative rate theorem additionally assumes positive exponents nu, mu
and two-sided spectral and spatial envelopes of the following form:

\[
c e^{-A|\xi|^\nu}\le |\widehat K(\xi)|
 \le C e^{-a|\xi|^\nu},\qquad
|K(t)|\le C_s e^{-b|t|^\mu}.
\]

The constants required to be positive are positive; A may be zero. In
particular, the spectral lower bound supplies the nonvanishing needed for
deconvolution. These are sufficient conditions, not a classification of every
activation that can approximate functions. ReLU is handled by separate spline
results because its relevant derivative is a distribution rather than a
Schwartz kernel.

A local analytic profile is first prepared from finitely many Chebyshev
samples using an explicit polynomial/window construction. This step supplies
a globally usable spectrum without requiring global analyticity of the
original profile. Its approximation error and growing spectral norm are both
bounded. In the local algebraic version, a stable filtered sample preparation
replaces the analytic preparation.

Write u for the prepared scalar profile and p=u^(r). With the cycles-frequency
Fourier convention used in Lean, define

\[
\rho_B(c)=\operatorname{Re}\int_{-B}^{B}
 \frac{\widehat p(\xi)}{\widehat K(\xi/\gamma)}
 e^{2\pi i\xi c}\,d\xi.
\]

For positive width parameter gamma and spacing h, the actual finite sum is

\[
R(s)=\frac{h\gamma}{m\gamma^r}
 \sum_{j\in J}\rho_B(hj)\phi(\gamma(s-hj)).
\]

Its r-th derivative is exactly h times the sum of rho_B(hj) multiplied by
K_gamma(s-hj), where K_gamma(t)=gamma K(gamma t). The prefactor above is valid
also for r=0. In the continuous integral, the Fourier multiplier cancels and
recovers the retained part of p. Replacing the integral by the finite sum has
three explicit errors:

1. The discarded part of the target spectrum outside [-B,B].
2. Every nonzero alias generated by the lattice spacing h.
3. Every omitted center outside the finite index set J, on both sides.

The finite spectral certificate proves these terms directly. The envelope
lemmas then bound them under the selected bandwidth, spacing, and halo schedule.

A degree-below-r polynomial restores the derivative values of u at zero through
order r−1. Integrating the r-th derivative error bounds the function error;
on [-T,T], the multiplier is T^r/r!. For r=0 there is no polynomial. For r=1 it
is an output bias. For higher orders, implementing that polynomial in the
chosen activation is an explicit architectural obligation. GELU implements the
combined affine anchor with two neurons total. The generic higher-order
endpoint charges an independently certified anchor's error and neuron count.

## 3. Choose directions and assemble the network

The actual folded positive Gauss–Jacobi sphere rule has M=q^(d−1) direction
indices at order q. Its weights alpha_m are positive and sum to one. They are
quadrature weights, not generally equal weights. The proof establishes the
nodes, normalization, polynomial exactness, and antipodal folding.

Let F_x(theta)=g_theta(theta·x)−g_theta(0). The canonical harmonic truncation P_x
through degree 2q−1 preserves its exact spherical mean: every positive-degree
harmonic has mean zero. The rule integrates P_x exactly. If I denotes the
spherical integral and Q the rule, then

\[
I(F_x)-Q(F_x)=I(P_x)-Q(F_x)=Q(P_x-F_x).
\]

Therefore the angular error is at most the uniform harmonic tail. There is no
extra factor two. One shared Lean lemma provides this step for analytic and
Sobolev tails.

Let u_m approximate the uncentered profile on spoke m with uniform error e_m.
The final anchored approximation is

\[
\widehat f(x)=f(0)+\sum_{m=1}^{M}\alpha_m
 [u_m(\theta_m\cdot x)-u_m(0)].
\]

Subtracting u_m(0) changes only the output constant of an already realized
network. Each centered spoke error is at most 2e_m. Positive-weight transport
now gives the single assembly inequality

\[
\|\widehat f-f\|_\infty
 \le E_{\rm angular}+2\sum_m\alpha_m e_m.
\]

For a common e_m=e, the scalar contribution is 2e, independent of M. The finite
geometry and coefficients are fixed before the theorem quantifies over all
inputs in the ball.

## 4. Count the actual neurons

Keep three quantities distinct: q is angular quadrature order, n is scalar
preparation order, and J is the actual finite center set including halos.
The Lean scalar files conventionally call preparation order N; it is not the
interior-neuron count from the experimental UI.

Set kappa=max(1,1/nu+1/mu). The finite construction proves

\[
|J|\le C_{\rm grid} n^\kappa,\qquad
W_{\rm grid}\le q^{d-1}C_{\rm grid}n^\kappa.
\]

The omitted-center estimate determines the halo schedule; halos are included
in |J|. Analytic regularity supplies errors U exp(−a q) and V exp(−b n).
Selecting sufficient positive integer orders yields

\[
W=O\!\left(\log^{d-1+\kappa}(1/\epsilon)\right).
\]

The spectral/spatial exponents for Gaussian, erf/CDF, and GELU give kappa=1,
hence log^d. The verified generic tanh envelopes give kappa=2, hence log^(d+1).
The main PDF proves and tests the stronger corrected-halo tanh result with
log^d width. Its contour argument is not yet formalized in this library; this
short document describes the verified generic construction. Applying the
correction after entire sample preparation also gives a linear scalar count
for the local algebraic tanh class, as shown in the PDF.

For algebraic angular error U q^(−t) and local scalar error V n^(−s), positive
s,t give W=O(epsilon^(−(d−1)/t−kappa/s)). Here t is the angular regularity after
the Sobolev uniform-norm loss (d−1)/2. The local scalar class is explicitly a
uniform polynomial-approximation class; mere nonanalyticity does not imply this
rate. A separate global Fourier-moment class proves exponent (d−1)/t+1/s.

## 5. Allocate resolution according to the proved bound

Allocation minimizes the sufficient continuous width bound; it does not claim
a globally optimal network or integer grid. For algebraic errors, assigning
angular budget x and scalar budget epsilon−x produces a cost proportional to
x^(−P)(epsilon−x)^(−Q), where P=(d−1)/t and Q=kappa/s. Its unique interior minimum
is x=epsilon P/(P+Q). For the analytic certificate the optimal angular fraction
tends to (d−1)/(d−1+kappa) as epsilon tends to zero.

The per-spoke version minimizes sum c_m n_m^kappa subject to
sum D_m exp(−b_m n_m)≤epsilon and prescribed minimum orders. The proof establishes
the common multiplier, unique scalar roots with finite brackets, global
optimality of the continuous certificate, and feasible rounding. Actual finite
row errors and full grid cardinalities are then attached to that allocation.

Data-based selection has its own deterministic coverage/noise and statistical
held-out certificates. Without quantitative coverage or regularity assumptions,
finite samples cannot certify an arbitrary function's uniform error; that
obstruction is also proved.
