# Sine can change direction without changing the sign of its driving error

This checkpoint diagnostic makes the need for evolving effective sensitivity
concrete. For sine, the cubic error drives contraction at 100k and expansion at
400k in all five seeds. Its sign stays positive. The signed effective
sensitivity reverses. Thus even an accessible, substantial target error does
not determine the direction of scale motion by itself.

The audit uses the existing 30 checkpoints: sine and degree 9, seeds 0–4,
at 100k, 400k, and 600k. It recomputes the degree-65 effective force from each
checkpoint, without training. This is a descriptive check of the mechanism;
it does not alter or independently validate the forecasts issued before the
matched continuations.

## Example: the cubic error survives while its slope effect reverses

The sine target has polynomial coefficients $Y_1=-0.275665$,
$Y_3=-0.261093$, and $Y_5=0.556016$ on the fixed training grid. At 100k,
the median cubic output is only $0.000389$. Its cubic residual
$e_3=f_3-Y_3$ is therefore about $0.261482$: it is mainly an unfitted target
component, rather than unwanted output generated by the network.

Define the signed effective sensitivity of mean scale to error $e_k$ by

$$
A_k(\theta)=-\frac1W\sum_j\operatorname{sign}(a_j)(T_a)_{jk}.
$$

Away from a zero slope, the effective contribution of mode $k$ to the
instantaneous mean-gamma velocity is exactly $A_ke_k$. The minus sign converts
gradient to velocity. The index $k$ denotes polynomial degree, not the column
number after removing the coarse modes.

| Sine checkpoint | Median $e_3$ | Median $A_3$ | Median cubic mean-gamma velocity | Seeds contracting through this mode |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 100k | $0.26148$ | $-2.0144\times10^{-5}$ | $-5.2672\times10^{-6}$ | 5/5 |
| 400k | $0.26057$ | $5.7410\times10^{-5}$ | $1.4959\times10^{-5}$ | 0/5 |
| 600k | $-0.16045$ | Positive in all five seeds | $-7.4992\times10^{-4}$ | 3/5 |

Each column is a separate median; products of medians need not equal median
products. At 400k, $e_3$ remains positive in every seed while $A_3$ becomes
positive in every seed. At 600k, $A_3$ remains positive but $e_3$ has crossed
zero in three seeds. The median fifth-mode contribution is then outward,
$7.2942\times10^{-4}$, comparable to the inward cubic contribution. A small
net mean velocity can coexist with large opposing effective modal forces.

For degree 9, cubic contraction persists at all 15 checkpoints. Quadratic
contraction is also substantial, whereas the median outward ninth-mode
velocity stays near $4.8\times10^{-12}$. This preserves the earlier
degree-9 explanation while showing why its fixed-sign picture does not
transfer unchanged to sine.

## Theory: accessible target error can initially favor smaller slopes

Consider the small-slope, identical-neuron, zero-hidden-bias example, with
the coarse output coefficient $\beta$ held fixed. To leading order, its
cubic output is $\alpha_3\beta a^2$, where the empirical polynomial
normalization gives $\alpha_3\simeq-0.087287$. Differentiating the cubic loss
along the coarse-preserving family gives

$$
\frac{d}{da}\frac12(\alpha_3\beta a^2-Y_3)^2
=2\alpha_3\beta a\,e_3.
$$

For positive common slope, $\alpha_3\beta e_3>0$ therefore predicts
contraction. Sine initially has negative $\beta$ and positive $e_3$, so
this sign prediction agrees with all five 100k checkpoints. A larger readout
can preserve the coarse output while slopes contract; the substantial cubic
target error need not oppose that motion.

This is an illustrative symmetric expansion, not a formula for the
heterogeneous network's full force. Applied unchanged at 400k, it predicts
the wrong sign in all five sine seeds. The exact effective-map projection
$A_3$ identifies the missing change. The framework therefore needs both
the evolving error and its evolving signed sensitivity.

Force concentration changes too. The normalized participation
$\|F_a\|_2^4/(W\|F_a\|_4^4)$ has sine medians $0.190$, $0.0358$, and
$0.00699$ at the three checkpoints. These correspond to roughly 34, 6, and
1 effective force-bearing neurons. This statistic describes concentration of
squared force; it is not a count of neurons acquiring a scale. Degree 9's
participation stays near $0.15$.

## Prediction: retain orientation and cancellation in the coupled model

An error-only explanation with fixed signed sensitivities cannot reproduce
the sine cubic reversal between 100k and 400k. A useful evolving-map model
must predict that reversal, then distinguish later error reversal from
sensitivity reversal. It must also predict the cancellation among modal
forces and the distribution of outward motion. A larger force norm alone
does not establish substantial population acquisition or precision fitting.

The matched-feedback campaign tests the consequences of allowing either
factor to evolve. These checkpoint observations explain why that test is
needed; its preissued branch forecasts provide the separate predictive test.

`forks.csv`, `modal_forces.csv`, and `cubic_map_sign.csv` retain individual
cases; `summary.json` records group summaries and the input digest.
The independently recomputed effective force agrees with the issued
checkpoint force to a maximum vector difference of $1.35\times10^{-16}$.
