# expD23 -- the zero-order ceiling: every `zero_order_rnn` solver, and why none can reach the floor

**Status: draft -- pending Sam's review.** Cell A (sine, $N{=}64$, qi) has every arm at one seed; the ceiling analysis covers all 12 qi cells; the breadth grid was cut for cost to $N{=}64$, three targets, seed 0, and the 3000-iteration `1.5-SPSA` line was dropped.

## TL;DR

- **Audit.** expD22 ported one of the five solvers in François's repo (`1SPSA` = CD-RGE). The other four (`1.5-SPSA`, `2SPSA`, `BanditSPSA`, `Sanger-SPSA`), the binary learning-rate search added in January 2026, and probe preconditioning were never run. All are ported here (`zo.py`), bit-checked against the upstream formulas, tuned, and run on the expD16 suite.
- **None of them beats Adam, and the best of them *is* Adam.** Every arm either tracks the Adam trajectory iteration-for-iteration (at $2n$ forwards per step instead of 3) or does worse; the record on cell A is $6.0\times10^{-3}$ (CD-RGE Adam-style at 3000 iterations, 600,000 forwards) against Adam's $3.5\times10^{-3}$ and the construction's $9\times10^{-15}$. Upstream's `1.5-SPSA` as actually executed is CD-RGE with $\text{lr}=\epsilon$ (its curvature division is computed and then not applied); `2SPSA` divides by a zero-mean random curvature and makes no progress; the binary LR search finds $\text{lr}=\epsilon\approx$ $3.2\times10^{-3}$ and then plateaus like everything else.
- **Proof, in three layers (section "Why", below).** (1) Any isotropic-probe estimator is the gradient plus zero-mean noise with an explicit covariance, and every curvature "normaliser" in the repo is a scalar, so the whole class is first-order with a scalar or diagonal step; on this spectrum ($\lambda_k/\lambda_1$ spans 23 decades, gapless) the expected iterate of any such method needs $\gtrsim10^{25}$ steps ($10^{12}$ with optimal momentum, $7\times10^{8}$ with a perfect diagonal preconditioner) to reach $10^{-13}$. (2) Curvature extracted from fp64 loss values has absolute error $\ge\varepsilon_{\rm mach}\,\max_i H_{ii}$ (the polarisation identity), so the finest preconditioner any zero-order method can build resolves the feature spectrum only to $\sigma_k/\sigma_1\approx\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\rm mach}}$: the loss is the normal equations, and the normal equations square the condition number. The Picard curve of the QI geometry then puts the zero-order *ceiling* at $E(\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\rm mach}})$, $10^{-11}$ to $10^{-7}$ across the 12 cells; measured with the full $m^2$ finite-difference Hessian on the readout block it is $10^{-8}$ to $10^{-6}$ -- $1.2\times10^{-7}$ on sine/$N{=}64$ vs $8.9\times10^{-15}$ for the SVD solve on the same $\Phi$. (3) A finite-difference gradient from fp64 loss values carries exactly the rounding error of the fp64 autograd gradient, so anything built on zero-order gradients is bounded by the exact-gradient record: expD16's best is $2\times10^{-7}$ (SSBroyden, $\Theta(m^2)$ state), expD11 showed no $O(dk)$ solver reaches the floor.
- François is right about the thing his method is for: at $n\gtrsim$ stable rank of $H$ (here $\approx1.2$), CD-RGE reproduces gradient descent per iteration, including its stability threshold ($\text{lr}<2/\lambda_1=6.1\times10^{-3}$, exactly where expD22 measured the divergence). What it reproduces is a first-order method, and no first-order method -- nor any method that sees only $L$ -- reaches machine precision on this problem.

## Question

Did expD22 miss a mechanism in François's zero-order repo that would let a zero-order optimizer train the one-layer tanh MLP past the first-order plateau; and if not, why not -- with a proof rather than a tuning table.

**Ceiling analysis (all 12 qi cells).** With the geometry frozen at the QI init the readout problem is exactly quadratic: $L(v,c)=\frac1n\|\Phi v+c\mathbf 1-y\|^2$, $H=\frac2n\Phi_a^T\Phi_a$, $\Phi_a=[\Phi,\mathbf 1]$. Two curves per cell:

- **Picard curve**: eval rel $L_2$ of the truncated-SVD solution keeping $\sigma_k>\tau\sigma_1$, for $\tau\in[10^{-18},1]$ -- what the geometry can deliver when directions down to $\tau$ are resolved. The lstsq floor is its value at $\tau=10^{-13}$ (the expD16 sanity gate).
- **Loss-oracle Newton**: gradient and Hessian of the readout block from **coordinate finite differences of the fp64 loss oracle only** ($2k$ and $2k+2k(k-1)$ evaluations, $k=W+1$; $\epsilon=10^{-1}$ for the Hessian, which on a quadratic has no truncation error), then one eigen-truncated Newton step at each $\tau$, then three refinement steps at the best $\tau$. This is the strongest zero-order method on the most favourable subproblem: a full second-order step from $\Theta(m^2)$ evaluations.

Plus, on cell A: the full-parameter Hessian ($616\times616$, autograd) at the qi init and at Adam's 3000-step plateau; its diagonally equilibrated version (van der Sluis); the distribution of $z^THz$ over Rademacher $z$ (the `1.5-SPSA` normaliser).

**Code & data.** `experiments/expD23_zo_ceiling/` (`zo.py`: oracles, the five solvers, LR search, FD gradient/Hessian, truncated Newton; `run.py`: arms, tuning, ceiling, spectra, grid, plots); tests `tests/test_expD23_zo_ceiling.py` (oracle == model loss; port == expD22 stream; `1.5-SPSA` normaliser == $z^THz$ with the predicted mean/std; FD gradient vs autograd $6\times10^{-12}$; FD Hessian at the polarisation floor; estimator covariance identity; truncated Newton solves a benign quadratic to $10^{-10}$). ## Why no zero-order method reaches the floor here (the proof)

Notation. $L(\theta)=\frac1n\|r(\theta)\|^2$, $r_i=f_\theta(x_i)-y_i$; $g=\nabla L$, $H=\nabla^2L$ with eigenpairs $(\lambda_k,u_k)$, $\lambda_1\ge\lambda_2\ge\cdots$; $\varepsilon_{\rm mach}=2^{-52}$. On the readout block $H=\frac2n\Phi_a^T\Phi_a$ exactly, so $\lambda_k=\frac2n\sigma_k^2$ with $\sigma_k$ the singular values of $\Phi_a$, and the error of the best fit using the directions $\sigma_k>\tau\sigma_1$ is the Picard curve $E(\tau)$ (figure 2). A **zero-order method** is any algorithm whose only access to the problem is fp64 values of $L$ at points of its choosing. Measured on cell A (figure 3): $\lambda_1=326$, $\mathrm{tr}H=396$ (stable rank $1.2$), $\lambda_k/\lambda_1$ gapless down to $10^{-23}$ at the init and $10^{-25}$ at Adam's plateau; $\sigma_k/\sigma_1$ of $\Phi_a$ reaches $10^{-16}$ at $k\approx100$ of 206; $E(10^{-13})=8.9\times10^{-15}$, $E(10^{-8})=1.8\times10^{-9}$, and reaching $10^{-13}$ needs $\tau\le$ $10^{-13}$.

**Lemma 1 (what a probe is).** For a Rademacher probe $z$, $c(z)=\frac{L(\theta+\epsilon z)-L(\theta-\epsilon z)}{2\epsilon}=z^Tg+\frac{\epsilon^2}{6}\nabla^3L[z,z,z]+\rho$, where $\rho$ is rounding. The estimate $\hat g=\frac1n\sum_jc(z_j)z_j$ satisfies $\mathbb E\hat g=g+O(\epsilon^2)$ and, on a quadratic, exactly
$$\mathrm{Cov}(\hat g)=\frac1n\big(\|g\|^2I+gg^T-2\,\mathrm{diag}(g_i^2)\big),$$
(verified numerically in the tests): zero-mean noise of relative size $\sqrt{(m-1)/n}$, isotropic up to a rank-one and a diagonal term. The `1.5-SPSA` normaliser is $\kappa(z)=z^THz+O(\epsilon^2)$ with $\mathbb E\kappa=\mathrm{tr}H$ and $\mathrm{Var}\,\kappa=2(\|H\|_F^2-\sum_iH_{ii}^2)$ (test; on cell A mean $392$, std $460$, figure 3b); the `2SPSA` normaliser is $v^THu$ with mean $0$ and std $\|H\|_F$. Both are scalars attached to $z_j$: they rescale a probe's coefficient, they cannot rotate the step. Hence every solver in the repo takes the step
$$\theta_{t+1}=\theta_t-\eta_tP_t\hat g_t,$$
with $P_t$ the identity (`1SPSA`, `1.5-SPSA`, `2SPSA`, `Bandit`, LR search), a diagonal built from past $\hat g$ (RMSProp/Adam), or $WW^T+\alpha I$ (`Sanger`), and $\hat g_t$ as above (for `Bandit`, a probe distribution biased toward directions of large $\lvert z^Tg\rvert$, which biases $\mathbb E\hat g$ toward the *stiff* directions -- the wrong ones).

**Lemma 2 (the noise does not even bind here).** On a quadratic, one scalar step gives $\mathbb E[L_{t+1}\mid\theta_t]=L_t-\eta\|g\|^2+\frac{\eta^2}2g^THg+\frac{\eta^2}{2}\mathrm{tr}(H\,\mathrm{Cov}\hat g)$, and by Lemma 1 the last term is $\frac{\eta^2}{2n}(\|g\|^2\mathrm{tr}H+g^THg-2\sum_iH_{ii}g_i^2)$. So the zero-order penalty relative to exact gradient descent is the factor $1+\mathrm{tr}H/(n\lambda_1)$ on the admissible step; with stable rank $\mathrm{tr}H/\lambda_1=1.2$ it is $1+1.2/n$: **at $n\ge10$, CD-RGE is gradient descent per iteration.** This is why expD22 found $n=100$ and $n=1000$ indistinguishable, why the Adam-style ZO line lies on Adam's trajectory, and why the author's tie $\text{lr}=\epsilon$ diverged exactly above $2/\lambda_1=6.1\times10^{-3}$ (expD22 stage 1: stable at $3\times10^{-3}$, divergent at $10^{-2}$). Zero-order buys the gradient at $2n$ forwards; nothing in the estimator changes what the gradient can do.

**Theorem A (first-order methods cannot resolve this spectrum).** Take the readout block (exact quadratic, $e_t=\theta_t-\theta^*$, $g_t=He_t$) and any method $\theta_{t+1}=\theta_t-\eta_t\hat g_t$ with $\hat g_t=g_t+\xi_t$, $\mathbb E[\xi_t\mid\theta_t]=0$ (Lemma 1), any step schedule, any $n$, any $\epsilon$. Then $\mathbb E\,e_t=\prod_{s<t}(I-\eta_sH)\,e_0$, so along each eigenvector
$$\lvert\mathbb E\,u_k^Te_t\rvert=\prod_{s<t}\lvert1-\eta_s\lambda_k\rvert\;\lvert u_k^Te_0\rvert .$$
Stability of the stiff direction forces $\eta_s\le2/\lambda_1$: a step with $\eta_s\lambda_1>2$ multiplies both $\mathbb E\,u_1^Te$ and, since $\mathbb E(u_1^T\xi)^2\ge(\|g\|^2-2\max_ig_i^2)/n>0$, the variance of $u_1^Te$ by a factor $>1$, which is the divergence expD22 measured. With $\eta_s\le2/\lambda_1$, $\lvert1-\eta_s\lambda_k\rvert\ge1-2\lambda_k/\lambda_1$, and Jensen ($\mathbb E X^2\ge(\mathbb EX)^2$) gives
$$\mathbb E\,L_t\;\ge\;\tfrac12\sum_k\lambda_k\,(1-2\lambda_k/\lambda_1)^{2t}\,(u_k^Te_0)^2\;\ge\;\tfrac12\sum_{k:\;\lambda_k\le\lambda_1/(8t)}\lambda_k\,e^{-1}\,(u_k^Te_0)^2 .$$
After $t$ steps every direction with $\lambda_k<\lambda_1/(8t)$ is untouched to within a factor $e$, and the loss is at least the Picard tail beyond it. Reaching rel $L_2=10^{-13}$ requires the directions down to $\sigma_k/\sigma_1=$ $10^{-13}$, i.e. $\lambda_k/\lambda_1=$ $10^{-26}$, i.e. $t\gtrsim$ $\lambda_1/(8\lambda_k)\approx10^{25}$ steps -- at $2n$ evaluations each. Momentum and Nesterov acceleration replace the product by a degree-$t$ polynomial with $p_t(0)=1$; the Chebyshev bound $\max_{[\lambda_k,\lambda_1]}\lvert p_t\rvert\ge2/(\rho^t+\rho^{-t})$, $\rho\approx1+2\sqrt{\lambda_k/\lambda_1}$, replaces $\lambda_1/\lambda_k$ by its square root: $\gtrsim10^{12}$ steps. A fixed diagonal preconditioner $D$ (RMSProp; Adam with its slowly varying $v$) replaces $H$ by $D^{1/2}HD^{1/2}$, whose condition number is at least $\kappa_{\rm eq}/m$ with $\kappa_{\rm eq}$ that of the unit-diagonal scaling (van der Sluis 1969); measured $\kappa_{\rm eq}=2.8\times10^{19}$ at Adam's plateau, so even a diagonally preconditioned, optimally accelerated method needs $\gtrsim\sqrt{\kappa_{\rm eq}/m}=7\times10^{8}$ steps. The Sanger preconditioner $WW^T+\alpha I$ adds curvature in the top-$r$ *gradient* directions, which are the stiff ones; it does not touch the $\lambda_k\ll\lambda_1$ directions that carry the error. The full problem adds the $O(\epsilon^2)$ bias and non-convexity (312 negative eigenvalues at the init) to all of the above; it does not remove the spectrum (figure 3a: the plateau spectrum is gapless over 25 decades).

**Theorem B (curvature from loss values is capped at $\varepsilon_{\rm mach}\max_iH_{ii}$).** Any second-order information a zero-order method extracts is a linear combination of loss values. For the mixed second difference of a quadratic the four values are $L(\theta\pm\epsilon e_i\pm\epsilon e_j)=L\pm\epsilon g_i\pm\epsilon g_j+\frac{\epsilon^2}2(H_{ii}+H_{jj})\pm\epsilon^2H_{ij}$, whose sum is $4L+2\epsilon^2(H_{ii}+H_{jj})$ exactly; each fp64 value carries an independent relative error of order $\varepsilon_{\rm mach}$, so
$$\big\lvert\hat H_{ij}-H_{ij}\big\rvert\;\gtrsim\;\varepsilon_{\rm mach}\,\frac{4L+2\epsilon^2(H_{ii}+H_{jj})}{4\epsilon^2}\;\ge\;\varepsilon_{\rm mach}\,\frac{H_{ii}+H_{jj}}2\qquad\text{for every }\epsilon .$$
The same bound holds for any other stencil (the coefficients sum in absolute value to at least what the curvature term contributes) and for any point $\theta$, including the minimiser. So $H$ is available to a zero-order method only to absolute accuracy $\mu\ge\varepsilon_{\rm mach}\max_iH_{ii}$ in norm, and its eigen-directions with $\lambda_k\lesssim\mu\sqrt m$ are noise. On the readout block $\lambda_k=\frac2n\sigma_k^2$ and $H_{ii}=\frac2n\|\Phi_{:,i}\|^2\le\lambda_1$, so the resolvable directions are $\sigma_k/\sigma_1\gtrsim\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\rm mach}}\approx1.5\times10^{-8}$: **the loss is the normal-equations functional, and the normal equations square the condition number** ($\kappa(H)=\kappa(\Phi)^2$; Higham, *Accuracy and Stability*, ch. 20). The QR/SVD solve that the construction uses sees the columns of $\Phi$ directly, whose rounding error is $\varepsilon_{\rm mach}\sigma_1$, and resolves $\sigma_k/\sigma_1$ down to $\varepsilon_{\rm mach}$ -- eight more decades of spectrum, which by the Picard curve is the difference between $E(10^{-8})\approx10^{-9}$ and $E(10^{-13})\approx10^{-14}$. Consequently the best step any zero-order method can take from curvature is the truncated (or shifted, $\mu$-regularised) Newton step at level $\mu$, whose one-shot floor is $E(\tau_\mu)$ and whose remaining directions are left to Theorem A with $\lambda_1$ replaced by $\mu$: $t\gtrsim\mu/\lambda_k$ steps of $\Theta(m^2)$ evaluations each. Measured on cell A: the coordinate FD Hessian ($341{,}140$ evaluations) has absolute error $2.6\times10^{-14}$ ($\approx60\,\varepsilon_{\rm mach}\max_iH_{ii}$, the $4L$ and gradient terms in the stencil), the best truncated Newton step lands at $1.2\times10^{-7}$ (at $\tau=3\times10^{-15}$ on $\lambda$, i.e. $\sigma/\sigma_1\approx5\times10^{-8}$), three refinement steps do not move it ($1.2\times10^{-7}$ after each of the three), and the SVD solve on the same $\Phi$ gives $8.9\times10^{-15}$. Across the 12 cells the loss-oracle Newton floor is $1.8e-08$ to $1.5e-06$ (after refinement) against lstsq floors of $10^{-14}$ (figure 2). This is also why expD16's exact-gradient SSBroyden stops at $2\times10^{-7}$: its inverse Hessian is built from gradient differences $g(\theta+s)-g(\theta)$ in fp64, whose rounding is $\varepsilon_{\rm mach}\lambda_1\|s\|$ -- the same $\mu$.

**Theorem C (zero-order gradients are fp64 gradients, not better).** The optimal coordinate central difference has error $\min_\epsilon\varepsilon_{\rm mach}\frac{2L+\epsilon^2H_{ii}}{2\epsilon}=\varepsilon_{\rm mach}\sqrt{2LH_{ii}}=\frac{2\varepsilon_{\rm mach}}{n}\|r\|\,\|\Phi_{:,i}\|$ on the readout block -- the same expression as the rounding error of the fp64 inner product $g_i=\frac2n\Phi_{:,i}^Tr$ that autograd computes (up to the $\sqrt n$ accumulation constant). Measured: the FD gradient agrees with autograd to $6\times10^{-12}$ relative at the init, and `fdgrad_adam` retraces Adam's trajectory (figure 1, identical to $1.8\times10^{-9}$ relative at every one of the 400 logged iterations). So a zero-order method with $2m$ evaluations per step has exactly a first-order oracle, and everything one can build on it -- L-BFGS, Newton-CG, SSBroyden, the block-QR whiteners of expD10 -- is bounded by the exact-gradient record: $2\times10^{-7}$ at best (expD16), the fp64 Krylov barrier of expD11 (reaching the floor needs $\Theta(d^2)$ stored orthogonality), all 6+ orders above the target, at $2m$ forwards per gradient instead of one backward.

**Putting it together.** A zero-order method on this problem is either (A) a first-order method with a scalar or diagonal step -- which, on a gapless 25-decade spectrum, cannot resolve the directions that carry the last 10 decades of error in any number of steps a computer will take, and which the measurements show is what every tuned arm becomes (it converges to Adam's trajectory and Adam's plateau); or (B) a method that learns curvature from loss values -- which is capped at $\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\rm mach}}$ of the feature spectrum by the polarisation identity, a ceiling of $10^{-7}$ to $10^{-9}$ on these cells that the $m^2$-evaluation oracle Newton arm attains and cannot pass; or (C) a method that builds on zero-order gradients -- which are fp64 gradients, bounded by the exact-gradient record at $2\times10^{-7}$. The target of $10^{-13}$ is reached in this repo only by algorithms that see $\Phi$ itself (QR/SVD, the construction), and that is not something a loss oracle provides. What François's method does deliver, it delivers here too: gradient descent per iteration at the cost of $2n$ forwards.

## Conclusions

*Pending Sam.* Data-obvious so far: none of the five upstream solvers, the LR search, or probe preconditioning moves the precision frontier -- the best zero-order line is Adam's line at $\sim50\times$ the forwards -- and the loss-oracle Newton ceiling ($10^{-8}$ to $10^{-6}$ on all 12 cells, from the polarisation bound) sits 5 to 7 decades above the construction's floor on the same geometry. The three-layer argument above is the reason: a loss oracle is a normal-equations oracle.

## Open questions

- None proposed for the zero-order class. The only route past $\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\rm mach}}$ is an oracle that exposes $\Phi$ (or $J$) rather than $L$ -- which is the existing step-2 program (block-QR on the stored activations), not a zero-order method.
