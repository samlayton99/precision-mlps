# expC08 -- The frequency-resolved $\lambda$ rule vs the expC07 constant rule

**Status:** draft-pending-Sam (data and figures in; conclusions not yet reviewed).

## TL;DR

- **The exact aliasing floor is confirmed, and the frequency-resolved rule is a conservative relative of it, not the floor itself**: in every resolved cell of all three activations the measured right wall lies on the fiber-theorem curve $\sqrt{\Lambda_r(\theta_0;\lambda)}$ (gray dash-dot in the grids). The tested rule uses the same two ghost ratios but drops the integration factor $(\theta_0/(2\pi\mp\theta_0))^r$ and adds the ghost amplitudes instead of their squares, so it sets a larger quantity equal to $\varepsilon$; the exact output formula was not the thing being tested as a rule here.
- **But the optimum does not follow it at high frequency per cell.** As $q=h\Omega/\pi$ rises to $0.5$ the new rule halves $\lambda$ (tanh $0.235\to0.12$); the measured wall only falls by about a quarter (tanh $0.31\to0.23$, swish $0.72\to0.52$, gelu $0.88\to0.63$), and the measured minima rise to $10^{-10}$-$10^{-12}$. The left (conditioning) wall moves right with $q$ as fast as the aliasing wall moves left, which the rule does not model; at the new rule's $\lambda$ the error in the $q=0.5$ cells is $10^2$-$10^4\times$ worse than at expC07's constant.
- **At low $q$ both rules are safe but conservative**: the measured wall sits at $1.3\times$ the new limit (tanh $0.31$ vs $0.236$, gelu $0.88$ vs $0.687$, swish $0.72$ vs $0.431$) because the least-squares floor carries the $(\theta_0/2\pi)^r$ attenuation the rule omits; the floor between is flat, so both choices reach the same error. Gelu is the one activation whose optimum tracks the new rule's drift, since its conditioning wall is mild (entire kernel).
- **Halo (Sam's question)**: the halo requirement is a kernel reach in grid cells, not a fraction of $N$. One rule, **halo $=32$ centers per side at every width**, is within $1.6\times$ of the default $\max(70,0.4N)$ at the 90th percentile of resolved cells for $\lambda\ge0.15$, typically *beats* it (the oversized halo adds floor noise), and cuts $W$ from 921 to 577 at $N=512$. Halo 16 fails on gelu/swish below $\lambda\approx0.18$; halo 0 loses 5-10 decades. All main figures use halo 32.

## Question / hypothesis

expC07's rule gives one $\lambda^*$ per activation, independent of the target and the width. Sam's frequency-resolved rule (2026-09-08) budgets fp64 $\varepsilon=2^{-52}$ against the two grid ghosts of the target's own frequency, so the certified $\lambda$ falls as the target approaches Nyquist. Does the measured $\lambda$ wall move with $(\Omega, N)$ the way the new rule says, or stay put the way expC07 says?

## Experiment design

**Model and solve** (identical to expC07). Frozen QI geometry on $[-1,1]$: uniform centers $c_k=-1+kh$ with $h=2/N$, halo of 32 centers on each side (chosen from a ladder, see below; the default $\max(70,0.4N)$ is shown for contrast), one shared $\gamma=\lambda/h$, features $\Phi_{ik}=\psi(\gamma(x_i-c_k))$ for $\psi\in\{\tanh,\mathrm{gelu},\mathrm{swish}\}$, augmented system $[\Phi,\mathbf 1]$ solved by SVD lstsq (`gelsd`) on a uniform grid of $\max(2003,\,2\cdot\#\text{centers}+3)$ points, scored on 4001 uniform points by rel $L_2=\|\hat f-f\|_2/\|f\|_2$ (and $L_\infty$, recorded but not plotted).

**Sweep.** 3 activations $\times$ 6 targets $\times$ 6 widths $N\in\{16,32,64,128,256,512\}$ $\times$ 60 bandwidths $\lambda\in\mathrm{geomspace}(0.03,1.5)$ $\times$ a halo ladder $\{0,4,8,16,32,64,\max(70,0.4N)\}$, 45360 solves. Targets, with their top frequency $\Omega/\pi$ and spectral mass $B=\sum|\hat f_k|$: $\sin\pi x$ (1), $\cos4\pi x$ (4), $f_8=\sin3\pi x\cos5\pi x=\tfrac12[\sin8\pi x-\sin2\pi x]$ (8), $\sin16\pi x$ (16), $f_{32}=0.8\sin2\pi x+0.2\cos32\pi x$ (32), $\sin100\pi x$ (100); $B=1$ for all six.

**The two rules being compared.** Both use the normalized kernel transform $\widehat K(\xi)/\widehat K(0)$ of the activation's kernel $K=\psi^{(r)}$ (tanh: $\tfrac{\pi\xi/2}{\sinh(\pi\xi/2)}$; gelu: $(1+\xi^2)e^{-\xi^2/2}$; swish: $\pi^2\xi^2\cosh(\pi\xi)/\sinh^2(\pi\xi)$).

- **Previous rule (expC07, black dotted).** $A(\lambda)=|\widehat K(2\pi/\lambda)|/|\widehat K(0)|=\varepsilon^*$ with $\varepsilon^*=A_{\tanh}(0.25)=5.65\times10^{-16}$: tanh $0.250$, gelu $0.707$, swish $0.455$, the same in every panel.
- **New rule (Sam's tables, red dashed).** With grid frequency $\theta_0=h\Omega$, $h=2/(N-1)$, $q=\theta_0/\pi$, solve for the largest $\lambda$ with
$$\frac{2\left[\widehat K\!\left(\frac{2\pi-\theta_0}{\lambda}\right)+\widehat K\!\left(\frac{2\pi+\theta_0}{\lambda}\right)\right]}{\widehat K(\theta_0/\lambda)}=\frac{\varepsilon}{B},\qquad \varepsilon=2^{-52},$$
rounded down to three decimals. `predictions.py` reproduces all 90 table entries from this one constraint (for tanh, Sam's closed form (2), $8ze^{-z}(1-e^{-z})^{-3}\sinh(qz)/(qz)=\varepsilon/B$ with $z=\pi^2/\lambda$, agrees to one unit in the third decimal). The $q\to0$ limits are tanh $0.2358$, gelu $0.6866$, swish $0.4313$, i.e. the expC07 rule with budget $\varepsilon/4$ instead of $\varepsilon^*$. Cells where $q\ge1$ (target at or past Nyquist) have no certified $\lambda$ and are marked in the panel. The $h=2/(N-1)$ vs $h=2/N$ convention moves entries by at most one unit in the third decimal.

**Halo rule.** Sam's requirement: one halo per width, big enough never to matter and no bigger, never tied to $\lambda$ (a $\lambda$-dependent halo would confound the $\lambda$ curves). Per width and ladder halo $h$, the penalty $\mathrm{rel}L_2(h,\lambda)/\mathrm{rel}L_2(\text{default},\lambda)$ (both 5-point running medians in $\log$) is collected over resolved cells (cell minimum $\le10^{-6}$) and $\lambda\in[0.15,1.5]$; the rule is the smallest halo whose 90th-percentile penalty is $\le1.6$ at every width. This gives halo 32 for all six widths (`halo_rule.py`).

**Fiber floor overlay.** The exact least-squares aliasing floor of `docs/lambda_rule_theory.md` (Theorem 2): for a target with modes $(\omega_k,a_k)$, $\big(\sum_k a_k^2\Lambda_r(\omega_kh;\lambda)/\sum_k a_k^2\big)^{1/2}$ with $\Lambda_r(\theta;\lambda)=\sum_{m\ne0}\widehat K(\tfrac{\theta+2\pi m}{\lambda})^2|\theta+2\pi m|^{-2r}\big/\sum_m(\cdot)$, $r=1$ (tanh), $2$ (gelu, swish), $h=2/N$, $|m|\le8$.

**Metrics per cell.** The measured argmin $\lambda$; the *wall*, defined as the largest $\lambda$ whose smoothed error is within $10\times$ of the smoothed minimum (the right shoulder of the floor); and the measured error at each rule's $\lambda$ (log-interpolated on the grid).

## Results

**Reading the grids** (`expC08_grid_{tanh,gelu,swish}.png`; rows = targets by rising $\Omega$, columns = widths, so $q=h\Omega/\pi$ halves along each row and doubles down each column; cells on the same anti-diagonal share $q$).

- **The right wall is the fiber floor.** In every cell with a resolved target the blue curve leaves the flat floor exactly where the gray dash-dot $\sqrt{\Lambda_r(\theta_0;\lambda)}$ curve crosses it and rides that curve upward. This holds for tanh ($r=1$), gelu and swish ($r=2$) alike, at every $q$ from $0.004$ to $0.5$; it is the strongest confirmation of the exact aliasing formula in the record (fiber_check.py covered one target). Since the new rule is precisely "$\sqrt{\Lambda}$-type quantity $=\varepsilon$", its $q$-dependence is right *for the wall it models*.
- **Low $q$ (the top-right triangle): flat floor, both rules left of the wall.** The floor runs from the conditioning side up to a wall at $\lambda\approx0.31$-$0.35$ (tanh), $0.83$-$0.94$ (gelu), $0.68$-$0.77$ (swish), i.e. about $1.3\times$ the new rule's $q\to0$ limit and $1.25\times$ expC07's constant. The gap is the $(\theta_0/2\pi)^r$ attenuation of the least-squares floor (theory doc, Cor. 2.2), which neither rule includes; on a flat floor the argmin wanders (dots scatter from $0.03$ to $0.3$ for tanh $\sin\pi x$), so the wall, not the argmin, is the observable. Errors at the new and previous $\lambda$ agree to within jitter in every such cell.
- **High $q$ (the anti-diagonal $q=0.5$: $\cos4\pi x$ at $N{=}16$, $f_8$ at 32, $\sin16\pi x$ at 64, $f_{32}$ at 128): the rules diverge and the data sides with the constant.** The U is now sharp with a minimum well above $\varepsilon$ ($10^{-10}$ tanh and swish, $10^{-12}$ gelu). For tanh the argmin sits at $0.18$-$0.21$ and the wall at $0.22$-$0.25$, against the new rule's $0.12$-$0.13$; the error at the new rule's $\lambda$ is $10^{-6}$-$10^{-7}$, at expC07's $0.25$ it is $10^{-9}$. Swish is the same story ($0.35$-$0.46$ measured vs $0.24$-$0.25$ new vs $0.455$ previous; $10^{-6}$ vs $10^{-9}$). Gelu differs: its argmin drops to $0.52$-$0.59$, below expC07's $0.707$ and close to the new rule's $0.49$-$0.50$, and the new rule's $\lambda$ is the better of the two there ($10^{-11}$ vs $10^{-9}$).
- **$\sin100\pi x$ ($q=0.78$ at $N{=}256$, $0.39$ at $512$)** is the extreme case: at $N=256$ every activation's minimum is $10^{-5}$-$10^{-7}$ and the new rule's $\lambda$ ($0.057$ tanh, $0.113$ swish, $0.336$ gelu) gives $O(1)$ error, i.e. the target is not even resolved there.

**Halo** (`expC08_halo_{act}.png`, `expC08_halo_adequacy.png`). Halo 0 is catastrophic everywhere (5-10 decades). Halo 4 and 8 lose at small $\lambda$ and, for gelu and swish, across most of the basin at $N\ge128$; halo 16 is enough for tanh but fails gelu/swish for $\lambda\lesssim0.18$ (worst cell $30$-$70\times$ the default). From halo 32 up the curves coincide with the default across the basin and the wall, and the geometric-mean penalty of halo 32 relative to the default is $0.6$-$0.9$, i.e. the default's extra saturated columns cost a factor of $1$-$2$ on the floor. The requirement does not grow with $N$ in cells; it grows as $\lambda$ shrinks (kernel reach), which is why a width-proportional rule is the wrong shape. The $\lambda$-dependent alternative was rejected because it would confound the $\lambda$ curves. A linear column for the $r=2$ activations (their polynomial part $\mathcal P_1$) was tested and helps only at halo $\le16$, so it is not the mechanism.

## Additional details

- **Which constraint the tables solve.** All 90 entries of Sam's three tables are reproduced exactly (floor to three decimals, $h=2/(N-1)$) by the single two-ghost constraint stated in the design section; tanh's closed form (2) differs from it by at most one unit in the third decimal. The constraint is the fiber theorem's leading-order floor with the $(\theta_0/(2\pi\mp\theta_0))^r$ attenuation dropped and the two ghosts added rather than root-sum-squared, i.e. the $r=0$/conservative form, with budget $\varepsilon$ against $4A(\lambda)$; hence the $q\to0$ limits are expC07's rule at $\varepsilon/4$ instead of $\varepsilon^*=5.65\times10^{-16}$.
- **Why tanh and swish do not follow the rule at high $q$ (reading, not yet a theorem).** The rule's wall is $\widehat K((2\pi-\theta_0)/\lambda)/\widehat K(\theta_0/\lambda)=\varepsilon$; the denominator is the same signal attenuation that sets the coefficient amplification $1/\widehat K(\theta_0/\lambda)$ on the conditioning side (theory doc, Section 5). For a pole-type kernel both are $e^{-\pi\theta_0/(2\lambda)}$-sized, so lowering $\lambda$ to buy aliasing margin raises the roundoff floor by the same exponential, and the two walls meet at a $\lambda$ that moves only slowly with $q$ while the floor between them rises. For the Gaussian-tailed gelu kernel the attenuation is $e^{-\theta_0^2/(2\lambda^2)}$, negligible at $q=0.5$, so the aliasing wall alone sets the optimum and the rule works. A two-wall rule would replace $\varepsilon$ on the right-hand side by $\varepsilon/\widehat K(\theta_0/\lambda)$; this was not fitted here.
- **Metrics caveat.** The "wall" is defined on a 5-point running median of the curve and is unreliable when the floor sits within a decade of $\varepsilon$ (two tanh $\sin\pi x$ cells at $N=128,256$ report walls at $0.13$-$0.16$ because their floor slopes downward to the left); the grids, not the summary numbers, are the evidence in those cells.
- **Halo selection numbers** (`halo_rule.json`): 90th-percentile penalty of halo 32 vs default over $\lambda\ge0.15$ by width $16\to512$: $1.25, 1.37, 1.25, 1.39, 1.50, 1.52$; geometric mean $0.59, 0.75, 0.75, 0.86, 0.90, 0.68$; worst single cell $5$-$12\times$, always either at $\lambda\approx0.16$ (edge of the kernel reach for swish/gelu) or on the steep right wall where a small shift is a large ratio. Halo 64 is no better ($1.3$-$2.1$ at the 90th percentile) and worse at $N=16,32$.

## Conclusions

Pending Sam's review. What the data shows plainly: the exact aliasing floor predicts the right wall in every resolved cell; the frequency-resolved rule inherits that and is correct about *which side* it models, but the measured optimum for tanh and swish drifts with $q$ far less than the rule (about $(1-q/2)$ rather than $(1-q)$ in the exponent), so expC07's constant remains the better single choice for those activations in the near-Nyquist regime, while gelu follows the new rule. The halo needed by the least-squares solve is 32 cells per side at every width.

## Open questions

- A two-wall rule: solve aliasing $=\varepsilon/\widehat K(\theta_0/\lambda)$ and test whether it lands on the $q=0.5$ minima of tanh and swish; the exponent count above predicts $\lambda\propto(1-q/2)$ for tanh.
- Is the $1.3\times$ gap between the measured wall and the $q\to0$ limit exactly the $(\theta_0/2\pi)^r$ attenuation (the leading-order rule in the theory doc, which gave $0.28$ for $\sin2\pi x$ at $N=128$), and does the $10\times$-shoulder convention account for the rest?
- The halo requirement in cells as a function of $\lambda$ and kernel (a reach rule $c_K/\lambda$); whether 32 remains enough for the QI construction proper, which the paper's halo term $e^{-c_3\lambda R}$ governs, as opposed to the lstsq readout tested here.
