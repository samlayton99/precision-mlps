# expC09 -- The fiber-theorem floor predicts the $\lambda$ curve for general targets

**Status:** draft-pending-Sam (data and figures in; no conclusions signed off).

## TL;DR

- With no fitted constant, the whole-line fiber projection of `docs/lambda_rule_theory.md` predicts the measured right wall of the $\lambda$ curve for rational functions, a near-step, a Gaussian bump, $e^{\sin3\pi x}$, $e^x$, $x^4-x^2$ and $|x|^3$, on all three activations and four widths: measured/predicted on the wall is $1.000$ to three or four digits for every bounded analytic target, $0.86$ to $1.00$ for the growing targets $e^x$ and $x^4-x^2$ (converging to $1$ as $N$ grows), and $0.93$ to $0.99$ for $|x|^3$.
- The same formula predicts the flat resolution floors: Runge at $N=64,128$ ($10^{-8}$, $10^{-4}$ for the sharper pole), $\tanh10x$ at $N=64$, and $|x|^3$ at every width, including the kink where the floor turns into the wall.
- The one failure is the chirp $\sin(10\pi x^2)$ at $N=64,128$, where the prediction depends on how the target is extended past the interval: the analytic continuation keeps accelerating and crosses Nyquist a few units out, and a smoothly frozen-frequency extension cannot be made accurate enough (any finite-order junction leaks at $10^{-7}$). At $N\ge256$ the chirp is predicted too.
- **The left wall is predicted too, with the right slope, once the solver's truncation rule is put into the projection.** Fibers whose Gram singular value falls below $\varepsilon\,\sigma_{\max}$ are dropped and their content becomes error; that curve (still no fitted constant) lies on the measured left wall of the rational, step and bump targets within a factor $1$-$3$ (gelu median $1.3$, tanh and swish $2.3$-$2.4$ over all left-wall points), and the backward-stability line $\varepsilon\|A\|_2\|\hat x\|_2/\|b\|_2$ with $c=1$ runs parallel to it a factor $3$-$6$ below. So the left wall of these targets is loss of their own high-frequency content past the truncation cutoff, not roundoff amplification. It is a model with an order-one constant, not an identity like the right wall; the ratio drifts upward with $N$.
- Neither left-wall line works for the growing targets or the chirp (the coefficient norm is dominated by the extension region) and the flat fp64 floor between the walls is not predicted by anything here.

## Question / hypothesis

expC08 showed the exact fiber floor $\sqrt{\Lambda_r(\theta_0;\lambda)}$ sitting on the measured wall for pure tones. Does the general form of the same theorem predict the curve for targets that are not sinusoids, and what does the prediction need?

## Experiment design

**Measured side** (expC08's solver). Frozen uniform grid on $[-1,1]$, $h=2/N$, halo 256 centers per side for the main curves (so the small-$\lambda$ side is not halo-limited; the halo-32 curve of the expC08 rule is drawn in light gray and coincides with it for $\lambda\ge0.15$), $\gamma=\lambda/h$, features $\psi(\gamma(x-c_k))$ for $\psi\in\{\tanh,\mathrm{gelu},\mathrm{swish}\}$, augmented $[\Phi,\mathbf 1]$ solved by SVD lstsq (`gelsd`) on $\max(2003, 2\cdot\#\text{centers}+3)$ uniform points, rel $L_2$ on 4001 points. Sweep: 3 activations $\times$ 10 targets $\times$ $N\in\{64,128,256,512\}$ $\times$ 60 $\lambda\in\mathrm{geomspace}(0.03,1.5)$, 7200 solves per halo. The solver also returns the singular values of $[\Phi,\mathbf 1]$ and the coefficient norm $\|\hat x\|_2$.

**Targets.** Group A (bounded on the line): $1/(1+25x^2)$, $1/(1+100x^2)$, $1/((x-0.5)^2+0.04)$, $\tanh(10x)$, $e^{-20x^2}$. Group B: $x^4-x^2$, $e^x$, $e^{\sin3\pi x}$, $\sin(10\pi x^2)$, $|x|^3$.

**Predicted side: the general fiber projection.** In grid units $u=x/h$ the network spans lattice translates of the kernel $K=\psi^{(r)}$ ($r=1$ tanh, $2$ gelu and swish) integrated $r$ times, plus a constant. For a target $\tilde f$ on the whole line with transform $\hat f$, the best $L_2$ approximant is a projection that acts fiber by fiber (Theorem 1 of the theory doc): on the fiber $\{\theta+2\pi m\}_m$ the coefficient is $c^*(\theta)=\langle F_\theta,\mathrm W_\theta\rangle/\|\mathrm W_\theta\|^2$ with $\mathrm W_\theta=(w_r(\theta+2\pi m))_m$, $w_r(\theta)=\lambda^{r-1}\widehat K(\theta/\lambda)/(i\theta)^r$, and the error transform is $\hat f-c^*w_r$. Implementation: a periodic box of $L$ cells sampled 16 times per cell, FFT, fibers as residues of the DFT index mod $L$, the $m=0$ residual computed as $(\hat f_0S_{\ne0}-w_0\sum_{m\ne0}\hat f_m\bar w_m)/S$ to avoid cancellation, each fiber normalized by its largest $|w|$, the constant fiber taken as exact, inverse FFT, and the RMS of the error taken over the interval only, divided by the RMS of $f$ on the interval. On a strictly periodic tone this reproduces $\sqrt{\Lambda_r}$ to five digits; its numerical floor is $6\times10^{-16}$.

**The extension.** The theorem needs the target on the whole line; the network only sees $[-1,1]$. The target is continued by its own formula for $R$ cells past each end and then tapered to zero over 200 cells with a $C^\infty$ window. The taper creates near-Nyquist content whose projection error leaks back toward the interior at about $\lambda/2$ per cell (measured: a taper 16 cells out contaminates the interior at $10^{-7}$), so $R=800$ cells for bounded targets; for growing targets the continuation is capped at $|x|\le4$ ($e^x$), $3$ ($x^4-x^2$), $6$ ($|x|^3$) so the box amplitude stays within fp64 dynamic range, and for the chirp at $|x|\le1.6$ with a 60-cell taper because its continuation crosses Nyquist. A second prediction at reach $R/2$ is drawn; where the two differ, the prediction depends on the extension. Extensions are chosen a priori, never from the measured curve.

**The truncation rule and the left wall.** `gelsd` discards singular directions below $\varepsilon\,\sigma_{\max}$. In the fiber model the Gram of fiber $\theta$ has singular value $\big(\sum_m|w_r(\theta+2\pi m)|^2\big)^{1/2}$; fibers below $\varepsilon$ times the largest fiber's value are dropped (coefficient $0$, their content left as error). This is applied inside the projection, so the red curve includes truncation loss. Separately, the backward-stability bound of the SVD solve, $\|A\hat x-Pb\|\le c\,\varepsilon(\|A\|\|\hat x\|+\|b\|)$, is drawn with $c=1$ as $E_{\text{left}}=\varepsilon\,\sigma_{\max}(\lambda)\,\|\hat x\|_{\text{fiber}}/\|b\|$, where $\sigma_{\max}$ is the solver's largest singular value and $\|\hat x\|_{\text{fiber}}$ is the 2-norm of the fiber coefficients $\hat a(\theta)=\hat f(\theta)/w_r(\theta)$ (kept fibers only) over the cells the solve uses, $|u_k|\le N/2+\text{halo}$. The solver's own coefficient norm agrees with the fiber formula to a median factor $0.7$-$1.1$ on the left-wall points.

**Metrics.** Per cell, the ratio measured/predicted at the $\lambda$ points on a wall (measured error above $30\times$ the cell's floor and below $10^{-3}$; right of the argmin for the aliasing wall, left of it for the conditioning wall); median, min and max over those points.

## Results

**Bounded analytic targets (group A): the curve is predicted to four digits on the wall.** For $1/(1+25x^2)$, $1/(1+100x^2)$, the off-center rational, $\tanh10x$ and the Gaussian bump, the wall ratio is $1.000$ (min to max over wall points within $\pm0.0005$, a few cells to $1.03$ at the fp64 edge) on tanh, gelu and swish at every width. The prediction also gives the flat resolution floors where the target's spectrum reaches Nyquist: $10^{-8}$ for Runge at $N=64$ and for the sharper Runge at $N=128$, $10^{-4}$ for the sharper Runge at $N=64$, $10^{-7}$ for $\tanh10x$ at $N=64$, with the kink into the wall at the right $\lambda$. At $N=64$ $\tanh10x$ dips below its predicted floor at one $\lambda$ (to $10^{-9}$ at $\lambda\approx0.3$ under tanh); the interval solve can do better than the whole-line projection of the analytic continuation there.

**Growing analytic targets: predicted, with a small extension bias at small $N$.** $e^x$ and $x^4-x^2$ come in at $0.86$ to $0.99$ (prediction slightly above measurement), rising to $1.00$ at $N=512$, and the two reaches separate on the floor but not on the wall. $e^{\sin3\pi x}$ is $0.99$ to $1.00$ everywhere.

**$|x|^3$: the algebraic floor is predicted at every width.** The measured curve is flat at the resolution floor ($10^{-6}$ at $N=64$ down to $10^{-9}$ at $N=512$) and turns up at the wall; the prediction reproduces both, ratio $0.93$ to $0.99$ on the wall (only tanh has enough wall points; gelu and swish sit on the floor).

**The chirp: extension-limited at small $N$.** At $N=64$ the ratio is $0.04$ to $0.2$ and at $N=128$ about $0.85$; at $N=256$ and $512$ it is $0.96$ to $1.00$. Below the wall the two reaches disagree by orders of magnitude, which is the signature of an extension-dominated prediction.

**Left wall.** With the truncation rule inside the projection, the red curve climbs on the left at the measured slope and sits on the measured curve for the rational, step and bump targets: over all left-wall points of the bounded targets the median measured/predicted is $1.28$ (gelu), $2.31$ (tanh), $2.44$ (swish), with a drift upward in $N$ (Runge on tanh: $1.4$ at $N=64$, $6.1$ at $N=256$). The backward-stability line with $c=1$ has the same slope and is low by a median factor $3$ (tanh), $5$ (gelu), $6$ (swish), the constant decreasing with $N$. For $e^{-20x^2}$ the measured left wall is steeper than either line at large $N$ (both under-predict by $10$-$10^3$ on swish); for $|x|^3$ both lines are within a factor $1$-$3$. For the growing targets and the chirp the coefficient norm is dominated by the extension and neither line is meaningful. The floor between the walls is not predicted by either.

## Additional details

- **Why the taper has to be far away.** The projection's coefficient filter $1/\|\mathrm W_\theta\|^2$ is smooth in $\theta$ but sharply peaked at $|\theta|=\pi$ where $|w_r|$ is smallest, so its spatial impulse response decays only like $e^{-(\lambda/2)d}$; near-Nyquist content created by a taper at distance $d$ therefore reaches the interior attenuated by that factor. This is a property of the whole-line projection, not of the network, whose halo of 32 cells is enough because the interval solve never sees a taper.
- **What the chirp shows.** Any extension of a target with unbounded local frequency either crosses Nyquist (analytic continuation) or has a junction of finite smoothness; a junction whose $k$-th derivative jumps by $J$ leaks spectral amplitude $\approx Jh^k/\pi^{k+1}$ at Nyquist, which for $k=4$, $N=128$ is $10^{-7}$. Only when the interval's own content already dominates the aliasing ($N\ge256$ here) is the prediction insensitive to this.
- **Precision of the prediction.** The FFT is float64; the residual numerical floor of the prediction is $6\times10^{-16}$, visible as the flat red line under the fp64 floors in group A.
- **What the left wall is, on this evidence.** For a target whose spectrum decays like $e^{-\rho|\theta|/h}$, lowering $\lambda$ moves the truncation cutoff $\theta_c(\lambda)$ (where the fiber singular value hits $\varepsilon\sigma_{\max}$; for tanh roughly $\widehat K(\theta_c/\lambda)\approx\varepsilon L$) to lower frequency, and the lost content $|\hat f(\theta_c)|$ is the error. The backward-stability line has the same slope because $\|\hat x\|$ is dominated by the fibers just above the cutoff, where $1/|w_r|$ is largest. The cutoff depends on $\sigma_{\max}$ relative to the fiber, i.e. on the box or halo size only logarithmically, which is the likely source of the slow drift of the ratio with $N$.

## Conclusions

Pending Sam's review. As data: the fiber projection with an a priori extension reproduces the measured aliasing wall to three or four digits for every bounded analytic target tested, within a few percent for growing analytic targets and $|x|^3$, and fails only for the chirp at small $N$, where the extension is the limiting input. With the solver's truncation rule applied fiber by fiber, the same projection reproduces the slope of the left wall and its level within a factor $1$-$3$ for the rational, step and bump targets; the backward-stability bound with $c=1$ has the same slope and is low by a factor $3$-$6$.

## Open questions

- A principled extension (the Fourier-continuation least-squares extension, or the network's own implicit extension read off from its halo coefficients) for targets like the chirp.
- The left wall as a theorem: the truncation model has the right slope and an order-one constant that drifts with $N$; pin down the cutoff (it depends on $\sigma_{\max}$, hence on the halo and sampling) and the $c$ of the backward-stability bound.
- The $L_\infty$ version of the same prediction, against the paper's sup-norm theorem.
- Sampling density: the measured side uses $\ge2$ samples per cell; the prediction assumes the continuous projection. Where does agreement break as samples per cell drop toward one?
- The QI construction proper (interpolation, Theorem 3's $E(\theta)$) on the same targets.
