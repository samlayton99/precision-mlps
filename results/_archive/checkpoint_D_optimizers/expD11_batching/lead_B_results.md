# lead_B -- an independent attack on the 7-requirement solver, and where it actually breaks

**Status: draft-pending-Sam.** Every number below is measured in this lead; nothing is inherited. Two items contradict claims currently written down in the campaign (`batching_test.md` T1, and expD09 round 6's reading of the double-double result) and are flagged as corrections.

## TL;DR

- **Nothing meets all 7 requirements, and the obstruction is now one measured sentence instead of a pile of separate negatives.** $\kappa(AM)=O(1)$ requires the preconditioner to match $R^{-1}$ to *additive* relative accuracy $1/\kappa\approx10^{-14}$; the $\varepsilon$-rank of $R^{-1}$ at that tolerance is $\approx d$ on every cell. Every $O(dk)$ family is a truncation, and every truncation is fatal.
- **Four new negatives, each killed at Gate 1 or 2:** sparse inverse-Cholesky / Vecchia (band$_k(R^{-1})$ gives $\kappa\ge10^{13}$ at every $k$ on every cell, while full $R^{-1}$ gives $1.15$); HODLR compression of $R^{-1}$ ($1.00\,d^2$ storage on 2-D and both twins); cascaded/butterfly block whitening ($\kappa$ does not move across 5 stages); Tikhonov continuation (the path reaches the floor, tracking it costs $\sqrt\kappa$).
- **Extended precision is not the lever the campaign thinks it is.** Unpreconditioned dd-LSQR is within $2$--$5\times$ of fp64-LSQR at matched iterations on all four cells -- the barrier is Krylov convergence rate, not rounding. dd only pays where a block preconditioner has *already* clustered the spectrum.
- **Where it does pay, it pays a lot:** dd + block-Jacobi at $O(dk)$ takes QI-1D $N{=}128$ from $3.3\times10^{-8}$ (a hard fp64 floor) to $1.1\times10^{-13}$, and twin-cluster from $2.0\times10^{-6}$ to $1.1\times10^{-12}$. Five orders, same state, one arithmetic change.
- **Correction to T1: baked-in whitening is *not* poisoned by staleness.** Scored against the *drifted problem's own* floor, stale block-QR sits at $0.5$--$1.5\times$ the floor across $\eta=0\dots10^{-8}$, while block-Jacobi steering is $180$--$2\times10^{6}\times$ above it. The earlier "8 orders" was the problem's floor moving, not the method degrading.

## Question

Does a solver exist that simultaneously satisfies all 7 requirements -- machine-$\varepsilon$, $O(dk)$ state with $k$ fixed, no $O(d^3)$ setup, one pass per iteration, fresh row batches down to $b=d/64$, front-loaded, drift-tolerant -- and if not, exactly which requirement pair is contradictory and why?

## Experiment design

All cells from `core11.py`: QI-1D $N\in\{128,256\}$ ($d=270,462$; $r=144,274$; $\kappa=5.2,8.9\times10^{14}$), QI-2D `radon_tensor` $N{=}576$ ($d=571$, $r=252$, $\kappa=9.5\times10^{14}$), twin-unstruct $d{=}512$ (Haar singular vectors, $r=307$, $\kappa=1.0\times10^{14}$), twin-cluster $d{=}512$ ($r=512$, $\kappa=1.4\times10^{12}$). Metric is `eval_err`, rel $L_2$ on the held-out grid; reference is `floor_pool`.

**A reframing that everything else rests on.** Measured on both a QI cell and a Haar cell, at every iteration count, $\text{eval rel }L_2 = \|Aw-y\|/\|y\|$ **to 2--3 digits**, and the backward-stable residual level $\varepsilon\|A\|_2\|w^\star\|/\|y\|$ equals `floor_pool` ($2.2$ vs $1.5\times10^{-14}$ on QI-1D $N{=}128$; $2.7$ vs $3.1\times10^{-15}$ on twin-unstruct). So the target is exactly **backward stability**, not resolution of the small singular directions, and the training residual is an *observable* stand-in for the eval metric -- no oracle needed for stopping.

**The measurement trap, handled.** $\kappa(AM)$ where $M\approx R^{-1}$ cannot be measured from an fp64 product: $A$ and $M$ each carry $\kappa\sim10^{14}$ and their product is $\approx I$, so fp64 formation injects $\varepsilon\kappa\approx10^{-2}$. Every product in E3 is accumulated in **double-double** (`dd_matmul`, Dekker two-product with dd accumulation) and only then rounded for the SVD. Validation that the machinery is sound: the full $R^{-1}$ measures $\kappa(AR^{-1})=1.151$ on QI-1D and $1.001$ on twin-cluster.

**Arms.**

- **E1/E6 -- precision.** fp64 LSQR vs dd LSQR (`xlsqr`, $\varepsilon_{dd}\sim10^{-32}$, 2 words/number so still $O(d)$), each with and without a block-Jacobi half-inverse $M=\mathrm{blockdiag}(V_j\Sigma_j^{-1}V_j^\top)$ built from the *SVD of the rows* (never a Gram), $k\in\{64,128\}$, state $dk$. Budgets to 1600 iterations, probed at 7 points, fixed cap for every cell.
- **E2 -- Tikhonov continuation.** $w_l=\arg\min\|Aw-y\|^2+\mu_l^2\|w\|^2$ down a geometric $\mu$ schedule, each stage an LSQR solve on the stacked increment problem $\min_\delta\|[A;\mu I]\delta-[r_{l-1};-\mu w_{l-1}]\|$. State $O(d)$, setup zero. Also an oracle arm: warm-start each stage *exactly* on the SVD-computed path and count the iterations one decade of $\mu$ costs.
- **E3 -- sparse inverse Cholesky.** $A=QR$ (dependent columns dropped by pivoted QR, then natural order **restored** -- bandedness is ordering-dependent). Gate 1 on band$_k(R^{-1})$, blockdiag$_k(R^{-1})$, and band$_k(R)^{-1}$ (incomplete Cholesky), $k=4\dots128$; plus the $\varepsilon$-rank of $\mathrm{triu}(R^{-1},1)$ at tolerance $1/\|A\|_2$ and a HODLR block-rank census at the same tolerance.
- **E4 -- cascaded whitening.** $B_{l+1}=B_l Z_l$, $Z_l=P_l^\top\mathrm{blockdiag}(R_{l,j}^{-1})P_l$, over $L=\log_k d$ stages with a radix-$k$ butterfly blocking (blocks $\{i,i{+}s,\dots,i{+}(k{-}1)s\}$, $s=k^l$, so after $L$ stages every column pair has shared a block) and a contiguous control. $\kappa(B_l)$ and $\max_j\kappa(R_{l,j})$ recorded per stage.
- **E5/E9B -- the window law.** $\kappa_{\rm loc}(k)$ = median $\kappa(A[:,S])$ over 30--60 random $k$-column subsets, before and after one whitening stage; plus $\kappa$ of the blocks the solver *actually* picks (`cluster_blocks`).
- **E9A -- the tolerance law.** $M=R^{-1}+\delta\|R^{-1}\|_2 G$ ($G$ random, unit spectral norm) and $M=R^{-1}(I+\delta G)$, $\delta=0\dots10^{-10}$, $\kappa(AM)$ by the dd product.
- **E8/E9C -- requirements 5, 7, 3.** Drift: factors built from $A$, solve on $A'=A(1+\eta\,\text{noise})$, in both a steering form (block-Jacobi inside the iteration) and a baked-in form ($B'=A'_{\rm kept}R_{\rm stale}^{-1}$, whitening rebuilt from the drifted rows with stale factors) -- and scored against the **drifted problem's own** truncated-SVD floor, which the earlier version of this test omitted. Batching: `core11.type_a` (the repo's tuned streaming solver, $k{=}128$, `rotqr`, $\tau{=}200$, 120 episodes) against `floor_1batch(b)`. Setup cost timed at $d=270\dots1384$ with the exponent fitted, not asserted.

**Code & data.** `experiments/expD11_batching/lead_B/` -- `lb.py` (shared), `xlsqr.py` (dd LSQR), `e1_precision.py`, `e2_continuation.py`, `e3_invchol.py`, `e4_butterfly.py`, `e5_local_global.py`, `e6_best_odk.py`, `e8_reqs2.py`, `figs.py`. ## Results

### The central negative, in two measurements

**The tolerance law.** An additive perturbation of the ideal preconditioner propagates one-for-one into the preconditioned condition number: $\kappa(AM)\approx\delta\,\kappa(A)$, measured over six decades of $\delta$ and agreeing with the prediction within $\sim20\times$ on both a QI and a Haar cell. A *multiplicative* perturbation $R^{-1}(I+\delta G)$ is **free** -- $\kappa(AM)$ is unchanged even at $\delta=10^{-6}$, because $AR^{-1}(I+\delta G)=Q(I+\delta G)$. That asymmetry is the whole story: column scalings, rotations and re-orderings of the preconditioner cost nothing, and *any dropped entry* costs everything. Reaching $\kappa=O(1)$ therefore requires reproducing $R^{-1}$ to relative $10^{-14}$.

**$R^{-1}$ is not compressible at that tolerance.** The $\varepsilon$-rank of its strictly-upper part at tolerance $1/\|A\|_2$ is $139/142$, $267/270$, $278/284$, $347/368$ across the cells -- essentially full. Consequently every $O(dk)$ truncation collapses: band$_k(R^{-1})$, blockdiag$_k(R^{-1})$ and band$_k(R)^{-1}$ all land at $\kappa=10^{13}$--$10^{14}$, i.e. **no reduction at all**, for every $k$ from 4 to 128, on all five cells -- against $\kappa(AR^{-1})=1.15$ for the untruncated factor. On QI-1D $N{=}128$ ($d=142$ after dropping dependent columns) even $k=128$ -- dropping only the far corner -- reads $2.2\times10^{13}$. HODLR is no better: at the required tolerance the off-diagonal block ranks total $0.35$--$0.45\,d^2$ on QI-1D (a constant factor, with ranks growing as $\approx d^{0.86}$) and $1.00\,d^2$ on QI-2D, twin-unstruct and twin-cluster.

**The two measurements agree quantitatively**, which is the strongest internal check available here. On QI-1D $N{=}128$ ($d=142$), the required accuracy is $1/\sigma_1=2.7\times10^{-3}$ and $\|R^{-1}\|_2=8.3\times10^{11}$. The part a band truncation drops has norm $8.2\times10^{10}$ -- i.e. $3\times10^{13}$ times the tolerance -- and that norm is **constant from $k=64$ to $k=140$**, because the mass of $R^{-1}$ that carries the conditioning sits in the last super-diagonal, at the corner. The tolerance law then predicts $\kappa(AM)\approx3\times10^{13}$, and the measured values run $7.3\times10^{13}\to3.7\times10^{12}$ over that range. At $k=141$, with nothing dropped, $\kappa$ collapses to $1.151$. There is no gradual degradation to trade against $k$: the preconditioner is all-or-nothing.

This **fixes the campaign's "banded Cholesky" negative**, which was recorded as "the Gram's off-diagonals do not decay". That is the wrong object -- for Green's-function-like Grams the classical fact is that the Gram is dense while the *inverse* Cholesky factor is localized (Brownian covariance is dense with a tridiagonal inverse; Schäfer-Katzfuss-Owhadi 2021). The right object has now been tested directly and it fails too, which is a strictly stronger statement.

### The whole block-diagonal family has a fixed point, and it is ill-conditioned

Cascaded whitening fails Gate 1 outright: $\kappa(B_l)$ stays at $\sim10^{14}$ across all $L=3$--$5$ stages, for butterfly and contiguous blockings alike, on every cell, and the accuracy is worse than one-shot block-QR at $k{=}128$. The per-stage diagnostic says why, and it is more general than the arm: $\max_j\kappa(R_{l,j})$ falls from $\sim10^{13}$ at stage 0 to $O(1)$--$O(10^2)$ at every later stage. **After a single whitening stage every $k$-column window is already well conditioned while the global $\kappa$ is untouched.** No blocking pattern and no amount of depth can extract what no window can see. This subsumes and explains the campaign's "offset two-stage whitening -- do not re-try".

The quantitative version is the window law. For Haar singular vectors, $\kappa_{\rm loc}(k)\approx\sigma_1/\sigma_k=\kappa^{k/r}$, confirmed within $3$--$6\times$ at $d=512$ and $d=1024$ over $k=16\dots256$. To expose the full conditioning inside a window you need $k\approx r$, i.e. state $O(d\cdot r)$ -- the $dr$ wall in closed form for the unstructured case. QI-1D is the opposite extreme: its columns are locally near-dependent, so $\kappa_{\rm loc}(16)$ is already $1.1\times10^{12}$, nine to eleven orders above the Haar law, and that surplus is exactly what block methods live on. **Honest caveat: $\kappa_{\rm loc}$ is directional, not a gate** -- it does not separate QI-2D (which fails) from QI-1D (which succeeds), so it should not be shipped as a go/no-go on its own.

### Precision: not the barrier, except where it is the only barrier

Unpreconditioned dd-LSQR tracks fp64-LSQR within $2$--$5\times$ at matched iterations on all four cells, and neither terminates near $r$. So the slow $\sim\!\text{it}^{-1.8}$ decay is genuine Krylov convergence on a 14-decade gapless spectrum, not a rounding artifact -- **expD09 round 6's framing of dd as the existence proof that "$O(d)$ suffices" does not survive on its own; it holds only in combination with a preconditioner that has already clustered the spectrum, and only on the cells where one exists.**

At $O(dk)$ state with block-Jacobi $k{=}128$ and a 1600-iteration budget the split is clean:

| cell | $\kappa_{\rm loc}/\kappa$ | fp64 + BJ | dd + BJ | pool floor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| QI-1D $N{=}128$ | $2.2\times10^{-1}$ | $3.3\times10^{-8}$ (flat from it 200) | $\mathbf{1.1\times10^{-13}}$ | $1.5\times10^{-14}$ |
| QI-1D $N{=}256$ | $8.5\times10^{-2}$ | $3.1\times10^{-8}$ | $\mathbf{6.8\times10^{-14}}$ | $1.6\times10^{-14}$ |
| twin-cluster $d{=}512$ | $7.8\times10^{-2}$ * | $2.0\times10^{-6}$ | $\mathbf{1.1\times10^{-12}}$ | $3.6\times10^{-15}$ |
| QI-2D $N{=}576$ | $3.3\times10^{-3}$ | $1.2\times10^{-8}$ | $5.7\times10^{-9}$ | $3.8\times10^{-15}$ |
| twin-unstruct $d{=}512$ | $4.1\times10^{-8}$ | $2.9\times10^{-8}$ | $1.3\times10^{-8}$ | $3.1\times10^{-15}$ |

(* measured on the blocking `cluster_blocks` actually selects, where twin-cluster reads $0.78$; the random-window figure is $7.8\times10^{-8}$ and is the reason $\kappa_{\rm loc}$ must be evaluated on the chosen blocks.)

Where the preconditioner clusters, fp64 hits a **hard floor** at $3\times10^{-8}$ -- flat from iteration 200 to 1600 -- and dd walks straight through it, five orders, with the same $dk$ state and no refinement. Where it does not cluster, dd buys a factor of 2. Twin-cluster converges by iteration 50 and is flat thereafter, i.e. maximally front-loaded.

### Two routes that reach the floor in principle and cannot be afforded

**Restarted refinement does not contract.** 14 rounds of $m$-iteration LSMR restarts on QI-1D $N{=}128$: the per-round reduction is $\approx\!1$ after the first round for every $m\in\{25,50,100,200\}$, and the entire restarted family is *worse* than never restarting at equal total iterations. The mechanism is not rounding -- after round 1 the residual sits in the slow-converging directions, where the sqrt-$\kappa$ wall applies with no easy directions left to pay for it.

**Tikhonov continuation reaches the floor along the exact path and costs $\sqrt\kappa$ to track.** The exact regularization path attains $2.4\times10^{-14}$ on QI-1D (floor $1.5\times10^{-14}$) and $2.7\times10^{-15}$ on twin-unstruct (floor $3.1\times10^{-15}$), so the route is valid. But the iterations to advance one decade of $\mu$, warm-started *exactly on the path*, grow as $\sqrt{\sigma_1/\mu}$: $13\to134\to1849\to$ cap-hit at $\mu/\sigma_1\le10^{-6}$ (caps 4000 and 6000, both flagged as hit in F3). Total to $\mu\sim\varepsilon\sigma_1$ is $\sim\sqrt\kappa=2\times10^{7}$ passes. As an implementable scheme with fixed inner budgets it stalls at $5\times10^{-5}$--$1\times10^{-3}$ on all four cells, and finer $\mu$ steps make it *worse* at equal budget. New negative; this route was not on the burned list.

### Requirements 5, 7, 3

**Requirement 7 -- and a correction to T1.** Scored against the drifted problem's own floor, stale block-QR whitening tracks the moving floor: $0.5\times$, $0.9\times$, $1.3\times$, $1.5\times$ at $\eta=0,10^{-12},10^{-10},10^{-8}$ on QI-1D. Block-Jacobi steering is $2\times10^{6}\times$, $1.2\times10^{5}\times$, $1.4\times10^{3}\times$, $1.8\times10^{2}\times$ above it over the same range -- it is limited by its own $3\times10^{-8}$ ceiling, not by staleness. **So the baked-in factor is not poisoned by drift; it is the better of the two at every $\eta$ tested, and T1's "$10^{-8}$ drift $=$ 8 orders" was measuring the problem's approximation floor moving.** Both do fail at $\eta\ge10^{-6}$ ($3.7\times10^{-2}$ against a floor of $9.5\times10^{-7}$).

**Requirement 5 is missed by orders, by the repo's own tuned solver.** `core11.type_a` at $b=d/64$ reaches $1.03$ on QI-1D against a one-batch floor of $1.10$ (no accumulation benefit at all) and $7.5\times10^{-3}$ on twin-unstruct against $0.71$ (a real $95\times$ gain); at $b=4d$ it reaches $2.5\times10^{-7}$ and $8.1\times10^{-6}$ against pool floors of $1.5\times10^{-14}$ and $3.1\times10^{-15}$, at $\approx\!4.8\times10^{4}$ passes. Seven to nine orders short at every $b$. This is structural, not a tuning failure: a Krylov process needs a fixed operator for its runway (measured in `core11`'s own $\tau$ table), and the only $O(dk)$ objects available to carry information across batches are the block factors, which Findings 3--4 show cannot carry enough.

**Requirement 3.** Fitted setup exponents in $d$ over $d=270\dots1384$: block-Jacobi $d^{2.30}$, block-QR $d^{2.64}$, sketch SVD $d^{2.79}$ (top-3-point fits $2.58/2.91/2.98$). The sketch route is confirmed cubic; the two $O(dk)$ setups are analytically $O(ndk+dk^2)=O(d^2)$ at $n=8d$ and fixed $k$, and the excess over 2 is BLAS-efficiency scaling, not algorithmic. **Caveat: these are wall-clock fits in a regime where BLAS throughput is still changing with $d$; they establish that the sketch is cubic and the others are not, and should not be read more finely than that.**

## Additional details

**What the negative does and does not cover.** $\kappa(AM)=O(1)$ is sufficient but not strictly necessary -- what a Krylov method actually needs is a preconditioned spectrum coverable by $O(m)$ clusters for an $m$-iteration budget, which is a weaker condition and is exactly what block-Jacobi buys on the structured cells ($\kappa(AM)=2.5\times10^8$ yet dd reaches $10^{-13}$ in 400 iterations). The negative should therefore be read as: no $O(dk)$ family gets to $\kappa=O(1)$, and the clustering relaxation only rescues cells whose columns are locally near-dependent. QI-2D is the awkward case that shows the relaxation is not captured by any single scalar we measured: $\kappa(AM)=7.4\times10^7$ at $k{=}128$ -- better than QI-1D's $2.5\times10^8$ -- yet it stalls at $5.7\times10^{-9}$.

**Ideas ruled out by argument rather than measurement.** Augmented/saddle-point MINRES, block-LSQR with block size $k$, and any other reformulation restricted to matvecs with $A$ and $A^\top$ live in the same Krylov space and cannot change the total-matvec count; only a preconditioner, extra precision, or stored deflation vectors change it, and all three are measured above. Adaptive/recycled deflation is excluded by memory: peeled directions must be projected out of every subsequent matvec, so $T$ cycles of $c$ vectors costs $O(dTc)$, and the campaign's own measurement that a static correction needs $c\sim r/2$ fixes $Tc\sim r$.

**Two claims in the campaign's notes that this lead contradicts,** both listed here rather than in Conclusions because they need Sam's review: T1 in `batching_test.md` (steering vs baked-in under staleness), and expD09 round 6's use of double-double as a standalone existence proof.

## Conclusions

Nothing meets all 7 requirements. The binding contradiction is requirement 2 against requirement 1, and it is now localized to a single measured statement: a preconditioner reaches $\kappa=O(1)$ only by matching $R^{-1}$ to additive relative accuracy $1/\kappa$, and $R^{-1}$ has $\varepsilon$-rank $\approx d$ at that tolerance, so every $O(dk)$ family -- band, block-diagonal, hierarchical, cascaded, or multi-level -- is a truncation that reduces $\kappa$ by nothing at all.

The one genuinely new positive is arithmetic, not algebraic: at unchanged $O(dk)$ state, double-double LSQR with a block-Jacobi preconditioner reaches $1.1\times10^{-13}$ / $6.8\times10^{-14}$ / $1.1\times10^{-12}$ on QI-1D $N{=}128,256$ and twin-cluster, five orders below the fp64 floor of the same method, with no refinement -- but it buys a factor of 2 on QI-2D and twin-unstruct, so it moves the *steering* family's ceiling rather than the frontier.

## Open questions

- Does the T1 correction change the Type A / Type B division of labour? If a stale baked-in factor tracks the moving floor, the case for a separate steering tier is much weaker than `batching_test.md` assumes.
- QI-2D has a *better* $\kappa(AM)$ than QI-1D and a worse outcome. Whatever spectral statistic actually predicts success is not $\kappa$ and not $\kappa_{\rm loc}$; identifying it would turn expD10's empirical `kappa_gate` into something with a mechanism.
- Is $\varepsilon$-rank of $R^{-1}$ at tolerance $1/\|A\|_2$ the right single-number summary to carry forward as the pre-solve gate, and does it stay $\approx d$ on Jacobian blocks from real training runs?
