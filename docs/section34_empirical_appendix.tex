\section{Experimental protocol and supporting evidence}\label{app:slope-experiments}

The frozen-feature experiment tests the GD output-error bound under its stated
assumptions. The joint-training experiment asks whether GD and Adam acquire
features that permit accurate output within the measured budget. They share
the target, width, sample grids, precision, and error metric; the frozen-GD
theorem does not apply to evolving features or to Adam.

\paragraph{Targets, width, and sampling.}
The primary target on $[-1,1]$ is
\begin{equation}
 f(x)=\frac{\sin(2\pi x)+\tfrac12\sin(6\pi x)
                 +\tfrac14\sin(14\pi x)}{\sqrt{21/32}}.
\end{equation}
Its components contain $16/21$, $4/21$, and $1/21$ of its squared norm.
A supporting experiment uses $x^2$, normalized by its training-grid RMS.
Neither target is generated by a small-slope network.
All models have $W=512$ hidden units and an output bias. Uniform frozen
dictionaries allocate this \emph{total} width as $N=467$ intervals and
$R=22$ halo centers on each side: $h=2/467$,
$x_j=-1+jh$ for $j=-22,\ldots,489$, and $W=N+2R+1$.
Their common slope is $\gamma=\lambda/h$.
Joint models train all parameters in
$q(x)=d+\sum_{j=1}^{512}c_j\tanh(a_jx+b_j)$.

Training, validation, and sampling-resolution checks use respectively
$2{,}048$, $4{,}096$, and $8{,}192$ equally spaced midpoints of $[-1,1]$.
Validation selects optimizer recipes; the denser grid checks sampling
sensitivity and is not an untouched test set. For a grid $X$, every reported
relative output error is
\begin{equation}
 e_X(q)=\frac{\left\|q(X)-f(X)\right\|_2}{\left\|f(X)\right\|_2}.
\end{equation}
Training is full-batch FP64 minimization of
$\|q(X)-f(X)\|_2^2/(2|X|)$.
The main trajectories show raw relative error, without an exponential moving
average or a tolerance-based definition of success.

\paragraph{Initialization and optimizer selection.}
Frozen readouts and their output biases start at zero, as do Adam's moments.
For joint training, five paired seeds draw input slopes, hidden intercepts,
and independently drawn readout coefficients uniformly from
$[-\sqrt{6/513},\sqrt{6/513}]$; the output bias is zero.
The initial parameter arrays are shared across optimizers, schedules, and
targets. The initial horizon is $H=2\times10^6$ updates.
Adam tests rates $\{0.0002,0.002,0.02,0.05,0.1\}$ and GD tests
$\{0.0002,0.002,0.02,0.05,0.1,0.2,0.5,1\}$.
Every rate uses both a constant schedule and the full-horizon cosine schedule
\begin{equation}
 \eta_n=\frac{\eta_0}{2}\left[1+\cos\left(\frac{\pi n}{H}\right)\right],
 \qquad 0\leq n<H.
\end{equation}
Adam uses $(\beta_1,\beta_2,\epsilon)=(0.9,0.999,10^{-8})$.
A winning boundary rate receives one factor-three outward expansion;
divergent recipes remain in the record.

For each optimizer, one complete recipe is selected by median final
validation error across all five seeds, requiring finite endpoints in every
seed. We neither choose recipes separately by seed nor splice recipes over
time. The reported endpoint is the final checkpoint, not an earlier minimum.
The selected joint-error and slope trajectories always refer to the same runs.

\paragraph{Duration checks.}
The best constant-rate recipe is checked separately from the selected
schedule. A greater-than-$10\%$ error reduction or $99$th-percentile slope
increase over the final fifth triggers a five-million-update follow-up with
the best two rates per optimizer and schedule. Error reduction compares
trailing windows of length $H/100$ ending at $0.8H$ and $H$. This allocates
computation; it is not a convergence criterion. Every longer cosine run
starts again from its paired initialization with decay over its new horizon.

For the primary target, neither optimizer met the constant-run trigger at
two million updates. A discretionary five-million-update duration check was
nevertheless queued because the selected Adam cosine trajectory still reduced
its trailing-window error by $20.60\%$ over the final fifth. The queued
comparison uses Adam constant rates $0.02,0.002$, Adam cosine rates
$0.05,0.02$, and GD rates $0.2,0.5$ under both schedules, restarting from the
paired initialization. These pending runs do not enter the completed
two-million-update evidence. In the quadratic experiment, Adam's constant
rate $0.0002$ \emph{did} meet the duration trigger: its error reduction was
$30.82\%$. Its lower-boundary expansion to $0.0002/3$ was also queued.
No conclusion about these pending checks is inferred from the earlier runs.

\paragraph{Slope statistics and trajectory preservation.}
Every raw joint-training error and RMS slope is saved; parameters are saved
every $10{,}000$ updates. The displayed slope statistics are the RMS and
$99$th percentile of $h|a_j|$. Here $h$ is the fixed reference spacing;
jointly learned centers need not be uniform. A horizontal common-slope
reference therefore does not assert a necessary threshold for heterogeneous
features. Plot reduction preserves the entire horizon, exact endpoints, and
within-bin extrema, so short instability is not removed by downsampling.

\paragraph{Theorem bounds and exact-spectrum checks.}
Frozen GD uses bandwidths $1/32$, $1/16$, $1/8$, and $1/4$, zero readout, and
$\eta=1/(2\mu_1)$, where $\mu_1$ is the largest eigenvalue of
$K=JJ^T$ and $J$ is the feature matrix, including its bias column, divided
by $\sqrt m$. Write $\mu_i,u_i$ for the kernel eigenpairs,
$\rho_i=\mu_i/\mu_1$, and
$p_i=|u_i^Tf(X)|^2/\|f(X)\|_2^2$.
The theorem supplies upper rate bounds $\overline\rho_i(\gamma)$,
clipped to $[0,1]$ where appropriate. Its predicted lower curve is
\begin{equation}
 e_{\mathrm{lower}}(n)
 =\left[\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_{\mathrm{resolved}}} p_i
       \left(1-\frac{\overline\rho_i(\gamma)}{2}\right)^{2n}\right]^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal I_{\mathrm{resolved}}$ retains computed ratios exceeding
$10^{-18}$. The plotted lower curve omits numerically unresolved modes and the
unresolved projection remainder rather than treating them as a certified
capacity floor. This omission only weakens a lower bound.
Target projections use the actual dictionary. The gamma-dependent upper
rate bounds, rather than its actual small eigenvalues, enter this prediction.
Using the computed $\rho_i$, all computed target weights, and the projection
remainder gives the separately identified exact-spectrum GD check.
Predictions are evaluated before reading executed
training trajectories, and the lower curve is compared with saved GD error
at every update. Two center-quadrature resolutions check numerical
sensitivity. These are checked floating-point evaluations of the analytic
theorem, not directed-rounding numerical certificates.

\paragraph{Uniform readout baselines and attainable error.}
Frozen Adam tests bandwidths $1/32$, $1/16$, $3/32$, $1/8$, $1/4$, $1/2$, and $1$ with rates
$\{10^{-5},10^{-4},10^{-3},0.002,0.01,0.1\}$ and both schedules.
Boundary winners prompted a shared expansion by $10^{-5}/3$ and $0.3$;
the expanded rates apply to every uniform dictionary and learned-feature
assay. The preselected main reference is $\lambda=1/4$, using one complete
recipe chosen by final validation error. The queued five-million-update
uniform comparison uses the complete expanded grid.

A direct SVD readout fit independently checks attainable accuracy. The
reference fit retains singular values exceeding $10^{-12}$ times the
largest singular value; cutoffs $10^{-14}$ and $10^{-16}$ provide sensitivity
checks. Output errors are recomputed from the fitted coefficients on all
three grids, and coefficient norms are retained. A truncated-SVD residual
is not a capacity floor, and the cutoff is not selected separately at each
slope to minimize displayed error.

\paragraph{Learned-feature and adaptive-metric diagnostics.}
Readout-restart assays freeze selected joint features at initialization,
$200{,}000$, $600{,}000$, and the final update, then reset the readout and
its optimizer moments. Separate final-feature interventions multiply both
$a_j$ and $b_j$ by four or sixteen, preserving each nonconstant feature's
center. These interventions test slope changes at learned centers and do
not impose the uniform-center assumptions of the theorem. Each assay has a
common two-million-update readout budget; this is additional diagnostic
computation, not a matched end-to-end training budget. Pending assay results
are not treated as evidence.

For a kernel or adaptive metric $A$, with eigenvalues $\nu_i$ and normalized
eigenvectors $z_i$, the energy below relative cutoff $\tau$ is reported as
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal E_A(v;\tau)=
 \frac{\sum_{\nu_i/\nu_1\leq\tau}|z_i^Tv|^2}{\|f(X)\|_2^2},
 \qquad v\in\{f(X),\,f(X)-q(X)\}.
\end{equation}
Thus target and residual energies use the same denominator. Adam's endpoint
second moments define the adaptive metric
\begin{equation}
 A=J\,\operatorname{diag}\!\left[
       (\sqrt{\widehat v}+\epsilon)^{-1}\right]J^T,
\end{equation}
where $\widehat v$ is the bias-corrected second-moment vector of the actual
run. Omitting the scalar learning rate leaves relative eigenvalues defined
even at the end of cosine decay. This is an endpoint diagnostic of adaptive
coordinate scaling, not an Adam convergence law: its momentum and changing
second moments are not captured by a fixed metric.

At actual joint-training endpoints, the readout Jacobian contains
$\tanh(a_jx+b_j)$ and the bias column. The full Jacobian additionally
contains $xc_j\operatorname{sech}^2(a_jx+b_j)$ and
$c_j\operatorname{sech}^2(a_jx+b_j)$, for slopes and hidden intercepts.
Both Jacobians are divided by $\sqrt m$; their adaptive metrics use the saved
moments of the joint Adam run, restricted to the relevant parameter blocks.
Moments from a restarted readout are not substituted for joint-training
moments. Selected recipes, continuation decisions, and figure provenance
remain attached to their run records. Finite-budget error is reported as
such and does not establish permanent optimization failure.

\paragraph{Constant-rate control for the adaptive-metric interpretation.}
At the selected two-million-update cosine Adam endpoints, over $99.998\%$
of residual energy lies below relative eigenvalue $10^{-6}$ in the full
Jacobian metric with actual Adam scaling. The best constant-rate recipe,
$\eta=0.02$, gives median $98.763\%$, with seed range
$47.671$--$99.309\%$. These fractions divide by the endpoint residual energy;
the cumulative curves themselves divide by target energy. Thus weak local
directions also matter without cosine cooldown, although the concentration
is not uniform across constant-run seeds.

The constant runs' median adaptive-trace-weighted contribution of $\epsilon$
to $\sqrt{\widehat v}+\epsilon$ is $7.71\times10^{-6}$ for the full Jacobian
and $1.09\times10^{-6}$ for its readout block, versus approximately $24\%$
and $23\%$ at the cosine endpoints. Counting coordinates with
$\sqrt{\widehat v}\le\epsilon$ exaggerates its effect because many have
negligible Jacobian columns. The constant-rate check therefore supports the
weak-direction interpretation without relying on an epsilon-dominated metric;
it remains a local diagnostic, not an Adam convergence theorem.
