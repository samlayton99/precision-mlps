\subsection{Why joint training acquires useful feature scales slowly}
\label{sec:feature-acquisition}

Allowing the features to evolve leaves a substantial accuracy gap over the
tested training budget. After five million updates on mixed sine at
$W=512$, joint Adam reaches median relative training error
$1.81\times10^{-3}$, compared with $6.62\times10^{-7}$ using the supplied
$\lambda=1/4$ dictionary;
joint GD remains at $0.244$ (Figure~\ref{fig:note-training}b).
Numerically resolved readout refits barely improve the learned Adam
dictionary, and increasing its slopes at fixed centers does not close the
gap (Appendix~\ref{app:feature-evidence}).
These interventions indicate that useful slopes and centers must be
acquired together.

We quantify one mechanism that limits this acquisition.
In the small-parameter regime, tanh's leading non-affine term is cubic,
so its target-driven growth depends on the target's low-degree polynomial
projection. Bounding this coupling, higher-order contributions, and
population concentration controls the growth of slopes, biases, and
readouts. The resulting bound on non-affine output yields an output-error
lower bound.

\begin{theorem}[Output error under slow feature acquisition]
\label{thm:feature-output}
Let $f\ne0$ be the sampled target and $f_\perp$ its component orthogonal
to affine functions. Under the population-concentration, target-coupling,
and coarse-tracking bounds of Appendix~\ref{app:feature-theory},
joint GD satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\|f-q_n\|_{2,Z}}{\|f\|_{2,Z}}
 \ge
 \frac{[\|f_\perp\|_{2,Z}-\mathcal Q_n(B_n)]_+}{\|f\|_{2,Z}}.
 \label{eq:feature-main-error}
\end{equation}
Here $B_n$ is an explicit scalar upper bound on the joint norm of hidden
slopes, biases, and readouts, and $\mathcal Q_n(B_n)$ bounds their
non-affine output. The same comparison gives
$\overline\lambda_n\le hB_n/\sqrt W$, with reference spacing $h$ and
$\overline\lambda_n=W^{-1}\sum_j h|\gamma_{j,n}|$.
The recurrence, coefficients, and precise conditions are given in the appendix.
\end{theorem}

The conditional bounds closely track the separate GD continuation
experiments: across ten continuations spanning six target families, the
output-error lower bound captures at least 97.7\% of executed error at
every stored diagnostic time (Figure~\ref{fig:feature-population-output};
Appendix~\ref{app:feature-evidence}). The structural coefficients are
evaluated along these trajectories. The longer Adam experiments establish
a corresponding empirical acquisition gap; the theorem concerns GD.
