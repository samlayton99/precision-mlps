\subsection{Bandwidth controls optimization access to high precision}
\label{sec:bandwidth-access}

The construction's nearly width-independent relative bandwidth $\lambda=\gamma h$ requires
$\gamma=\Theta(W)$, yet trained slopes fall short (Figure~\ref{fig:note-training}).
Even though larger widths admit smaller $\lambda$ at comparable construction error
(Figure~\ref{fig:construction}b), small slopes can delay readout learning by weakening
target-relevant kernel directions. We quantify this delay by replacing
Section~3.1's direct least-squares solve with GD on fixed features.
This connects prior analysis of spectral bias
\citep{xu2020frequency,tancik2020fourier} to readout optimization
in the quasi-interpolant dictionary.

\begin{theorem}[Slope-dependent output-error lower bound]
\label{thm:note-output}
For the fixed-center construction of Section~3.1 with common slope
$\gamma>0$, let $\mu_1\ge\mu_2\ge\cdots\ge0$ be the normalized
readout-kernel eigenvalues. For a nonzero sampled target, readout GD from
zero with $\eta=1/(2\mu_1)$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\|f-q_n\|_{2,Z}^{2}}{\|f\|_{2,Z}^{2}}
 \ge\sum_i p_i(\gamma)
 \left(1-\frac{\overline\rho_i(\gamma)}2\right)^{2n},\qquad n\ge0.
 \label{eq:note-output-bound}
\end{equation}
Here $\|\cdot\|_{2,Z}$ is the training-sample RMS norm,
$p_i$ is the fraction of sampled target energy projected onto kernel
eigenvector $i$, and $\mu_i/\mu_1\le\overline\rho_i(\gamma)\le1$.
The gamma-dependent upper bounds $\overline\rho_i$ come from the spectral
Theorem~\ref{thm:note-rates} in Appendix~\ref{app:note-spectrum}.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Proof sketch.}
The readout kernel contains normalized inner products of feature vectors
at pairs of training points. To bound its spectrum, we separate the constant
sample direction and approximate the sum over centers by an integral.
This approximation depends explicitly on $d\coth(\gamma d)$,
where $d$ separates those points. Bounding the finite-center corrections
gives $\overline\rho_i(\gamma)$; standard GD eigenmode decay then yields
the output-error bound.

Figure~\ref{fig:note-spectrum} (left) shows that small bandwidths leave
target-relevant eigenvalues tiny relative to $\mu_1$, as captured by the
appendix's spectral bounds. Since $\mu_1$ limits the stable GD step size,
these target components decay slowly even when the dictionary admits an
accurate fit. Theorem~\ref{thm:note-output} converts those rate bounds into
output-error lower bounds that closely track executed GD
(Figure~\ref{fig:note-spectrum}, right): bandwidth controls how quickly
training accesses the dictionary's accuracy.

The acquisition shortfall also appears empirically under Adam:
joint training leaves a bandwidth-acquisition gap and output error above
its frozen-feature counterpart
(Figure~\ref{fig:note-training}). This motivates the next question:
why does joint training fail to acquire useful slopes and centers together?
