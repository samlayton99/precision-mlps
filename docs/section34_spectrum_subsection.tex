\subsection{Bandwidth controls optimization access to high precision}
\label{sec:bandwidth-access}

The construction's nearly width-independent relative bandwidth
$\lambda=\gamma h$ requires $\gamma=\Theta(W)$.
Even though larger widths admit smaller $\lambda$ at comparable construction
error (Figure~2b), small slopes can delay readout optimization.
We quantify this spectral-bias mechanism~\citep{xu2020frequency,tancik2020fourier}
in the dictionary of Section~3.1, replacing the direct normalized
least-squares solve with GD while keeping the features fixed.

\begin{theorem}[Slope-dependent output-error lower bound]
\label{thm:note-output}
For the fixed-center construction of Section~3.1 with common slope
$\gamma>0$, let $\mu_1\ge\mu_2\ge\cdots\ge0$ be the normalized
readout-kernel eigenvalues. For a nonzero sampled target, readout GD from
zero with $\eta=1/(2\mu_1)$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\|f-q_n\|_{2,Z}^{2}}{\|f\|_{2,Z}^{2}}
 \ge\sum_i p_i(\gamma)
 \left(1-\frac{\overline\rho_i(\gamma)}2\right)^{2n},\qquad n\ge0.
 \label{eq:note-output-bound}
\end{equation}
Here $\|\cdot\|_{2,Z}$ is the training-sample RMS norm,
$p_i$ is the fraction of sampled target energy projected onto kernel
eigenvector $i$, and $\mu_i/\mu_1\le\overline\rho_i(\gamma)\le1$.
The explicit gamma-dependent rate bounds and finite-geometry allowances
are given in Appendix~\ref{app:note-spectrum}.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Proof sketch.}
The readout kernel contains normalized inner products of feature vectors
at pairs of training points. After removing the constant sample direction,
its center-integral approximation depends explicitly on $d\coth(\gamma d)$,
where $d$ separates those points. Bounding the finite-center corrections
gives $\overline\rho_i(\gamma)$; standard GD eigenmode decay then yields
the output-error bound.

Figure~\ref{fig:note-spectrum}(a) selects ranks $4,14,30$ because together
they carry 59--63\% of sampled target energy across the four bandwidths,
including at least 8\% in rank~14 and 2.5\% in rank~30.
Increasing $\lambda$ from $1/32$ to $1/4$ raises their relative rates by factors of
$1.3$, $47$, and $1.4\times10^5$, respectively.

At $\lambda=1/32$, a direct readout fit attains $6.2\times10^{-10}$ dense-grid
relative error, yet five million GD updates retain training error $0.210$
against the theorem's lower bound $0.2098$ (Figure~\ref{fig:note-spectrum}(b)).
At $\lambda=1/4$, GD reaches $3.88\times10^{-4}$ over the same budget.
Across the four bandwidths, the bound remains at least 75\% of executed error
throughout training: small slopes delay accuracy already available in the
dictionary.
