\subsection{Why joint training acquires scale slowly}
\label{sec:scale-acquisition}

Can joint training learn features that make high precision accessible?
Figure~\ref{fig:scale-observation} compares learning all parameters with
learning readouts on supplied features. After five million updates, joint
Adam reaches relative error $1.81\times10^{-3}$, versus $6.62\times10^{-7}$
with supplied features. Its RMS relative slope reaches only $0.0582$,
compared with the supplied scale $1/4$; GD acquires still less scale.
This is a finite-budget precision gap: Adam already passes 1\% accuracy.
The supplied geometry is a benchmark, not a necessary condition for every
accurate network. We therefore seek to explain both limited population
movement and limited output improvement.

\paragraph{Identify the force that remains after coarse fitting.}
For $q_\theta(x)=b+\sum_{j=1}^W w_j\tanh(\gamma_jx+\beta_j)$ and
$L=\frac12\|q_\theta-f\|^2$, gradient flow is $\dot\theta=-\nabla L$.
Splitting output into its affine part and orthogonal remainder gives the
exact decomposition $\nabla L=R+F$. The \emph{tracking gradient} $R$
measures departure from coarse equilibrium. The \emph{effective fine
gradient} $F$ combines the non-affine residual gradient with the
compensation needed to preserve coarse output. Compensation remains when
tracking becomes small. In Figure~\ref{fig:scale-mechanism}, tracking is
below 0.2\% of effective force on the sampled GD continuation. This
motivates studying effective flow $\dot\theta=-F$, with tracking and GD
discretization retained as explicit disturbances in the theorem.

\paragraph{Explain persistence while the features evolve.}
Write $e_H$ for the non-affine residual, $J_H=D_\theta e_H$ for its
Jacobian, and $v=F/\|F\|$. Effective flow obeys the exact identity
\begin{equation}
\frac{d}{dt}\log\|F\|
=-\underbrace{\|J_Hv\|^2}_{\text{residual relaxation}}
+\underbrace{\kappa_H+\kappa_C}_{\text{geometry and compensation feedback}}.
\label{eq:scale-force-identity}
\end{equation}
Residual relaxation fits away the error currently driving the force.
Feedback changes sensitivity to the remaining error as geometry and
readouts move; it can strengthen the force. The smooth-step example has
positive feedback and little relaxation: force grows 2.19-fold, yet error
remains near 49\%. Thus persistence need not mean contraction or equilibrium.
The question is whether initially weak force can reinforce itself enough
within the budget.

\paragraph{A conditional population bound.}
We upper-bound feedback by the second output response in direction $v$,
weighted by residual and compensation norms. Let $B_t$ bound its
accumulation. This is a condition on evolving output derivatives; it does
not assume that net force growth or slope movement is small.

\begin{theorem}[Slow scale acquisition, informal]
\label{thm:scale-informal}
Restart full-batch GD after tracking becomes small, and let $t=n\eta$ be
elapsed training time. Let $F_0$ be the restart effective fine gradient
and $E_0$ the non-affine residual norm. If accumulated feedback is bounded
by $B_t$, then, under the regularity and disturbance conditions in
Appendix~\ref{app:scale-theory},
\begin{align}
\|q_{\theta_n}-f\|^2
&\ge E_0^2-2t e^{2B_t}\|F_0\|^2-\Delta_{\mathrm{err}}(t),
\label{eq:scale-main-error}\\
\lambda_{\mathrm{RMS}}(t)
&\le\lambda_{\mathrm{RMS}}(0)
+\frac{h}{\sqrt W}t e^{B_t}\|F_0\|+\Delta_{\mathrm{scale}}(t).
\label{eq:scale-main-rms}
\end{align}
The nonnegative allowances account for tracking and finite GD steps;
both vanish for exact effective flow.
\end{theorem}

Bounded feedback limits force amplification. Integrating its square bounds
output improvement; integrating its magnitude bounds collective travel
and hence RMS scale growth. Individual neurons may escape: the appendix
also bounds the fraction ever acquiring a specified increment. Output norms
use the training measure; parameter norms are Euclidean.

\paragraph{Check the condition, then its consequences.}
Across 23 targets and two seeds at width 705, twice the restart feedback
rate covers accumulated feedback at every saved prefix over 20k further
updates. Six targets are checked densely over 100k further updates using
a factor-four allowance. They use at most 55\% of that allowance; the
effective-flow RMS growth bounds are below $8\times10^{-4}$ and relative-error
floors exceed 39\%. GD closely follows these effective dynamics
(Appendix~\ref{app:scale-experiments}). These are numerical checks of the
conditional mechanism, not interval certificates. They cover the measured
post-transient GD intervals; they do not extend the same allowance to all
five million updates or to Adam's adaptive dynamics.
