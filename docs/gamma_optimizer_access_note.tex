{\fontsize{20}{23}\selectfont\bfseries How gamma controls readout learning\par}
\vspace{5pt}
\textbf{An attainable target can still be expensive to learn.} With identical
centers, target, and zero initialization, frozen tanh readout reaches 1\%
relative error after \textbf{15.8 million GD updates at gamma 8}, versus
\textbf{16,013 at gamma 64}. Adam also misses 1\% at gamma 8 within
200,000 updates. The mechanism is explicit: gamma smooths features,
smoothing changes correlations with needed error patterns, and the
target-weighted spectrum determines GD speed.

\textbf{Notation.} Eigenvalues use $\mu$; norms are Euclidean.

\section{Why feature correlations control learning}
Train only readout $w$ in $J_\gamma w$ on $m$ input samples $x_a$,
starting at zero. The $W$ tanh
features share slope $\gamma$ and consecutive equally spaced centers $c_j$,
spacing $h$:
\[
J_\gamma[a,j]=\frac{\tanh(\gamma(x_a-c_j))}{\sqrt m},\qquad
J_\gamma[a,0]=\frac1{\sqrt m},\qquad y_a=\frac{f(x_a)}{\sqrt m}.
\]
Minimizing $\frac12\|J_\gamma w-y\|^2$ is half mean-squared error.
GD gives $r_{n+1}=(I-\eta K_\gamma)r_n$. For a unit error pattern $v$,
\[
q_\gamma(v)=v^TK_\gamma v=\|J_\gamma^Tv\|^2
=\sum_i\mu_i(\gamma)|u_i(\gamma)^Tv|^2.
\]
This is total squared correlation with the features: an eigenvector's
learning eigenvalue, or a weighted average for other patterns. If the
residual is $v$, the correction along $v$ is $\eta q_\gamma(v)$.
The largest eigenvalue limits the
shared stable step, $0<\eta<2/\mu_1$, making relative eigenvalues $\rho_i$
central to learning speed.
\section{The explicit gamma mechanism}
A tanh is a smoothed step:
$\tanh(\gamma\,\cdot)=s_\gamma*\sign$ with
$s_\gamma(x)=\frac\gamma2\sech^2(\gamma x)$. Its Fourier multiplier is
\[
M_\gamma(\omega)=\frac z{\sinh z},\qquad
z=\frac{\pi|\omega|}{2\gamma},\qquad M_\gamma(0)=1.
\]
Small gamma averages more widely and suppresses rapid variation. Replace
the center sum by an integral: subtracting the whole-line deficit of two
tanh features from their saturated-product baseline gives $B_\gamma$.
The correction $R_\gamma$ restores finite endpoints and discrete centers:
\[
B_\gamma[a,b]=\frac{W+1}{m}-\frac2{hm}d_{ab}\coth(\gamma d_{ab}),
\quad d_{ab}=x_a-x_b,\quad R_\gamma=K_\gamma-B_\gamma.
\]
At $d=0$, use $d\coth(\gamma d)=1/\gamma$.
\begin{theorem}[Gamma-dependent kernel response]
For these centers, fixed samples, $\Gamma>\gamma>0$, and any real $v$,
set $V_v(\omega)=\sum_a v_a e^{-i\omega x_a}$. Then
\[
\boxed{q_\Gamma(v)-q_\gamma(v)
=\mathcal A_{\Gamma,\gamma}(v)+v^T(R_\Gamma-R_\gamma)v,}
\]
\[
\mathcal A_{\Gamma,\gamma}(v)=\frac2{\pi hm}\int_{\mathbb R}
\frac{M_\Gamma(\omega)^2-M_\gamma(\omega)^2}{\omega^2}
|V_v(\omega)|^2\,d\omega\ \ge0.
\]
\end{theorem}
\textbf{In words:} response changes by explicit gain from reduced smoothing
plus a finite-geometry correction. The pattern enters through $|V_v|^2$;
gamma enters through $M_\Gamma^2-M_\gamma^2$. Pairing two features gives
the squared multiplier. The identity is exact, including the continuous
zero-frequency limit. The signed correction need not be small in every
direction. Appendix A gives the proof.

\textbf{Direct validation.} Use $W=559$, $h=1/256$, $m=8{,}193$ uniform
samples on $[-1,1]$, and
$f(x)=\sin(2\pi x)+\frac12\sin(6\pi x)+\frac14\sin(10\pi x)$.
At gamma 8, the most target-aligned resolved mode with
$0<\rho_i\le2\times10^{-6}$ is mode 22, carrying \textbf{4.14\% of
target energy}. This retrospective choice uses the initial kernel and
target, without training outcomes. Freeze $v$ and predict larger-gamma
response by $q_8(v)+\mathcal A_{\Gamma,8}(v)$. Figure 1 shows roughly
\textbf{1,000-fold growth}, with discrepancy below \textbf{0.061\%} at
gammas 12, 16, and 64. These corrections are measured for this probe.
The gain uses equivalent closed-form entries
$2[d\coth(8d)-d\coth(\Gamma d)]/(hm)$, with no new eigenspace calculation.

\section{From the mechanism to measured learning}
A larger average response can coexist with slow components. To determine
acquisition time, retain each eigenvalue and target weight. From zero,
classical GD gives
\[
e(n)^2:=\frac{\|r_n\|^2}{\|y\|^2}
=\sum_i p_i(1-\eta\mu_i)^{2n}
=\sum_i p_i(1-\rho_i/2)^{2n}\quad\text{if }\eta=1/(2\mu_1).
\]
Nullspace factors are one. A ratio $10^{-6}$ needs about two million
updates for one e-fold reduction. Measured finite spectra, target weights,
and actual archived steps predict \textbf{15,798,313; 186,057; 61,792;
and 16,013 updates} to 1\% at gammas 8, 12, 16, and 64, exactly matching
executed crossings. All four attain the studied accuracy; no decay rate
is fitted to training.

\textbf{Adam tests whether the same difficult directions retain error.}
Adaptive updates do not follow GD's eigenmode law. In Figure 2B, the
gamma-8 band $0<\rho_i\le2\times10^{-6}$ starts with \textbf{4.33\%}
target energy and retains \textbf{$1.47094\times10^{-4}$} after 200,000
Adam updates, above the $10^{-4}$ squared-error budget. At larger gamma,
band energies are below budget. Normalization uses $\|y\|^2$; ratio-defined
subspaces can change with gamma.

Figure 2C shows first crossings of the EMA of squared relative error with one shared
\textbf{100-update half-life}: no crossing at gamma 8, then \textbf{7,688;
1,900; and 1,486} updates. Recrossings occur: this is first acquisition,
not sustained accuracy. Initial-loss memory prevents crossing before
\textbf{1,329 updates}, compressing the fastest-run differences.
Appendix B gives loss curves, protocol, and sensitivity checks.

\section{Full proof}
Let $I=[c_1-h/2,c_W+h/2]$ and
$F_{ab,\gamma}(c)=\tanh(\gamma(x_a-c))\tanh(\gamma(x_b-c))$.
The identity $1-\tanh u\tanh v=\coth(u-v)(\tanh u-\tanh v)$ and
the integral of translated tanh profiles give
\[
\int_{\mathbb R}(1-F_{ab,\gamma}(c))\,dc
=2d_{ab}\coth(\gamma d_{ab}).
\]
The coincident limit is $2/\gamma$. Since $|I|=Wh$, subtract this deficit
from the constant baseline and restore the exterior part to get
$K_\gamma=B_\gamma+R_\gamma$, where
\begin{align*}
R_\gamma[a,b]={}&\frac1m\left[\sum_{j=1}^{W}F_{ab,\gamma}(c_j)
-\frac1h\int_I F_{ab,\gamma}(c)\,dc\right]\\
&+\frac1{hm}\int_{\mathbb R\setminus I}(1-F_{ab,\gamma}(c))\,dc.
\end{align*}
These are the quadrature discrepancy and finite-interval correction.
Bias contributes $1/m$ to $B_\gamma$ and cancels across slopes.

Both $s_\gamma*\sign$ and $\tanh(\gamma\,\cdot)$ vanish at zero and
have derivative $2s_\gamma$, proving the smoothing identity. With
$\widehat g(\omega)=\int g(x)e^{-i\omega x}\,dx$, substitute
$u=e^{2\gamma x}$ and $a=\omega/(2\gamma)$:
\begin{align*}
\widehat s_\gamma(\omega)
&=\int_0^\infty\frac{u^{-ia}}{(1+u)^2}\,du
=\Gamma(1-ia)\Gamma(1+ia)\\
&=\frac{\pi a}{\sinh(\pi a)}=M_\gamma(\omega).
\end{align*}
The beta integral and gamma-function reflection identity give the middle
equalities; $\Gamma(\cdot)$ here denotes the gamma function.

Put $t_\gamma(u)=\tanh(\gamma u)$. Differentiate
$\int[1-t_\gamma(u)t_\gamma(u-d)]\,du$ twice and integrate by parts:
\begin{align*}
\frac{d^2}{dd^2}[2d\coth(\gamma d)]
&=-\int t_\gamma(u)t_\gamma''(u-d)\,du\\
&=\int t_\gamma'(u)t_\gamma'(u-d)\,du
=4(s_\gamma*s_\gamma)(d).
\end{align*}
The boundary term vanishes and $s_\gamma$ is even. Thus the integrable
difference $D(d)=2[d\coth(\gamma d)-d\coth(\Gamma d)]$ has transform
\[
\widehat D(\omega)=\frac{4(M_\Gamma(\omega)^2-M_\gamma(\omega)^2)}{\omega^2},
\qquad\widehat D(0)=\frac{\pi^2}{3}(\gamma^{-2}-\Gamma^{-2}).
\]
Use $\widehat{D''}=-\omega^2\widehat D$ and expand $z/\sinh z$ at zero.
This multiplier decreases for $z>0$, so $M_\Gamma\ge M_\gamma$ and
$\widehat D\ge0$. Finally,
$B_\Gamma[a,b]-B_\gamma[a,b]=D(x_a-x_b)/(hm)$.
Fourier inversion contributes $1/(2\pi)$; summing against $v_av_b$
gives $|V_v|^2$ and prefactor $2/(\pi hm)$. Add the exact finite
correction to complete the proof.\hfill$\square$

\section{Adam protocol and supporting checks}
\setcounter{figure}{0}\renewcommand{\thefigure}{B\arabic{figure}}
\textbf{Metric and protocol.} Common Adam uses initial rate $10^{-3}$,
epsilon $10^{-12}$, and moments $(0.9,0.999)$. Rate stays fixed through
20,000, decays by cosine to $10^{-6}$ at 50,000, then stays fixed through
200,000. Define
\[
M_0=1,\qquad M_n=\beta M_{n-1}+(1-\beta)e(n)^2,\qquad\beta=2^{-1/100}.
\]
First EMA acquisition is the first $M_n\le10^{-4}$, calculated from every
update; plots show $\sqrt{M_n}$. Later upcrossings occur 73, 97, and 98
times at gammas 12, 16, and 64; raw sustained crossings occur around
41,000. Since $M_n\ge\beta^n$, memory imposes the 1,329-update floor.
The shared window is a retrospective visualization choice.

\textbf{Robustness.} Targets are the sine mixture, $\exp(\sin(3\pi x))$,
$1/(1+25x^2)$, $\sqrt5x^2$, and $\sqrt2\sin(2\pi x)$.
Besides the common setting, archived settings were selected from five
rates and two epsilons by median validation error at five checkpoints
from 40,000 to 50,000; continuation preserved optimizer state. Selection
rewards late accuracy, not early acquisition. Figure B2 shows target
and optimizer dependence.
