How gamma changes access to the target\\under GD and Adam\par}
\vspace{8pt}
Small tanh slopes can leave needed target components difficult to acquire
under both gradient descent and Adam. On the same finite dictionary, the
explicit spectral comparison below certifies rank-32 eigenvalue-ratio
increases of approximately 35, 428, and 36,790 times relative to the
actual gamma-8 ratio when gamma rises to 12, 16, and 64.
At gamma 8, the chosen slow subspace initially
holds 4.33\% of the target energy. After 200,000 common-recipe Adam updates,
its residual energy alone still exceeds the 1\% error tolerance. At larger
gammas it is below tolerance.

The distinction between rates and optimizer behavior remains essential.
The GD spectrum predicts its training curve exactly, up to numerical
evaluation. Adam changes the update geometry, so we measure residual
projections and first crossings of smoothed loss. With one shared
100-update EMA half-life, crossings occur at 7,688, 1,900, and 1,486
updates for gammas 12, 16, and 64. Gamma 8 does not cross within 200,000.
These are measured Adam times, not predictions from a GD theorem; later
recrossings occur. The corrected delay argument below supplies the upper-rate
and target-overlap bounds that a necessary GD delay actually requires.

\section{Why the largest eigenvalue matters}
For inputs $x_a$, centers $c_j$, and $m$ samples, use ordinary tanh features
and a bias:
\[
J_\gamma[a,j]=\frac{\tanh(\gamma(x_a-c_j))}{\sqrt m},\qquad
J_\gamma[a,0]=\frac1{\sqrt m}.
\]
With normalized targets $y_a=f(x_a)/\sqrt m$, minimizing
$\frac12\|J_\gamma w-y\|^2$ is ordinary half mean-squared error. Geometry
is frozen; only readout $w$ is trained. From $w_0=0$, GD gives
$r_{n+1}=(I-\eta K_\gamma)r_n$. The largest eigenvalue limits the stable
step shared by all directions. For $\eta=1/(2\mu_1)$,
\[
\frac{\|r_n\|^2}{\|y\|^2}
=\sum_i p_i(\gamma)\left(1-\frac{\rho_i(\gamma)}2\right)^{2n}.
\]
A ratio of one halves that component's residual every update. A ratio of
$10^{-6}$ requires about two million updates for one e-fold reduction.
A small ratio matters only if the target needs that direction. Both
eigenvectors and target weights can change with gamma; ordered rank is
not a fixed physical direction across dictionaries. For a prescribed common
step, the rate is $\eta\mu_i$, and ratios alone do not determine update
counts. The calculations below use the actual archived steps.

\section{A finite-dictionary theorem with gamma explicit}
This is the cross-gamma comparison from the collaborator's
\href{gamma_slow_learning_alt_view.pdf}{alternative-view note}, written
with $\mu$ for eigenvalues. Centers are consecutive and uniformly spaced;
inputs are fixed. Uniform input sampling makes the explicit increment
Toeplitz and easy to apply, but the quadratic-form identity itself does
not require uniform samples.

Let there be $W$ centers, spacing $h$, and center-cell interval
$I=[c_1-h/2,c_W+h/2]$. Define $d_{ab}=x_a-x_b$ and
\[
K^{(0)}_\gamma[a,b]=\frac{W+1}{m}
-\frac2{hm}d_{ab}\coth(\gamma d_{ab}),\qquad
E_\gamma=K_\gamma-K^{(0)}_\gamma,
\]
using the continuous diagonal value $d\coth(\gamma d)=1/\gamma$ at
$d=0$. The correction $E_\gamma$ includes the finite center interval
and replacement of a center integral by discrete centers. It is retained.
For $\gamma_b>\gamma_a$, set
\[
G_{ba}[a,b]=\frac2{hm}\left[
d_{ab}\coth(\gamma_a d_{ab})-d_{ab}\coth(\gamma_b d_{ab})\right].
\]
\begin{theorem}[Explicit gamma gain and finite spectral comparison]
The matrix $G_{ba}$ is positive semidefinite. For every real sample vector
$\nu$,
\begin{align*}
\nu^TG_{ba}\nu
&=\frac2{\pi hm}\int_{\mathbb R}
\frac{M_{\gamma_b}(\omega)^2-M_{\gamma_a}(\omega)^2}{\omega^2}
\left|\sum_a\nu_a e^{-i\omega x_a}\right|^2\,d\omega,\\
M_\gamma(\omega)&=\frac z{\sinh z},\qquad
z=\frac{\pi|\omega|}{2\gamma}.
\end{align*}
Let $U_i$ contain orthonormal eigenvectors for the largest $i$ positive
eigenvalues of $K_{\gamma_a}$. Set
\begin{align*}
C_i&=\diag(\mu_1(\gamma_a),\ldots,\mu_i(\gamma_a))+U_i^TG_{ba}U_i,\\
B_i&=U_i^T(E_{\gamma_b}-E_{\gamma_a})U_i,
\qquad \delta_i=\|C_i^{-1/2}B_iC_i^{-1/2}\|_2.
\end{align*}
For any $L_b\ge\mu_1(\gamma_b)$,
\[
\boxed{\frac{\mu_i(\gamma_b)}{\mu_1(\gamma_b)}
\ge\frac{[1-\delta_i]_+\,\mu_{\min}(C_i)}{L_b}.}
\]
Here $\mu_{\min}(C_i)$ is the smallest eigenvalue of the displayed small
matrix. The computed bound uses the maximum absolute row sum of the
actual finite kernel for $L_b$.
\end{theorem}
The multiplier increases with gamma: larger gamma removes less
high-frequency content. The theorem carries that gain into an entire
trial subspace, then accounts for the finite correction and normalization
by the largest eigenvalue. It does not assert that every finite-dictionary
ratio increases. An improvement is guaranteed only when the bound exceeds
the old ratio. The calculation uses an old eigenspace and small spectral
problems; it is not a scalar law depending on gamma alone.

\subsection*{Proof}
Write $g_{ab}(c)=\tanh(\gamma(x_a-c))\tanh(\gamma(x_b-c))$.
Direct integration gives
\[
\int_{\mathbb R}(1-g_{ab}(c))\,dc=2d_{ab}\coth(\gamma d_{ab}).
\]
For distinct inputs, the identity
$1-\tanh u\tanh v=\coth(u-v)(\tanh u-\tanh v)$ reduces the integral
to two translated tanh profiles. Their difference integrates to twice
the translation. The coincident limit follows from
$\int\sech^2(\gamma c)\,dc=2/\gamma$.

The center integral over $I$ equals $Wh$ minus the whole-line deficit
plus the deficit outside $I$. Its normalized kernel is therefore
$K^{(0)}_\gamma$ plus the omitted-exterior correction. Adding the
discrete-center quadrature difference gives exactly
$K_\gamma=K^{(0)}_\gamma+E_\gamma$.

The density $s_\gamma(x)=\frac\gamma2\sech^2(\gamma x)$ satisfies
$\tanh(\gamma\,\cdot)=s_\gamma*\sign$: both derivatives equal
$2s_\gamma$ and both vanish at zero. Under convention
$\widehat f(\omega)=\int f(x)e^{-i\omega x}\,dx$, substituting
$u=e^{2\gamma x}$ and $a=\omega/(2\gamma)$ gives
\begin{align*}
\widehat s_\gamma(\omega)
&=\int_0^\infty\frac{u^{-ia}}{(1+u)^2}\,du
=\Gamma(1-ia)\Gamma(1+ia)\\
&=\frac{\pi a}{\sinh(\pi a)}=M_\gamma(\omega).
\end{align*}
This uses the beta integral and gamma reflection identity, with
continuous value one at zero. The difference
\[
D(d)=2[d\coth(\gamma_a d)-d\coth(\gamma_b d)]
\]
is integrable. The second derivative of $2d\coth(\gamma d)$ is
$4(s_\gamma*s_\gamma)(d)$, by differentiating the deficit integral and
integrating once by parts. Hence
$\widehat{D''}=4(M_{\gamma_a}^2-M_{\gamma_b}^2)$ and
\[
\widehat D(\omega)
=\frac{4(M_{\gamma_b}(\omega)^2-M_{\gamma_a}(\omega)^2)}{\omega^2}.
\]
The continuous zero-frequency limit is
$\widehat D(0)=\frac{\pi^2}{3}(\gamma_a^{-2}-\gamma_b^{-2})$.
Fourier inversion contributes $1/(2\pi)$, giving precisely
$2/(\pi hm)$ in the quadratic-form identity.

The larger-gamma multiplier is at least as large, so the transform is
nonnegative. Fourier inversion proves the identity and positivity.
In particular $C_i$ is positive definite. Compressing the actual new
kernel gives $U_i^TK_{\gamma_b}U_i=C_i+B_i$. By definition of
$\delta_i$, this is at least $(1-\delta_i)C_i$ in quadratic-form order.
Min--max yields
$\mu_i(\gamma_b)\ge(1-\delta_i)\mu_{\min}(C_i)$ for $\delta_i<1$.
When $\delta_i\ge1$, positivity of the actual kernel gives zero as a
lower bound. Divide by $\mu_1(\gamma_b)\le L_b$.\hfill$\square$

\section{From needed directions to a GD delay}
Define positive slow energy
\[
P_\gamma(\rho)=\sum_{0<\rho_i(\gamma)\le\rho}p_i(\gamma).
\]
If $P_\gamma(\rho)\ge p>\varepsilon^2$, retaining these terms in the
exact GD residual gives
\[
E_\gamma(n)\ge\sqrt p(1-\rho/2)^n,\qquad
n\ge\left\lceil\frac{\log(\sqrt p/\varepsilon)}{-\log(1-\rho/2)}\right\rceil
\]
as necessary time to reach relative error $\varepsilon$. For the actual
archived step, replace $\rho/2$ by the per-update rate cutoff. The positive
restriction separates slow acquisition from permanent nullspace error.
The compact-filter calculation in the
\href{gamma_structured_kernel_note.pdf}{structured-kernel note} supplies
lower bounds on positive slow energy with its declared numerical allowances.

This time bound is a separate target-dependent statement. A lower bound
on an eigenvalue ratio establishes a rate floor; it does not by itself
establish a necessary delay. The full target-weighted spectrum gives the
reference forecast; compact positive-energy bounds give conservative
necessary times. The collaborator's larger-gamma comparison is valid;
using that lower bound alone to conclude necessary small-gamma delay
would reverse the needed inequality.

\subsection*{The missing direction: an upper rate and target-overlap bound}
Fix reference slope $\Gamma\ge\gamma$. The same decomposition gives
\[
K_\gamma=K_\Gamma-G_{\Gamma,\gamma}+R_{\gamma,\Gamma},\qquad
R_{\gamma,\Gamma}=E_\gamma-E_\Gamma.
\]
The explicit positive gamma gain from Section~2 is now subtracted.
This is the correct direction for proving weak smaller-slope action on
needed directions. The correction must remain:
$K_\Gamma-G_{\Gamma,\gamma}$ need not itself be positive semidefinite.

Choose any orthonormal trial basis $V$ and set $P=VV^T$. Its columns are
candidate slow sample-space directions, not necessarily eigenvectors.
Define
\[
A_\gamma=V^TK_\gamma V,\qquad C_\gamma=(I-P)K_\gamma V,\qquad
q=\frac{\|V^Ty\|}{\|y\|}.
\]
$A_\gamma$ measures action inside the proposed slow subspace;
$C_\gamma$ measures action sent outside it. Both follow from the gamma
decomposition including its finite correction. For example,
\begin{align*}
\alpha_\gamma
&=\mu_{\max}\!\left(V^T(K_\Gamma-G_{\Gamma,\gamma})V\right)
+\|V^TR_{\gamma,\Gamma}V\|_2,\\
\mu_{\max}(A_\gamma)&\le\alpha_\gamma.
\end{align*}
Retaining the signed correction inside the small matrix can give a tighter
upper bound. Likewise retain corrected off-subspace action to obtain
$c_\gamma\ge\|C_\gamma\|_2$. Compressed action alone does not establish
that trial directions remain slow: coupling is the missing check.

\begin{theorem}[Small-gamma slow target energy and necessary GD delay]
Let $K_\gamma=J_\gamma J_\gamma^T$, $y\ne0$, $V^TV=I$, and suppose
the bounds above on $A_\gamma$ and $C_\gamma$ hold. For $b>\alpha_\gamma$,
set
\[
\delta=\min\!\left(1,\frac{c_\gamma}{b-\alpha_\gamma}\right),\qquad
L=\left[q\sqrt{1-\delta^2}-\delta\sqrt{1-q^2}\right]_+^2.
\]
At least a fraction $L$ of target energy lies in actual kernel modes
with eigenvalues in $[0,b]$. For any explicit readout witness $w_*$, put
\[
e_0=\frac{\|y-J_\gamma w_*\|^2}{\|y\|^2},\qquad p=[L-e_0]_+.
\]
At least $p$ target energy lies in positive modes $0<\mu_i(\gamma)\le b$.
With zero initialization, $0<\eta\|K_\gamma\|_2\le1$, and $\eta b<1$,
\[
\boxed{E_\gamma(n)\ge\sqrt p\,(1-\eta b)^n.}
\]
If $p>\varepsilon^2$, reaching relative error $\varepsilon$ requires
\[
\boxed{n\ge\left\lceil
\frac{\log(\sqrt p/\varepsilon)}{-\log(1-\eta b)}\right\rceil.}
\]
\end{theorem}
For a dimensionless ratio upper bound, divide $b$ by a \emph{lower}
bound on $\mu_1$, such as a nonzero Rayleigh quotient. Dividing by
Section~2's row-sum upper bound would give the wrong direction. Timing
above uses the actual prescribed step and avoids extra normalization.

\textbf{Proof.} Let $F=\mathbf1_{(b,\infty)}(K_\gamma)$ project onto
true fast modes. With $X=FV$, compressing
$K_\gamma V=VA_\gamma+C_\gamma$ gives the Sylvester equation
\[
(K_\gamma|_{\operatorname{ran}F})X-XA_\gamma=FC_\gamma.
\]
Its solution is the integral of
$e^{-tK_\gamma|_{\operatorname{ran}F}}FC_\gamma e^{tA_\gamma}$
over $t\ge0$. The exponential norms give the integrable factor
$e^{-t(b-\alpha_\gamma)}$, hence $\|FV\|_2\le\delta$.
If $q=0$ or $\delta=1$, the bound is zero and immediate. Otherwise
normalize $y$ and put $v=Py/q$, $d=\|Fv\|\le\delta$, and
$s=(I-F)v/\sqrt{1-d^2}$. The unit slow vector $s$ has overlap
$\sqrt{1-d^2}$ with $v$ and overlap at most $d$ with any unit vector
orthogonal to $v$. Decompose $y=qv+\sqrt{1-q^2}z$ to get
\[
\|(I-F)y\|\ge|s^Ty|
\ge\left[q\sqrt{1-d^2}-d\sqrt{1-q^2}\right]_+
\ge\sqrt L.
\]
The nullspace part of $y$ is orthogonal to every $J_\gamma w_*$, so
its squared norm fraction is at most $e_0$. Subtracting leaves $p$ in
positive slow modes. Each retains residual factor at least
$(1-\eta b)^n$ under the stated step condition. Summing squared
residuals proves the error bound; solving for $n$ gives the time bound.
\hfill$\square$

Eigenvectors are not assumed fixed across gamma. Gamma determines weak
action and coupling; the target enters through $q$ and the witness.
An easy target can make the conclusion trivial. The target-angle conversion
is sharp given its leakage bound, but coupling and kernel bounds can
remain conservative. This is a GD theorem, not an Adam rate law.

\subsection*{What the reverse comparison gives numerically}
We evaluate actual finite tanh action on trial spaces and audit the explicit
gamma gain and finite correction. We neither discard the correction nor
substitute a periodic spectrum. Candidate spaces are tails of a fixed
gamma-64 reference basis and eigenbands of the kernel projected onto
discrete polynomial spaces of degrees 32, 64, and 128. The latter require
a small spectral solve at each gamma. Polynomial truncation selects a
trial space; omitted action remains in $C_\gamma$.

A fixed spectral-cutoff grid selects the strongest necessary bound without
reading observed GD acquisition times. Taking the maximum valid lower
bound over these spaces is valid. The default adds explicit floating-point
allowances to compressed upper rates and coupling, with zero-, one-, and
ten-times allowance sensitivity. These are FP64 evaluations of an
exact-arithmetic theorem, not interval-certified numerical bounds.

Strongest necessary times at gammas 8, 12, 16, and 64 are
\textbf{6,458,359; 169,485; 34,555; and 5,927 updates}, compared with
actual crossings of \textbf{15,798,313; 186,057; 61,792; and 16,013}.
They retain about \textbf{41\%, 91\%, 56\%, and 37\%} of measured delay.
Degree-128 projected spaces win at the first three gammas; a reference
tail wins at gamma 64. Earlier compact-filter bounds remain separate.

Fixed reference directions are substantially less informative at small
gamma: they give only 361, 644, and 951 updates at gammas 8, 12, and 16.
The useful bound retains changing finite geometry and target alignment.
This repairs the logical direction and improves measured delay bounds,
but does not create a scalar formula in gamma alone.

\section{What we measure for Adam}
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.6980}{Adam} rescales readout-coordinate
updates and uses gradient history. For
$r_n=Jw_n-y$, its update is
\[
r_{n+1}=r_n-\alpha_n JD_n\widehat m_n,\qquad
D_n=\diag\bigl((\sqrt{\widehat v_n}+\epsilon_{\rm Adam})^{-1}\bigr).
\]
Raw-kernel eigenmodes are diagnostic coordinates, not independent Adam
decay modes. Even without momentum, the effective kernel would be the
time-dependent $JD_nJ^T$. We do not apply the GD time law to Adam.

Instead, at saved checkpoints measure
\[
R_{\gamma,n}(\rho)=\sum_{0<\rho_i(\gamma)\le\rho}
\frac{|u_i(\gamma)^Tr_n|^2}{\|y\|^2}.
\]
Initially this equals $P_\gamma(\rho)$. At a fixed update count, it asks
whether Adam removed target components occupying the GD-slow subspace.
A substantial remaining value supports the proposed obstruction for this
optimizer and protocol. A small value indicates acquisition of those
components; that outcome must also be reported.

\textbf{First EMA acquisition (main Adam metric).}
Let $\ell_n=E(n)^2$ be squared relative error. With half-life $s=100$
updates and $\beta=2^{-1/s}$, define
\begin{align*}
M_0&=\ell_0=1,\qquad M_n=\beta M_{n-1}+(1-\beta)\ell_n,\\
T_{\rm EMA}&=\min\{n\le H:M_n\le10^{-4}\}.
\end{align*}
Plots show $\sqrt{M_n}$ in relative-error units. Averaging squared error
matches training loss; this is not an EMA of the error norm. Initialization
retains the true initial loss instead of a falsely low average. Crossing
means first acquisition of the smoothed-loss threshold, not permanent
settlement. Runs without a crossing are censored at $H=200{,}000$.

The half-life is one shared, post-hoc plotting choice, not a gamma-specific
tuning. Sensitivity uses 10, 30, 100, 300, 1,000, and 3,000 updates.
Metrics use every recorded update, not sparse-checkpoint interpolation.
No optimizer reruns or settings changes are involved.

The EMA itself has a resolution limit. Nonnegative losses and $M_0=1$
imply $M_n\ge\beta^n$. Even zero loss after initialization cannot cross
before $\lceil s\log_2(10^4)\rceil$ updates:
\textbf{1,329 updates at half-life 100}. The gamma-64 crossing at 1,486
is close to this smoothing floor. EMA compresses differences between
sufficiently fast runs; it is not an exact optimizer time. The sensitivity
figure explicitly displays this floor.

\textbf{Sustained acquisition (supporting diagnostic).}
With the same horizon and $\varepsilon=0.01$,
\[
T_{\rm sust}=\min\{n:E(k)\le\varepsilon\text{ for all }k=n,\ldots,H\}.
\]
If $E(H)>\varepsilon$, acquisition is unobserved within the horizon.
For a successful run, this is one plus the last update above threshold,
or zero when no update is above threshold. Report the confirmation length
$H-T_{\rm sust}+1$. No smoothing is used and nothing is asserted beyond $H$.

\section{Matched experimental protocol}
The primary target is
$f(x)=\sin(2\pi x)+\frac12\sin(6\pi x)+\frac14\sin(10\pi x)$ on
$[-1,1]$. The archive uses spacing $h=1/256$, 559 tanh features plus bias,
and 8,193 uniformly spaced training inputs. Centers, samples, target, raw
readout coordinates, and zero initialization are fixed; slopes are 8,
12, 16, and 64.

Common-recipe Adam uses initial rate $10^{-3}$, additive epsilon
$10^{-12}$, and moments $(0.9,0.999)$. Its rate is constant through
20,000 updates, decays by cosine to $10^{-3}$ of its initial value at
50,000, then remains fixed. Runs continue to 200,000 updates. This
schedule is a material part of the acquisition result.

Sensitivity comparisons reuse validation-selected recipes: five initial
rates crossed with two epsilons, selected by median validation error at
40,000, 42,500, 45,000, 47,500, and 50,000 updates; optimizer states
continue intact. No new selection uses present spectral projections.
All plots concern training-grid optimization; separate validation data
selected the sensitivity recipes. No new held-out generalization claim
is made.

Supporting targets are $\exp(\sin(3\pi x))$, $1/(1+25x^2)$,
$\sqrt5\,x^2$, and $\sqrt2\sin(2\pi x)$. Targets and checkpoints are not
selected for agreement with the hypothesis. These scalings are retained
because Adam's additive epsilon makes scaling part of the protocol.
The saved SVD cutoff is numerical, not an exact-rank statement. Omitted
energy is reported as unresolved, not automatically as nullspace.

\subsection*{The spectral theorem is informative, with measurable slack}
At rank 32 the old ratio is $5.2174\times10^{-9}$. Bounds at gammas
12, 16, and 64 are $1.8172\times10^{-7}$, $2.2340\times10^{-6}$,
and $1.9195\times10^{-4}$, retaining about 26\%, 30\%, and 35\% of
actual new ratios. The bound is within a factor of four at this prescribed
rank while exposing a large explicit gamma effect. Rank 64 is less tight:
bounds retain only 5.3\%, 5.7\%, and 8.0\%. The gamma-8 baseline rank-64
bound is numerically unresolved and labeled accordingly. Ranks are not
selected after seeing tightness.

\subsection*{The slow subspace explains the remaining gamma-8 error}
At illustrative cutoff $\rho=2\times10^{-6}$, gamma 8 has initial
positive slow energy $0.0433333$. After 200,000 common-recipe Adam
updates, the remaining energy is $1.4709443\times10^{-4}$, alone
exceeding the $10^{-4}$ budget for 1\% error. Total relative error is
0.0121283; 99.9998\% of residual energy lies in that band. Adam removed
about 99.66\% of its initial energy, but the remainder prevents acquisition.

At the same cutoff, final slow residual energies are
$2.2660\times10^{-6}$, $1.0437\times10^{-7}$, and $3.8307\times10^{-8}$
at gammas 12, 16, and 64, below tolerance. The cutoff is a descriptive
probe inherited from the earlier $\eta\mu\le10^{-6}$ analysis under
$\eta\mu_1\simeq1/2$. Main cumulative curves expose full cutoff
dependence. The subspaces share a spectral definition across gamma,
not the same eigenvectors.

Initial slow energy is not monotone: it is $2.85\times10^{-7}$ at
gamma 16 and $1.48\times10^{-6}$ at gamma 64. Adam can increase a
diagnostic subspace's residual energy: at 20,000 updates the gamma-12
and gamma-16 slow energies exceed their initial values. These are
projected residuals, not necessarily untouched initial target content.

\subsection*{First EMA crossings reveal early acquisition}
Common Adam first crosses raw 1\% at 4,084, 1,361, and 333 updates
for gammas 12, 16, and 64. Loss-EMA first crossings are 7,688, 1,900,
and 1,486. This resolves earlier learning concealed by the previous
sustained-crossing plot. EMA later recrosses above threshold 73, 97,
and 98 times: first acquisition is not settled acquisition. Raw sustained
crossings remain 41,397, 41,412, and 41,539, reflecting oscillations
and rate decay. Gamma 8 never crosses within 200,000 under either recipe.

Window sensitivity matters. Half-life 30 gives common crossings 4,374,
1,430, and 510; half-life 300 gives 26,465, 11,278, and 5,342.
At half-life 1,000 they cluster near 28,000--30,000 and lose monotone
ordering. Long memory and oscillations can restore a nearly flat comparison.
Selected-recipe half-life-100 crossings are 40,208, 41,716, and 10,076.
The evidence supports the stated target, recipe, and metric, not universal
monotonic Adam improvement.

Executed GD reaches 1\% at 15,798,313, 186,057, 61,792, and 16,013
updates. Compact necessary times are 5,072,048, 130,057, 29,640, and
5,421. The full constructed-spectrum forecast agrees with executed
crossings; conservative bounds retain about 32\%, 70\%, 48\%, and
34\% of observed delay. These times do not follow by substituting
panel A's ratio lower bounds.
