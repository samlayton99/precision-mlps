# GD scale acquisition: the effective gradient and the bounds it supports

The question is how quickly ordinary gradient descent acquires a specified
population of slopes, and why that rate can be very small even when useful
representations exist. Our constructions and readout solves address available
capacity. This note concerns the dynamics of reaching useful geometry.

The strongest supported reduction is that, after the coarse transient, the
**effective fine-gradient** accounts for almost all slope motion in the tested
GD trajectories. Its gain contains two terms: a direct fine-residual gain and
a correction from the coarse residual at its moving balance. Both must be
retained. Small departure from that balance is a separate observation.

For degree 9, the resulting force mainly corrects small quadratic and cubic
errors generated by the network; the large unresolved target component exerts
a much weaker force. Measurements identify the relaxation rates of these
generated errors. A reduced model turns them into a conditional movement
budget, while a complementary argument already gives a finite-time bound for
full GD from specified checkpoints. The remaining task is to obtain a sharper,
interpretable bound on the evolving effective gain, with assumptions supported
by the measurements.

**Notation used throughout. Gradients have the opposite sign to GD motion.**

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $W,m$ | Number of neurons and training points. |
| $a,b,c,d$ | Slopes, hidden biases, readout weights, and output bias. |
| $\theta,g$ | All parameters and their full loss gradient. |
| $\gamma_j=\lvert a_j\rvert$, $\bar\gamma$ | Individual absolute slope and its population mean. |
| $q_k,e_k$ | An empirical orthonormal output mode and its residual coefficient. |
| $C,H$ | Coarse modes $0,1$ and their fine complement. These label residual groups. |
| $J_{I,C},J_{I,H}$ | Derivatives of those residual coefficients with respect to parameter block $I$. |
| $K_{CC},K_{CH},K_{HH}$ | Blocks of the residual kernel, including every trained parameter block. |
| $B=K_{CC}^{-1}K_{CH}$ | Response map determining the instantaneous coarse balance. |
| $z_C=e_C+Be_H$ | Departure of the actual coarse residual from that balance. |
| $T_a,F_a=T_ae_H$ | Effective slope gain and effective slope gradient. |
| $S$ | Effective kernel for fine-residual relaxation. |
| $\eta,n,t=\eta n$ | GD step size, update count, and physical time. |
| $\mathcal D_{p,\Gamma}$ | Distance in slope space to acquiring fraction $p$ above scale $\Gamma$. |

## 1. Fix the model, the question, and the normalization

**Purpose.** Here we specify the actual training dynamics so that the later
gain decomposition and time bounds refer to the same algorithm. We also
separate a scale-acquisition event from approximation capacity.

The network, residual, and loss are

$$
f_\theta(x)=d+\sum_{j=1}^W c_j\tanh(a_jx+b_j),\qquad
r=f_\theta-y,\qquad
L=\tfrac12\langle r,r\rangle_m,
$$

where $\langle u,v\rangle_m=m^{-1}\sum_i u(x_i)v(x_i)$. Ordinary simultaneous
GD updates all four parameter blocks using the same old state:

$$
\theta_{n+1}=\theta_n-\eta\nabla L(\theta_n).
$$

The main experiments use $W=177$, $m=2048$, $\eta=0.002$, random nonzero
readouts, and FP64. The independent 8192-point evaluation grid measures
approximation error. It is distinct from the empirical training loss used
in the theorem. Larger slopes and lower evaluation error are related
measurements, but neither is a definition of the other.

A specified acquisition event is

$$
P_\Gamma(a)=\frac1W\#\{j:|a_j|\ge\Gamma\}\ge p.
$$

The construction motivates scales and populations worth investigating. It
does not, by itself, prove that every accurate representation must satisfy
this event. Our dynamical result should state the threshold and time horizon
it actually controls.

## 2. Derive the two-term effective gain

### 2.1 Residual coordinates reveal the coupled forces

**Purpose.** In this section we express the gradient in coarse and fine
residual coordinates. This shows how fine fitting can regenerate coarse
error even after the initial coarse transient.

Choose fixed output modes orthonormal in the empirical mean inner product.
Let $e_k=\langle q_k,r\rangle_m$, with $q_0,q_1$ spanning constant and linear
functions. For the identities here, let $H$ contain the complete empirical
complement. A truncated implementation must additionally retain its omitted
residual terms; truncation is not implicit in these equations.

For slopes,

$$
(J_a)_{kj}=\frac{\partial e_k}{\partial a_j}
=c_j\left\langle q_k,x\operatorname{sech}^2(a_jx+b_j)\right\rangle_m.
$$

Writing $J=[J_a\ J_b\ J_c\ J_d]$, orthonormality gives

$$
g=J^Te,\qquad K=JJ^T,\qquad \dot e=-Ke.
$$

The last equation is the gradient-flow vector field. It can be evaluated at
a GD state; integrating it requires care about finite steps. The algebraic
gradient identities hold at each GD state without a flow approximation.

Partitioning by residual group gives

$$
\dot e_C=-K_{CC}e_C-K_{CH}e_H,\qquad
\dot e_H=-K_{HC}e_C-K_{HH}e_H.
$$

For example,
$K_{CH}=\sum_{I\in\{a,b,c,d\}}J_{I,C}J_{I,H}^T$.
The coupling includes slopes, biases, and readouts. Coarse fitting is not
synonymous with readout-only fitting.

### 2.2 Balance the coarse residual, then substitute into the slope gradient

**Purpose.** We now derive the gain that survives accurate coarse tracking.
This distinguishes the coarse force at balance from the correction due to
failure to track that balance.

Assume $K_{CC}$ is invertible. Holding the current kernel and $e_H$ fixed,
the coarse equation has zero velocity at

$$
e_C^{\rm bal}=-K_{CC}^{-1}K_{CH}e_H=-Be_H.
$$

This is an instantaneous balance, not a stationary state of the whole
network. It moves as the parameters and fine residual evolve. Define
$z_C=e_C-e_C^{\rm bal}$, so that $e_C=-Be_H+z_C$.
Substitute this identity into the slope gradient:

$$
\begin{aligned}
g_a
&=J_{a,H}^Te_H+J_{a,C}^Te_C\\
&=\underbrace{J_{a,H}^Te_H}_{F_{\rm direct}}
+\underbrace{\left(-J_{a,C}^TK_{CC}^{-1}K_{CH}e_H\right)}_{F_{\rm balanced}}
+\underbrace{J_{a,C}^Tz_C}_{F_{\rm tracking}}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{T_a=J_{a,H}^T-J_{a,C}^TK_{CC}^{-1}K_{CH}},\qquad
F_a=F_{\rm direct}+F_{\rm balanced}.
$$

The direct term maps fine residual into a slope gradient. In the second term,
$K_{CH}e_H$ is the coarse forcing induced by fine fitting,
$K_{CC}^{-1}$ determines the compensating coarse residual, and
$J_{a,C}^T$ turns that residual into a slope gradient.

**The empirically small term is $F_{\rm tracking}$, not
$F_{\rm balanced}$.** The latter remains present when $z_C=0$. Replacing
accurate tracking by $e_C=0$ would discard part of the leading force.

Nor does the minus sign prove that the correction always suppresses outward
motion. Put $u=J_{a,H}^Te_H$ and
$v=J_{a,C}^TK_{CC}^{-1}K_{CH}e_H$. Then

$$
\|F_a\|^2=\|u\|^2+\|v\|^2-2u^Tv.
$$

Their magnitudes and relative directions both matter. Signed scale motion
additionally depends on their alignment with the current slopes.

### 2.3 Fine-error fitting and slope movement have different gains

**Purpose.** We derive the residual kernel after coarse balance to show why
fitting progress alone cannot determine scale-acquisition speed.

Substituting $e_C=-Be_H+z_C$ into the fine equation gives

$$
\dot e_H=-Se_H-K_{HC}z_C,\qquad
S=K_{HH}-K_{HC}K_{CC}^{-1}K_{CH}.
$$

For each parameter block define $T_I=J_{I,H}^T-J_{I,C}^TB$.
Expanding the stacked effective gain and using $K_{CC}B=K_{CH}$ yields

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_I T_I^TT_I
&=K_{HH}-K_{HC}B-B^TK_{CH}+B^TK_{CC}B\\
&=S.
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus $G_a:=T_a^TT_a\preceq S$. At exact coarse balance,

$$
-\frac{d}{dt}\frac12\|e_H\|^2=e_H^TSe_H,\qquad
\|\dot a\|^2=e_H^TG_ae_H.
$$

The first includes fine fitting through every parameter block. The second
counts slope motion. A small ratio of the second to the first means that
fine-error dissipation mostly occurs through other blocks. It does not yet
specify outward direction or the distribution of motion across neurons.

## 3. Examples: which of the two gain terms matter?

**Purpose.** These examples give the algebra a concrete interpretation.
They distinguish cancellation inside the effective gain from negligible
tracking outside it.

### 3.1 A small exact algebraic example

Consider a local linear model with one slope coordinate and one readout
coordinate, whose coarse and fine Jacobian rows are

$$
J_C=(3,0),\qquad J_H=(1,1).
$$

Then $K_{CC}=9$, $K_{CH}=3$, and $B=1/3$. The slope gain is
$T_a=1-3(1/3)=0$, while the readout gain is $T_c=1$ and $S=1$.
At a state with $e_H=1$ and $e_C=-1/3$, direct fine slope gradient $+1$
and balanced coarse slope gradient $-1$ cancel exactly. The instantaneous
slope velocity is zero, while the fine residual decreases at rate $-1$.

This illustrates the distinction between fitting and slope motion. It is a
calculation at a balanced state: ordinary dynamics can subsequently create
tracking error as the balance moves. It is not an assertion that perfect
balance is invariant.

For a gradient contribution $F$, its instantaneous contribution to mean-scale

## 4. Split the residual into modes only after retaining both gain terms

**Purpose.** We now ask which residual components drive the combined
effective gradient. This is a second decomposition, distinct from the
two-term decomposition of the gain.

For each fine mode $k$,

$$
T_{a,k}=J_{a,k}^T-J_{a,C}^TK_{CC}^{-1}K_{Ck},\qquad
F_a=\sum_{k\ge2}T_{a,k}e_k.
$$

Every modal force in this sum already includes its balanced coarse
correction. Statements about quadratic or cubic dominance refer to these
combined columns, not to the first gain term alone.

For the degree-9 target,

$$
y=0.3q_0+0.4q_1+\sqrt{0.75}\,q_9.
$$

Target coefficients in modes 2–8 vanish. Residual in those modes is output
generated by the network itself. Correcting it is legitimate loss descent,
but need not advance the hard target or increase slopes.

The [mode audit](d34_coarse_balance_stagnation.md#7-what-the-audit-and-continuations-establish)
establishes the following in the retained degree-9 states:

- The signed inward contribution of modes 2–8 is 8,098–29,718 times the
  outward contribution of mode 9 across the audited trajectories. These are
  correlated checkpoints from five seeds, not independent trials.
- Quadratic error accounts for 14.1–63.4% of generated inward mean motion;
  the cubic mode supplies almost all the remainder. A cubic-only
  explanation misses a substantial contribution from biases and
  heterogeneous particles.
- The hard residual has magnitude approximately 0.866, yet its effective
  force is only of order $10^{-10}$, compared with $10^{-6}$–$10^{-5}$ for
  generated errors. Residual size and slope coupling are different factors.

The penalty interventions test this explanation by changing the weights on
modes 2–8 while retaining the target and initial parameters.

**Degree-9 mean-scale changes over 600k–1.1m, medians over independent seeds
20–24. Weight 1 is ordinary GD; other weights change the diagnostic loss.**

| Lower-mode weight | Change in mean absolute slope |
|---|---:|
| 0 | $+3.117\times10^{-8}$ |
| 0.1 | $-7.857\times10^{-6}$ |
| 1 | $-7.455\times10^{-5}$ |
| 10 | $-6.394\times10^{-4}$ |

All retain relative evaluation MSE approximately 0.750006. Lower-mode
penalties control contraction, but removing them supplies no appreciable
acquisition over this interval. This extends the earlier short intervention;
it is confirmation of that mechanism. The tiny positive weight-zero motion
should not be treated as a grid-independent acquisition result.

There is a precision issue for a sharper theorem: modes 4–8 are small
relative to total contraction, but their combined inward contribution is
18–84% of the outward hard-mode contribution in the earlier audited states.
A model accurate for bulk motion may therefore be inadequate for predicting
the much smaller outward rate. Bounds must be compared with the quantity
they are intended to explain.

## 5. Derive what changes the effective force, then identify the slow process

**Purpose.** Small tracking identifies the main instantaneous gradient.
It does not explain its future magnitude. Here we derive the evolution of
that gradient and use the measurements to distinguish sensitivity changes,
residual relaxation, and tracking influence.

Differentiate $F_a=T_ae_H$ and substitute the fine-residual equation:

$$
\boxed{
\dot F_a=\dot T_ae_H-T_aSe_H-T_aK_{HC}z_C.
}
$$

The first term changes the gain; the second changes the residual acting
through it; the third is the influence of imperfect tracking on residual
evolution. In particular,

$$
\dot T_a=\dot J_{a,H}^T-\dot J_{a,C}^TB-J_{a,C}^T\dot B,
\qquad
\dot B=K_{CC}^{-1}(\dot K_{CH}-\dot K_{CC}B).
$$

Thus a small tracking error does not imply a fixed gain. Readout and hidden
parameter changes enter both Jacobians and the response map $B$.

For any contribution $D$ to $\dot F_a$, its signed contribution to the
logarithmic force-amplitude rate is $F_a^TD/\|F_a\|^2$. This is how the
derivative measurements are interpreted. Positive values strengthen the
force norm; they do not directly mean outward slope motion.

In the 25 original degree-9 diagnostic states, fine-residual evolution
accounts for 89–95% of force decline, shape changes for about 5–9%, and
readout changes for less than 2%. Tracking is negligible. The contributions
do not hide a large cancellation of rapidly changing forces. Hard-mode
replenishment is also tiny relative to generated-error relaxation.

Two loaded directions closely associated with quadratic and cubic output
errors explain the initial force. Their local relaxation times are
2.8–3.7 million and 52–80 million updates per e-fold, respectively. Over
the actual 600k–6m continuation, quadratic error falls to 15–25% of its
starting magnitude, while cubic error retains 90–94% and the hard residual
barely changes. These observations identify slow generated-error relaxation
as the main process in this regime.

The [autonomous forecasts](d34_coarse_balance_stagnation.md#how-much-coupling-must-evolve-to-predict-the-motion)
test this account without using future true states as coefficients. The
frozen full tangent predicts the 600k–6m slope displacement to 4.1–5.5%
relative error across five seeds; constant-gradient errors are 71–114%.
The discrepancy is normalized by the actual displacement, not the absolute
parameter size. This supports a local model while retaining measurable
nonlinear corrections.

Fresh-seed forecasts in the
[latest campaign](../results/checkpoint_D_optimizers/expD34_readout_race/force_plateaus/README.md)
were saved before subsequent training and confirm accurate degree-9 force
prediction over 500k-update intervals. Degree 5 supplies a limitation:
late force-prediction error rises substantially during a changing regime.
A theorem should specify a controlled neighborhood and horizon, rather than
assume fixed sensitivities for every target and stage of training.

## 6. Turn gradient control into a scale-acquisition bound

### 6.1 Direction, crossings, and population distance

**Purpose.** Here we connect the gradient to the quantity the theorem must
control. This explains why a force norm or a contracting mean alone is not
the desired population result.

For one GD step, $\delta a_n=-\eta g_{a,n}$, with
$\operatorname{sign}(0)=0$, the exact mean-scale increment is

$$
\bar\gamma_{n+1}-\bar\gamma_n
=-\frac\eta W\operatorname{sign}(a_n)^Tg_{a,n}+X_n,
$$

$$
X_n=\frac1W\sum_j\left(
|a_{j,n}+\delta a_{j,n}|-|a_{j,n}|
-\operatorname{sign}(a_{j,n})\delta a_{j,n}
\right)\ge0.
$$

$X_n$ handles crossings through zero. Even a negative mean increment does
not imply that every neuron contracts: roughly 46–50% of neurons have
positive instantaneous absolute-slope velocity in the audited degree-9
states. A particlewise contraction assumption would describe the data
incorrectly.

For the population event, sort the deficits
$d_j=(\Gamma-|a_{j,s}|)_+$ increasingly. The exact distance to acquisition is

$$
\mathcal D_{p,\Gamma}(a_s)
=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\lceil pW\rceil}d_{(j)}^2\right)^{1/2}.
$$

Each acquired coordinate must move at least its deficit, and selecting the
cheapest required coordinates attains this distance. Meanwhile, the triangle
inequality bounds displacement at every prefix by the slope path:

$$
\|a_{s+k}-a_s\|\le
\eta\sum_{n=s}^{s+k-1}\|g_{a,n}\|
\le\eta\sum_{n=s}^{s+N-1}
\big(\|F_{a,n}\|+\|F_{{\rm tracking},n}\|\big).
$$

An upper bound on the last expression below $\mathcal D_{p,\Gamma}$ rules
out acquisition through $N$ further updates. This statement is exact for
discrete GD. Summing an observed trajectory gives a retrospective check;
a predictive theorem needs a bound obtained without that future trajectory.

### 6.2 The mechanism supplies a conditional bound for a reduced model

**Purpose.** We solve the two-generated-mode approximation to show how its
relaxation rates and weak hard forcing produce a movement budget. We then
identify what must be controlled to transfer it to actual GD.

Freeze the full-parameter effective tangents of modes 2 and 3 at a starting
state, collect them as the columns of $T_G$, and let $u=(e_2,e_3)^T$.
Hold the hard residual and its effective tangent fixed, giving a full
parameter force $h=T_{\cdot,9}e_9$. Set the coarse-tracking correction to
zero and neglect fine modes other than 2, 3, and 9. Under these explicit
approximations,

$$
u_{n+1}=u_n-\eta(A_Gu_n+T_G^Th),\qquad
\widehat\theta_{n+1}=\widehat\theta_n-\eta(T_Gu_n+h),
\quad A_G=T_G^TT_G.
$$

If $A_G$ is positive definite, define
$u_*=-A_G^{-1}T_G^Th$ and $f_\infty=T_Gu_*+h$.
Let $v_i,\nu_i$ be its orthonormal eigenvectors and positive eigenvalues,
and set $b_i=v_i^T(u_0-u_*)$. The recurrence has the explicit solution

$$
\widehat g_n=f_\infty+
\sum_{i=1}^{2}b_i(1-\eta\nu_i)^nT_Gv_i.
$$

For $0<\eta\nu_i\le1$, apply the triangle inequality to the slope update
and sum the geometric series:

$$
\sum_{n<N}\|\widehat a_{n+1}-\widehat a_n\|
\le
\underbrace{\sum_{i=1}^{2}|b_i|\|(T_Gv_i)_a\|
\frac{1-(1-\eta\nu_i)^N}{\nu_i}}_{\text{generated-error movement}}
+\underbrace{\eta N\|(f_\infty)_a\|}_{\text{remaining hard-drive movement}}.
$$

The first term has a finite total budget. The second accumulates slowly
when the remaining hard force is weak. This is a rate statement about a
specific mechanism. It is not obtained by assuming every slope contracts.

The derivation is a theorem for the reduced model. Transferring it to actual
GD requires an independently bounded prediction error or cumulative force
defect, including gain drift, other residual modes, hard-residual drift,
and tracking. Small observed error is validation, not itself that bound.
The existing conservative frozen-model enclosure closes for approximately
278k–438k further updates. That identifies a limitation of this bound even
though the predictor remains accurate over longer measured intervals.

## 7. A complementary finite-time theorem already closes for full GD

**Purpose.** We now show how the same weak-access mechanism motivates a
bound that handles changing parameters directly. The key is to bound the
loss that can decrease inside a neighborhood, rather than charge the entire
large hard residual to the available movement budget.

### 7.1 Construct a local hard-mode loss floor

Consider a parameter ball $\|\theta-\theta_s\|\le R$. Suppose an analytic
degree-8 polynomial approximation to each tanh feature has uniform error
at most $\delta_j(R)$ throughout that ball. Since $q_9$ is orthogonal to
degree-8 polynomials and has empirical norm one,

$$
|\langle q_9,\tanh(a_jx+b_j)\rangle_m|\le\delta_j(R).
$$

The output bias has zero projection on $q_9$. Splitting readouts into their
starting values and increments, then applying Cauchy–Schwarz, gives

$$
|\langle q_9,f_\theta\rangle_m|
\le H_R:=\sum_j|c_{j,s}|\delta_j(R)+R\|\delta(R)\|_2.
$$

Writing $Y_9=\langle q_9,y\rangle_m$ for the target coefficient, the full
empirical loss obeys

$$
L(\theta)\ge\ell_R:=\tfrac12[|Y_9|-H_R]_+^2.
$$

The [existing derivation](d34_coarse_balance_stagnation.md#a-stronger-bound-from-the-loss-the-network-cannot-yet-remove)
constructs $\delta_j(R)$ using analyticity of tanh on a Bernstein ellipse
and a geometric bound on its polynomial tail. It also bounds the Hessian
throughout the parameter ball. These are neighborhood bounds; they do not
insert the future measured slopes as an assumption.

### 7.2 Use descent to bound travel, then close the neighborhood condition

Let $U_R$ bound the Hessian operator norm in the ball, let
$q_R=1-\eta U_R/2>0$, and define

$$
\Delta_R=L(\theta_s)-\ell_R,\qquad
r_R=\frac{R-\eta\|g_s\|}{1+\eta U_R}>0.
$$

If the previous cumulative path is below $r_R$, the gradient bound
$\|g_n\|\le\|g_s\|+U_Rr_R$ keeps the entire next step inside radius $R$,
because

$$
r_R+\eta(\|g_s\|+U_Rr_R)=R.
$$

The descent lemma consequently applies. Sum it over $k$ steps and use the
loss floor:

$$
\eta q_R\sum_{n=s}^{s+k-1}\|g_n\|^2\le\Delta_R.
$$

Cauchy–Schwarz now gives the cumulative full-parameter path bound

$$
\eta\sum_{n=s}^{s+k-1}\|g_n\|
\le\sqrt{\frac{\eta k\Delta_R}{q_R}}=:Q_k.
$$

If $Q_N<r_R$, then every prefix satisfies $Q_k\le Q_N<r_R$, closing the
induction. No prior assumption that the actual trajectory stays in the
neighborhood is needed. If additionally
$Q_N<\mathcal D_{p,\Gamma}(a_s)$, the specified slope population cannot be
acquired through those $N$ updates.

For $\Delta_R>0$, the resulting sufficient horizon is

$$
N<\frac{q_R}{\eta\Delta_R}
\min\{r_R^2,\mathcal D_{p,\Gamma}(a_s)^2\}.
$$

When $\Delta_R=0$, use the preceding $Q_N$ criterion directly instead of
dividing by $\Delta_R$.

This theorem applies to full simultaneous GD. It retains tracking and
changing sensitivities, and does not require mean contraction. The modal
analysis explains the observed force and rates; the loss-floor theorem
provides a complementary bound on how far full GD can travel.

### 7.3 What has actually been established numerically

At ten degree-9 checkpoints, five seeds at 100k and 600k, the remaining
half-MSE is about 0.375, but the loss available above the local floor is only
$7.74\times10^{-7}$–$1.52\times10^{-6}$. Evaluating the theorem conditions
gives horizons of 10.237–17.094 million additional updates. A common
ten-million-update statement has margin below every evaluated horizon.
Every absolute slope is bounded above by 0.388, and empirical relative
training MSE remains above 0.749998 on those intervals.

These are evaluations of a real-arithmetic theorem using ordinary FP64
constants, not directed-rounding numerical certificates. They predict
beyond the simulated interval; those longer trajectories were not all run.
The result starts from the specified checkpoints. A theorem deriving entry
into this regime from random initialization is a further question, not a
prerequisite for stating this conditional finite-time result correctly.

The [bounds and validation records](d34_coarse_balance_stagnation.md#what-can-be-bounded-without-the-future-trajectory)
retain the constants, candidate radii, and independent trajectory checks.

## 8. Current assumptions, conclusions, and proof obligations

**Purpose.** This section separates what the framework defines, what the
experiments support, and what remains to be bounded. It is the checklist
against which an additional experiment should justify its value.

**Current claim ledger. Evidence scope is ordinary equal-rate GD unless
explicitly stated otherwise.**

| Claim | Present status | Consequence for the theorem |
|---|---|---|
| The effective gain contains direct fine and balanced coarse terms. | Exact identity; both contributions are substantial in measured examples. | Retain both terms and their interference. |
| Departure from coarse balance contributes little slope motion. | Supported by integrated budgets and tracking-removal continuations. Across 65 GD cases, tracking is at most 0.469% of the summed component-norm budget over 20k–600k. | Use an explicit small tracking allowance; observed smallness motivates a hypothesis, not a uniform proof. |
| The hard residual dominates the effective force. | False in the degree-9 regime. Generated quadratic/cubic errors dominate motion. | Bound residual-weighted gain columns, not residual magnitude alone. |
| Every slope moves inward. | False despite mean contraction. | Use population distances or outward-travel budgets. |
| Slow force evolution requires rapid replenishment balancing rapid depletion. | Unsupported in the audited degree-9 regime; slow generated-error relaxation explains most evolution. | Use the measured relaxation structure as the reduced-model hypothesis. |
| A frozen gain predicts all stages. | False; it is accurate locally for degree 9 and less reliable during recovery. | Bound drift over a specified neighborhood and horizon. |
| A small total-force error controls tiny hard-directed motion. | Not generally true. Smaller omitted terms can rival the hard-mode drive. | Choose the error tolerance for the claimed acquisition bound, not only total-force accuracy. |
| A finite-time theorem for actual GD requires solving initialization first. | False. The local loss-floor argument already gives a conditional result. | State the post-transient theorem and its numerical qualifications explicitly. |

The 0.469% statistic divides accumulated tracking norms by accumulated
effective-plus-tracking norms. It is neither a signed-motion fraction nor
the same statistic as the earlier degree-9 median ratio of integrated
tracking norm to full-gradient norm, 0.00111%.

Small slope tracking also does not automatically bound its influence on
future fine-residual evolution, $K_{HC}z_C$, or certify stability of every
parameter block. The [tracking theory](d34_transport_scale_barrier.md#eliminate-the-coarse-residual-without-discarding-its-regeneration)
gives a kernel-metric estimate and an exact discrete recurrence with step
defects. Those are available routes to a bound; kernel conditioning, drift,
and forcing must still be controlled on the interval being claimed.

The latest campaign strengthens the tracking intervention, lower-mode
penalty response, and independent checkpoint forecasts. Most of the
degree-9 mechanism was identified earlier. Longer runs also delimit the
regime in which the approximations remain useful. Adam is a separate
comparison: its momentum and adaptive scaling invalidate a direct transfer
of these GD small-tracking observations, so its oscillation gallery is not
a premise of the GD theorem.

The next mathematical obligation within this framework is to sharpen the
bound on accumulated effective-gradient motion while retaining the two-term
gain, generated-error relaxation, weak hard drive, and explicit defects.
An additional experiment should identify a specific assumption or term,
state its predicted behavior and a contrary outcome, and explain how either
result changes that bound. A further trajectory with no such consequence
does not advance this argument.
