```latex
\subsection{Practical implementation versus theoretical recovery}
\label{sec:quill-practical-recovery}

For fixed neurons, unscaled truncated SVD fits the readout
from sampled target values without bounds on the target's
analytic extension. We use one relative cutoff across widths.
The recovery guarantee in Appendix~A applies to reference-scaled
SVD. Figure~\ref{fig:quill-practical-vs-theorem} compares
these two procedures.

Both use the same neurons, samples, sample normalization,
and FP64 DGEJSV solver. The raw solve uses relative cutoff
$2^{-52}$ at every width. The reference-scaled solve uses
(A.65) with $E_{\mathrm{sharp}}$ and the numerical bounds
in Appendix~A.3.3. The original width and bandwidth experiments
use DGELSD, and the precision-controlled experiment above
uses DGELSS; this comparison holds the SVD backend fixed.
```

```latex
We fix $\lambda=1/4$,
$R=\lceil(11/8)\sqrt N\rceil$, $\delta=4$, and
$\Delta=9/16$, using the general construction in Appendix~A.1.
The tested resolutions are
\[
N\in\{64,128,196,256,320,384,448,512,576,640,704,768\},
\]
with total hidden width $W=N+2R+1$ and $M=8N+1$
equispaced training samples on $[-1,1]$.
The sampling parameters are $s=8$, $p=1$, and $\varrho=2$;
non-dyadic widths use the uniform input-error bound (A.81).

For the reference-scaled solve, the analytic magnitude
bounds $B$ for $e^x$, $\sin(4\pi x)$, $\sin(24\pi x)$,
the chirp, and the wave packet, respectively, are outward
upper bounds on
\[
e^{89/16},\qquad \cosh(16\pi),\qquad
\cosh(96\pi),\qquad \cosh(420\pi),\qquad
e^{128}\cosh(32\pi).
\]
These follow from
$U_\delta(I_\Delta)\subseteq
\{z:|\operatorname{Re}z|\le1+\Delta+\delta,
|\operatorname{Im}z|\le\delta\}$.
MPFR stores these bounds, including those outside the FP64
exponent range.
```

```latex
We store $\gamma=N/8$ and $\beta_j=N/8-j/4$ exactly
in FP64, giving neurons $\tanh(\gamma x+\beta_j)$.
Target samples are computed offline at higher precision and
rounded to FP64. We use correctly rounded tanh values and
pairwise output summation. Model arrays and readout arithmetic
are FP64; bound verification uses directed 256-bit arithmetic
and FP64 expansion products.

Errors are measured on 16,384 independent uniform midpoint
samples. Doubling this grid changes the reported errors by
less than $0.32\%$. For all five functions at every tested
$W\ge301$, both methods have relative error below
$6\times10^{-13}$, and their readout $\ell^1$ norms differ
by less than $20\%$. At the smallest tested width, the
theoretical procedure has larger errors. In the tested
larger-width range, raw SVD achieves high accuracy and
similar weight magnitudes without the analytic bounds or
width-dependent cutoff calculation.
```
