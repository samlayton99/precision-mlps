\paragraph{Adam: a coarse stability constraint on fine motion.}
Adam reaches larger, more concentrated parameter energies, where the preceding
GD bound is uninformative. Its remaining acquisition gap has an additional
optimizer mechanism: coarse tracking contributes to the shared second moment
that scales the effective fine update. Across six targets, two widths, and two
seeds at update 130k, the leading adaptive curvature has median 98.7\% of the
local momentum stability threshold; the coarse contribution accounts for a
median 98.8\% of this curvature. Large-step GD provides a causal comparison:
attenuating tracking removes the observed loss oscillations in all 24 paired
starts. Removing the fine update's first-order coarse disturbance barely
changes native Adam acquisition. Turning off fine displacement altogether
preserves substantial late tracking for degree five, sine, and kink, while
several localized cases settle. Thus continuing fine displacement is not a
universal requirement for the observed coarse activity. A reduced adaptive
model illustrates how coarse oscillation can maintain a denominator that
limits fine drift even without such forcing
(Appendix~\ref{app:coarse-feedback}). These optimizer-specific results complement
the GD population theorem; they do not provide an Adam rate bound.
