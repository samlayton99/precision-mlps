Status: complete; one nonlinear Runge check and one primary figure, following Sam’s request to keep this small. Sam asks whether freezing readout coefficients makes slopes continue the amplitude adjustment the readout was making, and requests a fitted nonlinear example with mean readout magnitude and mean gamma over time.

Protocol: reuse early-Xavier freezing data to show actual error, mean absolute neuron readout (excluding output bias), and mean absolute slope. New control: Runge, the matched N=128/QI gamma-16 geometry, zero initial readout, identical samples and constant GD rate 0.002. Stop warmup at the first state with training and independent-grid relative error <=1%, with a 20,000-step cap. Clone that exact state into joint-GD and frozen-readout branches for 5,000 updates. Freeze output bias too. Save full scalar histories and sparse geometry/readout states. Plot the full history and a zoom around the branch. A best-affine error reference verifies that the fitted predictor is not approximately affine. This intentionally changes the initialization and fitting stage; it tests whether the early-Xavier trend persists, not which single change causes a difference.

Requirements checklist: ordinary one-forward/one-backward SGD per update; no optimizer momentum/state or per-sample persistent optimizer cache; no Krylov memory or width-dependent pass/reduction count; no numerical-zero detector. Same float64 for comparability, without a precision-agnostic claim. The 1% threshold is a prescribed diagnostic branch point far above roundoff, not a solver or line-search controller. No killed optimizer is being promoted; ordinary joint GD is the classical control. The architecture-specific freezing experiment is not a production candidate or litmus-test submission. Falsification: coefficient and gamma trends need not align after the predictor is nonlinear; lack of extra useful gamma motion after freezing weakens the proposed transfer interpretation.

Checks: first frozen geometry update equals continued joint GD; frozen coefficients including output bias stay bitwise fixed; analytic gradients match the recorded step. Independently confirm <=1% error and a much larger best-affine approximation error at the branch. Means use absolute values, all 177 neurons, and exclude output bias from readout size. No least-squares readout solve enters training.

Result: readout magnitude grows before the nonlinear branch. Frozen gamma grows by 6.32e-7, versus 3.93e-7 with continuing readout; fit remains near 1% when frozen versus 0.565% with joint GD. This is directionally consistent with the compensation hypothesis, not a general mechanism proof. The initial affine-case explanation uses already saved data. No further sweep is planned.
