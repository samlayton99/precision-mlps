\section{Coarse feedback and optimizer-dependent population motion}
\label{app:coarse-feedback}

The question in this appendix is whether coarse tracking can restrict useful
fine motion even when its signed contribution to slope growth is small.
We retain the effective fine and tracking gradients used in the GD analysis.
Adam processes these gradients differently, so a constraint on the raw fine
gradient need not constrain the update that actually moves the parameters.
The exact identities below explain what the interventions remove. The
fixed-preconditioner model illustrates a possible restriction; it is not a
persistence theorem for Adam.

\paragraph{Model and decomposition.}
On the fixed training grid, write
\[
 q_\theta(x)=d+\sum_{j=1}^W c_j\tanh(a_jx+b_j),\qquad
 L(\theta)=\tfrac12\|q_\theta-y\|^2.
\]
Inner products are normalized sample averages. Let $P_C$ project onto the
constant and linear sample directions, and put $P_H=I-P_C$.
In an orthonormal basis of the two-dimensional coarse space, define
$C=P_Cq_\theta$, $H=P_Hq_\theta$, $e_C=C-P_Cy$, and $e_H=H-P_Hy$.
Their Jacobians are $J_C=DC$ and $J_H=DH$. In particular, for a coarse basis
function $\phi_\ell$, the corresponding row of $J_C$ contains the averages
of $\phi_\ell$ times
\[
 \left(c_jx\operatorname{sech}^2(a_jx+b_j),\;
 c_j\operatorname{sech}^2(a_jx+b_j),\;
 \tanh(a_jx+b_j),\;1\right),
\]
with the first three blocks indexed by $j$ and a single output-bias entry.
When $K=J_CJ_C^\top$ is invertible, let
\begin{align}
 g_H&=J_H^\top e_H,&
 F&=(I-J_C^\top K^{-1}J_C)g_H,\label{eq:feedback-fine}\\
 z&=e_C+K^{-1}J_Cg_H,&R&=J_C^\top z.
\end{align}
Then $\nabla L=F+R$ and $J_CF=0$. The compensating coarse gradient remains
part of $F$; $R$ measures departure from the compensating equilibrium.

\paragraph{Two sources of renewed tracking.}
Let $e_C^*(\theta)=-K^{-1}J_Cg_H$, so $z=e_C-e_C^*$.
For any finite parameter increment $u$,
\begin{equation}
 z(\theta+u)-z(\theta)
 =J_Cu+\mathcal E_C(\theta,u)
   -\big[e_C^*(\theta+u)-e_C^*(\theta)\big],
 \label{eq:feedback-renewal}
\end{equation}
where
\[
 \mathcal E_C(\theta,u)=\int_0^1(1-s)D^2C(\theta+su)[u,u]\,ds.
\]
This follows by Taylor's formula with integral remainder and subtraction
of the two equilibrium values. A GD fine step $u=-\eta F$ has $J_Cu=0$.
Its equilibrium can nevertheless move at first order. In Adam,
$u^F=-\eta D\widehat m^F$ generally has $J_Cu^F\ne0$, where
$D=\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{\widehat v}+\epsilon)^{-1}$ and $\widehat m^F$
is the bias-corrected fine first moment. Equation~\eqref{eq:feedback-renewal}
therefore separates direct coarse disturbance, moving equilibrium, and a
finite-step remainder. It does not infer their relative sizes.

\paragraph{Population accounting.}
For $A=\sum_j a_j^2$, with $u_a^F,u_a^R$ the slope blocks of the updates,
\begin{equation}
 A_{n+1}-A_n=2\langle a_n,u_{a,n}^F\rangle
 +2\langle a_n,u_{a,n}^R\rangle
 +\|u_{a,n}^F+u_{a,n}^R\|^2.
 \label{eq:feedback-population}
\end{equation}
The first two terms are signed linear contributions; the last term is
shared and must be retained when interpreting oscillations. Computations
also retain the numerically unresolved gradient channel in this identity.
We measure population scale by $\lambda_{\rm RMS}=h\sqrt{A/W}$, rather than
the largest slope. The reference $1/4$ is a construction scale, not a
target-independent necessary condition for accuracy.

\paragraph{An illustrative restriction with persistent motion.}
Consider collective coordinates $(x,s)$ and the local loss
$L(x,s)=\kappa x^2/2+fs$, with $\kappa>0$ and a fixed positive definite
preconditioner
$B=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}d_c&d_{cf}\\d_{cf}&d_f\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.
If $0<\eta\kappa d_c<2$, set
$x_*=-d_{cf}f/(\kappa d_c)$, $\alpha=1-\eta\kappa d_c$, and
$\mu=d_f-d_{cf}^2/d_c>0$. Preconditioned GD satisfies
\begin{equation}
 s_N-s_0=-N\eta\mu f
 -\frac{d_{cf}}{d_c}(1-\alpha^N)(x_0-x_*).
 \label{eq:feedback-mobility}
\end{equation}
Indeed, the coarse recurrence gives $x_n-x_*=\alpha^n(x_0-x_*)$;
substitution in $s_{n+1}-s_n=-\eta(\kappa d_{cf}x_n+d_ff)$ and summation
prove the formula. Correction reduces the direct fine mobility $d_f$ to
its Schur complement $\mu$. Motion continues when $f\ne0$.
Neither small $\mu$ nor a negative slope response follows automatically.
For ordinary GD, $B=I$ and this separable example has no such restriction:
coarse oscillation alone does not slow the fine coordinate.

\paragraph{Stability diagnostic.}
For locally fixed $D$, an eigenmode of $D^{1/2}\nabla^2L D^{1/2}$ with
positive eigenvalue $\ell$ and EMA momentum coefficient $0\le\beta_1<1$ has
characteristic polynomial
\[
 r^2-[1+\beta_1-\eta(1-\beta_1)\ell]r+\beta_1.
\]
Its roots lie inside the unit circle precisely when
$0<\eta\ell<2(1+\beta_1)/(1-\beta_1)$.
Thus the reference is $2$ for GD and $38$ for $\beta_1=0.9$.
For moving Adam this is a local diagnostic, not a stability theorem.
We use the full loss Hessian. For its leading scaled unit eigenvector $v$,
the coarse contribution to its curvature is
\[
 \frac{\|J_CD^{1/2}v\|^2}
 {v^\top D^{1/2}\nabla^2L D^{1/2}v}.
\]
We also project $v$ onto $\operatorname{range}(D^{1/2}J_C^\top)$.
These measurements distinguish coarse stiffness from a merely similar
oscillatory trace. Negative-curvature modes are recorded separately;
failure to resolve their smallest eigenvalue is not reported as convexity.
Related analyses describe GD self-stabilization and Adam's adaptive
stability boundary
(\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15594}{Damian, Nichani, and Lee, 2023};
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.14484}{Cohen et al., 2024}).

\paragraph{An endogenous oscillation in a reduced model.}
A coarse oscillation can also maintain the adaptive denominator without
continuing fine disturbance. To isolate this possibility, use the same
loss $L(x,s)=\kappa x^2/2+fs$ with EMA momentum $m$ and a shared scalar
RMS denominator, with no offset or bias correction:
\begin{align*}
 m_{n+1}&=\beta m_n+(1-\beta)(\kappa x_n,f),\\
 v_{n+1}&=\beta_2v_n+(1-\beta_2)(\kappa^2x_n^2+f^2)/2,\\
 (x_{n+1},s_{n+1})&=(x_n,s_n)-\eta m_{n+1}/\sqrt{v_{n+1}}.
\end{align*}
Let $T_\beta=2(1+\beta)/(1-\beta)$ and
$|f|<\sqrt2\eta\kappa/T_\beta$. There is an exact solution
\[
 x_n=(-1)^n b,\qquad
 b^2=2\eta^2/T_\beta^2-f^2/\kappa^2,\qquad
 \sqrt{v_n}=\eta\kappa/T_\beta,\qquad
 s_{n+1}-s_n=-T_\beta f/\kappa.
\]
To verify it, initialize $x_0=b$, $v_0=(\eta\kappa/T_\beta)^2$,
$m_0^s=f$, and $m_0^x=-(1-\beta)\kappa b/(1+\beta)$.
The squared-gradient input is constant, while
$m_{n+1}^x=(1-\beta)\kappa x_n/(1+\beta)$, so each coarse update
subtracts $2x_n$. The fine update gives the stated drift.

At $f=0$, tracking persists without fine motion. At small $f\ne0$, it
sets the denominator at the coarse stability scale, and fine motion
continues at a rate proportional to $f/\kappa$. This is an exact solution
of the stated reduced model, not an attraction or persistence theorem
for coordinatewise Adam. In the orthonormal physical coordinates
$(s+x,s-x)/\sqrt2$, the two gradients are $(f\pm\kappa x)/\sqrt2$.
For the prescribed alternating input, their stationary coordinatewise
second moments differ from the shared mean by a relative amount at most
$(1-\beta_2)/(1+\beta_2)$, about $0.0005$ at $\beta_2=0.999$.
This motivates the shared-scale simplification but supplies no
long-horizon shadowing guarantee. The network fine-off test checks its
qualitative prediction directly.

\paragraph{Interventions that distinguish direction from denominator effects.}
Given the processed fine update $u^F$, we form
\begin{equation}
 u^{F,\mathrm{bal}}=u^F-DJ_C^\top(J_CDJ_C^\top)^{-1}J_Cu^F.
 \label{eq:feedback-balanced}
\end{equation}
Multiplication by $J_C$ proves its zero first-order coarse response. We
then scale the entire vector, including its output-bias entry, to match
the designated hidden-update norm; this preserves the constraint.
The six Adam branches are native, fine amplification by ten, balanced
fine motion at native amplitude, balanced fine motion at tenfold
amplitude, and the last two tenfold comparisons with the fine denominator
frozen at the fork. The frozen branches use that same fixed denominator
throughout the pulse, rather than matching a future native denominator.
Tracking uses native Adam processing in every branch. Full-gradient
first and second moments, and the decomposed first moments, continue
to evolve at each branch's own parameters. They are retained on release.

A passive second-moment comparison uses
\[
 v_{n+1}^{\rm shadow}=\beta_2v_n^{\rm shadow}
 +(1-\beta_2)(\nabla L_n-0.9R_n)^{\odot2}.
\]
Its fine-update norm gain uses the same fine first moment as the native
denominator. Squaring the combined gradient retains coordinatewise cross
terms; this is not a sum of independently squared fine and tracking norms.
This comparison diagnoses denominator restriction. The actual trajectory
interventions test whether releasing or avoiding it improves acquisition.

\paragraph{Targets, budgets, and evaluation.}
The panel contains six targets, widths $705$ and $1409$, and two seeds.
Training uses 2048 equally weighted points on $[-1,1]$ in full-batch
float64 arithmetic. Apart from the degree-five mixture below, targets
are divided by their training-grid RMS. They are mixed sine
$\sin(2\pi x)+\tfrac12\sin(6\pi x)+\tfrac14\sin(14\pi x)$;
a Gaussian $\exp(-((x+0.35)/0.22)^2)$; a compact bump
$\exp(1-1/(1-u^2))$ for $|u|<1$, zero otherwise, with $u=(x-0.35)/0.22$;
a smooth step $\tanh(14(x-0.31))$; and a kink $|x+0.23|$.
The polynomial target is $0.3q_0+0.4q_1+\sqrt{0.75}q_5$, where $q_j$
are orthonormal polynomials for the training measure.

Saved-state audits use updates 25,000 and 130,000 under both optimizers.
Continuations fork at 130,000. GD starts from both GD and Adam geometries
and runs for 10,000 updates at its native rate $0.002$ or at
$0.25,0.5,0.9,1.05,1.25$ times the initial $2/\lambda_{\max}(\nabla^2L)$.
Two further branches replace $F+R$ by $F+0.1R$ at the $0.9$ and $1.05$
rates. These are modified-force interventions, not ordinary GD.
Adam uses $(\eta,\beta_1,\beta_2,\epsilon)=(0.002,0.9,0.999,10^{-8})$,
with a 10,000-update pulse followed by 10,000 native updates.
The same six policies are applied to every target--width--seed case.

We record attached-network raw relative RMS error every update, retaining
the minimum separately from the current endpoint. There is no readout
refit. Endpoints are also evaluated on an independent 8192-point grid
with the original target normalization. These are optimization and
quadrature comparisons, not a held-out model-selection claim. The targets
and checkpoints come from previous development work; the second seed
and wider networks test transfer of the fixed intervention matrix.
Equal-update comparisons are primary. Interpolation on the common recorded
GD flow-time interval is a supplementary diagnostic, with its interpolation
bracket retained; it is not an additional finely sampled run.
The $1\%$ threshold is reported separately from the $10^{-6}$ diagnostic
and from the supplied geometry's target-dependent accuracy.

\paragraph{Secondary controls.}
After inspecting the first width-705, seed-30 panel, we add a matched native
control, zero fine displacement, a unit-gain frozen fine denominator, and a
unit-gain frozen balanced proposal. These use the same pulse and release
budgets on all 24 Adam starting states. The first test asks whether tracking
persists without continuing fine displacement; the latter two distinguish
tenfold-amplification failures from instability already at native amplitude.
All full-gradient moment recurrences remain active, so suppressing fine
displacement does not remove the fine gradient from the second moment.
This is a result-motivated follow-up, not part of the original fixed matrix.

\paragraph{Verification and outcome handling.}
Tests compare native checkpoint continuation and release against the original
optimizer, check the coarse projection, verify the exact population balance,
compare full Hessian products with automatic differentiation of the loss,
and compare batched branches with independent execution. Each branch stops
if parameters become nonfinite or the coarse solve is no longer numerically
resolved. These exits are retained with their last error and update count;
they are not silently omitted from the run totals. Endpoint plots distinguish
completed comparisons from these failures. Eigenvalue residuals, source
checkpoint hashes, execution receipts, and sparse parameter endpoints are
retained. All numerical execution uses Modal, with a three-GPU-hour ceiling;
no large arrays are loaded on the local machine.
